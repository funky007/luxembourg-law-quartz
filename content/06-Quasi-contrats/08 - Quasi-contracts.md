## 1. General Concept

### 1.1 Definition (art. 1371)

> Quasi-contracts are purely voluntary acts that result in an obligation toward a third party or reciprocal obligations.

### 1.2 Three Quasi-contracts

```
QUASI-CONTRATS
        │
        ├─→ 1. GESTION D'AFFAIRES (arts. 1372-1375)
        │
        ├─→ 2. PAIEMENT DE L'INDU (arts. 1376-1381)
        │
        └─→ 3. ENRICHISSEMENT SANS CAUSE (jurisprudential)
```

---

## 2. Gestion d'Affaires (arts. 1372-1375)

### 2.1 Definition

Voluntary management of another's affairs without mandate.

### 2.2 Conditions

| Condition | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Act of management** | Real intervention in another's affairs |
| **Without mandate** | No authorization from owner |
| **Animus gerendi** | Intention to manage for another (not self) |
| **Useful management** | Benefit to owner |

### 2.3 Effects

**Obligations of Gérant**:
- Continue management once begun
- Exercise diligence of bonus pater familias
- Account for management

**Obligations of Maître**:
- Reimburse useful expenses
- Indemnify for necessary engagements
- Execute contracts properly concluded

### 2.4 Ratification

If owner ratifies → transforms into mandate; retroactive effect.

## 3. Paiement de l'Indu (arts. 1376-1381)

### 3.1 Definition

Right to recover what was paid without being owed.

### 3.2 Conditions

1. **Payment** — transfer of value
2. **Absence of debt** — either never existed or already extinguished
3. **Error** — payer believed debt existed (generally required)

### 3.3 Types

| Type | Situation |
|------|-----------|
| **Indu objectif** | Debt doesn't exist at all |
| **Indu subjectif** | Paid to wrong person or wrong payer |

### 3.4 Effects — Good Faith vs. Bad Faith

**Recipient in Good Faith**:
- Return only what received
- No interest
- If thing destroyed/lost: no liability (unless enriched)

**Recipient in Bad Faith**:
- Return thing plus fruits/interest
- Liable even if thing destroyed/lost
- Accountable from day of receipt

## 4. Enrichissement Sans Cause

### 4.1 Definition (Poelmans §257)

> Action granting person (*appauvri*) right to claim against another (*enrichi*) when enrichment corresponds to impoverishment and neither is justified by any cause.

### 4.2 Legal Basis

> **Poelmans §258**: Jurisprudence considers true foundation is **equity** (*équité*).

### 4.3 Five Cumulative Conditions

| Condition | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| 1. **Enrichissement** | Defendant's patrimony increased |
| 2. **Appauvrissement** | Claimant's patrimony decreased |
| 3. **Corrélation** | Direct link between them |
| 4. **Absence de cause** | No legal justification |
| 5. **Subsidiarité** | No other action available |

### 4.4 Subsidiarity — CRITICAL

> **Strictly subsidiary**: Action only available when claimant has **no other remedy** — contractual, delictual, or other.

If contract exists → must use contractual action.
If delict → must use art. 1382.
If specific quasi-contract applies → must use that.

### 4.5 Measure of Recovery

Lesser of:
- Enrichment of defendant
- Impoverishment of claimant

Not damages — restitution only.

### 4.6 Applicable Law

> **Poelmans §258**: Before Rome II Regulation, courts applied law of underlying relationship (if any). After Rome II: law governing the relationship to which enrichment relates.

## 5. Banking & Finance Application

### Gestion d'Affaires

- Rare in banking context
- Possible: Bank preserving client assets during absence

### Paiement de l'Indu

**Common scenarios**:
- Erroneous wire transfers
- Duplicate payments
- Payment to wrong account

> Recovery conditioned on recipient's good faith and ability to identify payment as erroneous.

### Enrichissement Sans Cause

- Last resort when no contractual or delictual basis
- Often fails due to subsidiarity requirement
- May apply in complex tripartite situations
