# Part 02 — Formation of Contracts

> **Poelmans Reference**: Chapters II-III (La formation des contrats)  
> **Navigation**: [[🧭 Droit des obligations — Index|← Back to Index]]

## 1. Essential Conditions (art. 1108 C.civ.)

Four cumulative conditions for valid contract formation:

```
ART. 1108 — CONDITIONS DE VALIDITÉ
        │
        ├─→ 1. CONSENTEMENT (consent)
        │       └─ Free from vices
        │
        ├─→ 2. CAPACITÉ (capacity)
        │       └─ Legal ability to contract
        │
        ├─→ 3. OBJET (object)
        │       └─ Certain, possible, lawful
        │
        └─→ 4. CAUSE (cause)
                └─ Lawful purpose
```

---

## 2. Consentement (Consent)

### 2.1 Formation of Consent

Consent requires:
- **Offer** (*offre*) — firm, precise, complete
- **Acceptance** (*acceptation*) — unconditional, matching offer

> **Poelmans**: The distinction between firm offer and mere *pourparlers* is a question of fact for the judge. Parties remain at preliminary stage when elements of the projected contract are not yet fixed with certainty.

### 2.2 Pre-contractual Liability (*Culpa in contrahendo*)

Even without contractual bond, parties have obligations:
- **Devoir de loyauté** — duty of good faith in negotiations
- **Legitimate expectation** — breaking negotiations abruptly without serious reason = fault

Liability is **extra-contractual** (art. 1382 C.civ.).

### 2.3 Vices du Consentement

Four grounds for nullity based on defective consent:

| Vice                           | Articles  | Key Feature                               |
| ------------------------------ | --------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| [[Erreur (arts. 1110-1117)]]   | 1110-1117 | Distortion between declared and real will |
| [[Violence (arts. 1111-1114)]] | 1111-1114 | Threat weighing on consent                |
| [[Dol (art. 1116)]]            | 1116      | Maneuvers to deceive                      |
| [[Lésion (art. 1118)]]         | 1118      | Gross imbalance (dual test)               |

## 3. Vices du Consentement — Detail

### 3.1 Erreur (Error)

**See full note**: [[Erreur (arts. 1110-1117)]]

**Types**:
1. **Erreur-obstacle** — prevents meeting of minds (e.g., one thinks sale, other thinks lease)
2. **Erreur vice du consentement** — error on *qualités substantielles*

**Requirements** (per Poelmans):
- Must be **déterminante** — would not have contracted without it
- Must concern **qualités substantielles** — essential qualities of the object
- Must be **excusable** — party had no duty to self-inform

> **Erreur inexcusable**: Error is not excusable when party could have informed themselves. Example: caution who didn't verify debtor's finances; buyer who didn't read copropriété rules.

**Sanction**: Nullité relative (5 years from discovery)

### 3.2 Violence (Duress)

**See full note**: [[Violence (arts. 1111-1114)]]

**Definition** (Poelmans §66): A person contracts under threat that weighs on their consent.

**Requirements**:
- **Mal considérable et présent** — considerable and present harm
- Threat to person, goods (art. 1112), or close relatives (art. 1113)
- Can be by co-contractor or third party (art. 1111)
- Must be **illégitime** — not exercise of normal right (art. 1114)
- Must affect a **reasonable person** (art. 1112)

> **Case** (Poelmans §67): Blind person pressured to sign credit for another's benefit by threat of abandonment. Court held this did not constitute violence — the person could have recourse to public services for dependents.

**Sanction**: Nullité relative (5 years from cessation of violence)

### 3.3 Dol (Fraud)

**See full note**: [[Dol (art. 1116)]]

**Definition**: Maneuvers used to deceive co-contractor into consenting.

**Requirements**:
- **Manœuvres** — active deception OR silence (*réticence dolosive*)
- **Déterminant** — would not have contracted without deception
- Must emanate from **co-contractor** (not third party)

**Réticence dolosive** (Silence as fraud):
> **Poelmans**: Requires *obligation de parler* — duty to speak. This is a *condition nécessaire* for silent fraud.

> **Employment case**: Employee did not disclose prior incarceration. Court found no fraud because employer had no right to ask about criminal record for that position.

**Sanction**: Nullité relative (5 years from discovery)

### 3.4 Lésion (Gross Disproportion)

**See full note**: [[Lésion (art. 1118)]]

**Definition** (art. 1118 C.civ.): Lésion does not vitiate contracts *unless* dual test is met.

**Luxembourg Dual Test** (since 2011 reform):
1. **Objective element**: Serious disproportion in prestations
2. **Subjective element**: Exploitation of weakness, ignorance, or precarious situation

> **Key Case** (Poelmans §75): Sale of shares for €225,000 when company had zero value — Court annulled for lésion.

**⚠️ CRITICAL — Prescription**: **1 year from contract conclusion** (art. 1118 al. 3)

This is a major exception to the 5-year rule for vices du consentement.

**Sanction**: Rescision or reduction of obligations (nullité relative)

**Specific Lésion Regimes**:
- Art. 1674: Immovable sales (< 5/12 of value)
- Art. 887: Partition (lésion de plus d'un quart)
- Art. 1305: Minors (any prejudice)

## 4. Capacité (Capacity)

**See full note**: [[Capacité (arts. 1123-1125)]]

### 4.1 Principle (art. 1123)

> "Toute personne peut contracter, si elle n'en est pas déclarée incapable par la loi."

Two types of capacity:
- **Capacité de jouissance** — ability to hold rights
- **Capacité d'exercice** — ability to exercise rights oneself

### 4.2 Incapacities (art. 1124)

| Category                  | Basis          | Protection                   |
| ------------------------- | -------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **Mineurs non émancipés** | art. 388       | Represented by parents/tutor |
| **Majeurs protégés**      | art. 488       | Curatorship, tutorship       |
| **Faillis**               | art. 444 C.com | Loss of disposition rights   |

### 4.3 Legal Persons

Per Poelmans §80-83:
- Capacity depends on **legal attribution** of personality
- Limited by **objet social** (corporate purpose)
- Public entities: capacity limited to *intérêt communal* or statutory purpose

> **Case**: Commune's contract for wind farm project — held outside *intérêt communal* as electricity supply already assured.

### 4.4 Specific Incapacities

- **Spouses in community regime**: Cannot dispose of common property without consent (arts. 1421-1 et s.)
- **Ultra vires acts**: Sanction depends on type — nullité relative usually

**Sanction**: Nullité relative (only incapable party may invoke — art. 1125)

## 5. Objet (Object)

**See full note**: [[Objet (arts. 1126-1130)]]

### 5.1 Definition (art. 1126)

The **object of the obligation** is the *prestation* the party undertakes to provide.

### 5.2 Requirements

| Requirement                   | Article | Test                                   |
| ----------------------------- | ------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **Dans le commerce**          | 1128    | Not human body, drugs, dangerous goods |
| **Licite**                    | 1128    | Not contrary to law/public policy      |
| **Possible**                  | —       | Absolutely impossible = null           |
| **Déterminé ou déterminable** | 1129    | Nature and extent ascertainable        |

### 5.3 Determination of Object

> **Poelmans §87**: Object is *déterminé* when nature and extent appear from the contract. It is *déterminable* when contract contains elements for determination.

**Third-party determination**: Parties may delegate to third party (arts. 1592-1593 for price). If third party cannot or refuses → contract *caduc*.

### 5.4 Things Outside Commerce (art. 1128)

Per Poelmans §91:
- Human body (except regulated donations)
- Narcotics, toxic substances
- Dangerous products (loi 31 juillet 2006)
- Family personal relations (art. 1388) — though evolving

**Sanction**: Nullité absolue

## 6. Cause (Cause)

**See full note**: [[Cause (arts. 1131-1133)]]

### 6.1 Luxembourg Specificity

> **CRITICAL**: Luxembourg retains the *cause* doctrine. Unlike France (2016 reform), Luxembourg did not abolish cause.

### 6.2 Definition

**Cause** = the *pourquoi de l'opération juridique* — why the party contracted.

### 6.3 Requirements

| Condition          | Article | Sanction         |
| ------------------ | ------- | ---------------- |
| **Must exist**     | 1131    | Nullité relative |
| **Must be lawful** | 1133    | Nullité absolue  |

> **Poelmans**: No need for common illicit motive — knowledge by one party suffices for nullity based on illicit cause.

### 6.4 Banking Application — Corporate Benefit

For upstream guarantees, *cause* analysis requires:
- Demonstrable benefit to guarantor company
- Not purely gratuitous to detriment of company

## 7. Forme (Form)

### 7.1 Principle: Consensualism

Contracts are valid by mere exchange of consent (no form required).

### 7.2 Exceptions

| Type                   | Requirement            | Example                   |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------- |
| **Contrats solennels** | Form for validity      | Marriage, donation        |
| **Contrats réels**     | Delivery for formation | Loan, deposit             |
| **Ad probationem**     | Form for proof only    | > €2,500 requires writing |

## 8. Nullité (Nullity)

**See full note**: [[Nullité]]

### 8.1 Two Regimes

| | Nullité Absolue | Nullité Relative |
|--|-----------------|------------------|
| **Protects** | General interest | Private interest |
| **Grounds** | Ordre public, illicit object/cause | Vices du consentement, incapacity |
| **Who may invoke** | Any interested party | Protected party only |
| **Ex officio** | Yes, by judge | No |
| **Prescription** | 30 years | 5 years (1 year for lésion) |
| **Confirmation** | Not possible | Possible |
| **Exception de nullité** | Imprescriptible | Imprescriptible |

### 8.2 Effects of Nullity

- Contract disappears *ab initio*
- Restitution of prestations
- May be total or partial (only severable clauses)
