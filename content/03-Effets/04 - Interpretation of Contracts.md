# Part 04 — Interpretation of Contracts

> **Poelmans Reference**: Chapter VI (L'interprétation du contrat)  
> **Navigation**: [[🧭 Droit des obligations — Index|← Back to Index]]

---

## 1. Fundamental Principle (art. 1156)

### 1.1 Search for Real Intent

> **Art. 1156**: "On doit dans les conventions rechercher quelle a été la commune intention des parties contractantes, plutôt que de s'arrêter au sens littéral des termes."

The judge must seek the **common and real intention** of parties, not just literal meaning.

### 1.2 Question of Fact

> **Poelmans §180**: In Luxembourg, the interpretive precepts of arts. 1157-1164 are merely **advice** to the judge, not absolute rules. Their violation does not give rise to cassation.

### 1.3 Limits on Interpretation

The judge must:
- Respect contract content as parties willed it (**convention-loi**)
- Respect **foi due aux actes** (arts. 1319, 1320, 1322)
- Not denature clear and precise terms
- Only deviate from clear terms if justified by **extrinsic facts**

> **Poelmans §178**: Even if terms appear clear, interpretation is not precluded when parties make divergent readings that gave rise to litigation.

## 2. Interpretive Precepts (arts. 1157-1164)

### 2.1 Overview Table

| Article | Precept | Application |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| **1157** | Effective interpretation | If two readings possible, choose one giving effect |
| **1158** | Contextual meaning | Terms take sense fitting subject matter |
| **1159** | Usage-based interpretation | Interpret according to local usage |
| **1160** | Implied usual clauses | Supply customary clauses not expressed |
| **1161** | Unity of contract | Interpret clauses together, not in isolation |
| **1162** | *Contra proferentem* | Doubt resolved against drafter |
| **1163** | No extension beyond intent | Don't extend to matters parties didn't envision |
| **1164** | Inclusive interpretation | Specific case doesn't exclude others if intent broader |

### 2.2 Effective Interpretation (art. 1157)

> Choose interpretation that gives effect to the contract over one that renders it void or ineffective.

**Case** (Poelmans §181): Bank attestations for SICAV shares — Court held attestations must be interpreted as commitment to exchange, otherwise they have no useful effect.

**Case**: Clause stating seller not bound by employee signature until countersigned by manager — interpreted as limiting employee's power to negotiate (not as potestative condition which would nullify under art. 1174).

### 2.3 Contra Proferentem (art. 1162)

Original rule: In doubt, interpret in favor of the party undertaking obligation.

> **Modern evolution** (Poelmans §182): Art. 1162 has become an instrument for interpreting **contrats d'adhésion**. Contract interpreted **against the drafter**.

Rationale: The drafter bears responsibility for obscurities and lacunae.

**Case**: Architect's contract pre-established by client → still applied normal principles; contract not automatically a contrat d'adhésion just because pre-drafted.

### 2.4 Consumer Contracts (art. L.211-2(2) Code conso.)

> In case of doubt, interpretation most favorable to consumer prevails.

## 3. Restrictive Interpretation

### 3.1 Exorbitant Clauses

> **Poelmans §183**: Clauses derogating from common law must be interpreted **restrictively**.

**Examples**:
- Non-warranty clauses in vehicle sales — must be explicit
- Liability limitations
- Penalty clauses

### 3.2 Transactions (Settlements)

> **Poelmans §184**: Transactions settle only disputes expressly covered. In doubt, judge presumes element stayed outside transaction rather than included.

**Case**: Bank-employee transaction limited to employment contract — did not cover separate loan contract and rental guarantee.

---

## 4. Qualification of Contracts

### 4.1 Judge's Power

> **Poelmans §185**: Judge may qualify or requalify contract. Not bound by parties' denomination, though must act prudently when changing it.

### 4.2 Method

Judge examines characteristic elements:
- Nature of prestations
- Economic purpose
- Parties' conduct

**Case**: Concubines claiming société créée de fait — Court rejected: contribution explained by marriage project, not *affectio societatis*; mere common funding of house construction ≠ company.

## 5. Key Rules Summary

```
INTERPRETATION HIERARCHY
        │
        ├─→ 1. Common real intention (art. 1156)
        │
        ├─→ 2. Give effect to contract (art. 1157)
        │
        ├─→ 3. Context and usage (arts. 1158-1160)
        │
        ├─→ 4. Unity of contract (art. 1161)
        │
        ├─→ 5. Against drafter (art. 1162)
        │
        └─→ 6. No extension beyond intent (art. 1163)
```

### Question of Fact vs. Law

| Aspect | Nature | Cassation |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Application of interpretive precepts | Fact | No cassation |
| Dénaturation of clear terms | Law | Cassation possible |
| Faith due to acts (arts. 1319 et s.) | Law | Cassation possible |

## 6. Banking & Finance Application

### Contract Documentation

- Clear drafting essential to avoid interpretation disputes
- Entire agreement clauses (not conclusive but influential)
- Definitions should be precise and consistent

### Guarantees

- Cautionnement interpreted restrictively (security for another's debt)
- Extent of guarantee limited to express terms
- Doubt resolved in favor of *caution*
