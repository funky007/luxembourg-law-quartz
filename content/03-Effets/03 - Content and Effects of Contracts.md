# Part 03 — Content and Effects of Contracts

> **Poelmans Reference**: Chapters V-VI (Les effets des contrats)  
> **Navigation**: [[🧭 Droit des obligations — Index|← Back to Index]]

## 1. Force Obligatoire (art. 1134)

**See full note**: [[Force obligatoire (art. 1134)]]

### 1.1 Principle — Convention-Loi

> **Art. 1134 al. 1**: "Les conventions légalement formées tiennent lieu de loi à ceux qui les ont faites."

This means:
- Parties must execute their obligations
- Judge cannot dispense party from obligations
- Judge cannot modify contract content
- **Pacta sunt servanda**

### 1.2 Modification and Revocation (art. 1134 al. 2)

Contracts may only be revoked:
- By **mutual consent** (*mutuus dissensus*)
- For causes **authorized by law**

> **Poelmans §221**: Mutuus dissensus ends contract entirely. Parties must restore what they received. For successive contracts, termination operates only for the future.

## 2. Bonne Foi (art. 1134 al. 3)

**See full note**: [[Bonne foi (art. 1134 al. 3)]]

### 2.1 Principle

> **Art. 1134 al. 3**: Contracts "doivent être exécutées de bonne foi."

This creates **supplementary duties** beyond express terms.

### 2.2 Three Duties Derived from Bonne Foi

| Duty | Content | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Devoir de loyauté** | Honest dealing; inform other party | Agent must inform seller of better offer |
| **Devoir de modération** | No overly scrupulous enforcement | Cannot exploit minor technical breach |
| **Devoir de collaboration** | Cooperate for contract success | Subcontractor must advise on technical issues |

### 2.3 Jurisprudential Applications (Poelmans §147)

**Agent immobilier case**: Agent found buyer at 8M LUF but did not inform seller of other buyer at 9M LUF — breach of duty of loyalty, contractual liability.

**Subcontractor case**: Subcontractor followed instructions for defective work — still liable because duty to advise and refuse manifestly improper instructions.

**Employment**: Before specific harassment legislation, moral harassment was sanctioned via art. 1134 al. 3 — acts degrading dignity violate good faith duty.

### 2.4 Condition Accomplishment (art. 1178)

> **Poelmans**: Art. 1178 is an application of good faith — condition is deemed fulfilled if debtor prevented its accomplishment. Creates positive obligation to facilitate condition.

## 3. Effet Relatif (Privity)

### 3.1 Principle (art. 1165)

> Contracts only create effects between contracting parties. Third parties are neither bound nor benefited.

### 3.2 Exceptions

| Exception | Effect |
|-----------|--------|
| **Stipulation pour autrui** | Third party acquires direct right |
| **Promesse de porte-fort** | Promise that third party will act |
| **Action directe** | Legislatively granted (e.g., subcontractor vs. owner) |

### 3.3 Opposabilité

Contracts are **opposable** to third parties as *facts*:
- Third parties cannot ignore contract's existence
- But cannot invoke contract to their benefit (generally)

---

## 4. Contenu du Contrat

### 4.1 Express Terms

What parties explicitly agreed.

### 4.2 Implied Terms (art. 1135)

> Contract binds parties not only to express terms but to all consequences that **equity, usage, or law** attach to the obligation.

### 4.3 Conditions Générales (General Terms)

Per Poelmans §46-47:
- Must be **known and accepted** at contract formation
- Party invoking them bears burden of proof
- Language barrier does not excuse if party should have inquired
- Illegible fine print = not binding

> **Case**: Signature on contract stating GTC received and approved → GTC are binding.

## 5. Autonomie de la Volonté

### 5.1 Principle (art. 1134)

Parties are free to:
- Contract or not
- Choose co-contractor
- Determine contract content
- Choose applicable law (art. 1106)

### 5.2 Limits

| Limit | Source |
|-------|--------|
| **Ordre public** | Cannot derogate |
| **Bonnes mœurs** | Cannot violate |
| **Lois impératives** | Mandatory rules |
| **Consumer protection** | Code de la consommation |

### 5.3 Ordre Public de Protection

> **Poelmans §117-118**: If rule protects private interest → nullité relative. If rule protects general interest → nullité absolue.

**Examples of ordre public de protection** (nullité relative):
- Prohibition of *pacte commissoire* — protects debtor
- Consumer clauses abusives

---

## 6. Clauses Abusives (Consumer Contracts)

### 6.1 General Clause (art. L.211-2 Code conso.)

Clause is abusive if it creates **significant imbalance** between professional and consumer.

### 6.2 Black List (art. L.211-3)

Certain clauses are presumed abusive, including:
- Unilateral modification rights without valid reason
- Excluding professional's liability for death/injury
- Imposing penalties on consumer but not professional

**Sanction**: Nullity of abusive clause; rest of contract survives if possible.
