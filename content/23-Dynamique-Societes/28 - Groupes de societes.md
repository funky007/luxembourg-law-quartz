# 28 - Groupes de societes

## Overview

This note covers corporate groups (groupes de societes) under Luxembourg law. In the absence of a legal definition, a corporate group is generally understood as a set of companies which, while legally distinct, are linked to one another such that one of them, called the "dominant company" (societe dominante), is able to impose unity of decision on the other group components, which are in the position of "dominated companies" (societes dominees) (Steichen, Chapter 3, pp. 438-472).

---

## 1. Definition and Characteristics

### A. Core Concept

The group combines **legal independence** (independance juridique) with **economic dependence** (dependance economique). The essential criterion of the group is **unified power of direction** (pouvoir unique de direction), which typically rests on capital links between the parent company and dominated companies (Steichen, p. 438).

**Key Principle:** The group has no legal personality. Only the companies forming part of a group are subjects of law; the group itself has no legal existence. This distinguishes corporate groups from merged companies. There is thus a principle of **legal impermeability** (impermeabilite juridique) between the companies of a group (Steichen, p. 438).

**Ambiguity of Groups:**
The existence of the group is invoked with radically opposed intent by different parties (Steichen, p. 438):
- The **dominant company** invokes it to justify operations intrinsically contrary to the corporate interest of the dominated company
- **Third parties** invoke it to oblige the dominant company to address the financial difficulties of the dominated company with which they contracted

### B. Types of Groups

**By Vocation (Steichen, pp. 439-440):**

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Groupe industriel** | Parent company exercises industrial or commercial activity; most common type, formed progressively as business expands |
| **Groupe financier** | Parent company is a holding company limited to holding financial participations; more numerous than thought, as industrial groups often transform into financial groups at maturity |
| **Groupe personnel** | Unity of decision results from community of shareholders or directors; e.g., real estate company leasing to operating company with same ownership |

**By Structure (Steichen, p. 439):**

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Groupe pyramidal** | Parent holds participations in subsidiaries which themselves hold participations in sub-subsidiaries (cascade structure) |
| **Groupe en eventail** | "Starfish" or Japanese-style; parent directly holds all participations in subsidiaries which remain financially isolated from each other |
| **Groupe circulaire** | Parent participates in capital of a subsidiary which holds participation in a third company, the last company itself being present in the capital of the parent |
| **Groupe complexe** | Combination of these variants |

### C. Advantages of Corporate Groups

**Risk Compartmentalization (Steichen, p. 440):**
- All eggs are not in one basket
- Obligation to debts is limited company by company
- Risk-taking in a particular sector does not engage all group interests

**Management Tool (Steichen, p. 440):**
- Facilitates control of various branches and their results
- More flexible and decentralized management

**vs. Unitary Structure:**
- Unitary structure offers administrative cost savings
- Greater management unity
- Fiscal consolidation of results

## 2. Members of the Group

### A. Filiale (Subsidiary)

**Definition:** A legally distinct entity from the parent company but placed under its control (Art. 1711-1 al. 1 L1915) (Steichen, p. 441).

**Control Criteria (Art. 1711-1 L1915):**
A company is a subsidiary of another if that other company:
- (a) has the **majority of voting rights** of shareholders or associates, or
- (b) has the right to **appoint or dismiss the majority of members** of the administrative, management, or supervisory body and is simultaneously a shareholder or associate, or
- (c) is a shareholder or associate and **controls alone, by virtue of an agreement** with other shareholders, the majority of voting rights

**Types of Control:**
- **Controle de droit** (legal control): Holding majority of voting rights (a)
- **Controle contractuel** (contractual control): Control via contractual arrangements (b, c)
- Note: De facto control resulting from mere appointment of directors over time is not relevant for delimiting subsidiaries (Steichen, p. 441)

**Calculation of Rights (Art. 1711-2 par. 1 L1915):**
Voting rights must be aggregated with those of any subsidiary and those of persons acting in their own name but on behalf of the parent or any subsidiary (Steichen, p. 441).

### B. Participation (Affiliate)

**Definition (Art. 41 L19.12.2002):** Rights in the capital of other companies, whether evidenced by securities or not, which, by creating a **durable link** (lien durable), are intended to contribute to the company's activity (Steichen, p. 443).

**Presumption:** Holding a part of the capital of another company is presumed to be a participation when it exceeds **20%** (Art. 41 L19.12.2002) (Steichen, p. 443).

**Distinction:**
- Subsidiary: 50%+ voting rights
- Participation: typically 20-50%
- Simple placement: less than 20%

**A participation must be (Steichen, p. 443):**
1. Intended to create a **durable link** (long-term holding)
2. **Useful to the company's activity** (not mere financial placement for yield)

### C. Entreprises liees (Related Companies)

**Definition (Art. 1760-1 no. 11 referencing Art. 1790-2 par. 1 L1915):** Companies between which exist the relations referred to in Art. 1711-1, as well as other companies in such a relation with any of them (Steichen, p. 442).

**IAS Definition (Art. 1790-2 par. 1bis L1915):** "Related party" has the same meaning as in international accounting standards adopted under Regulation EC 1606/2002. Any company forming part of the same group for consolidation purposes is a related company (Steichen, p. 442).

### D. Societe Holding (Holding Company)

**Definition:** A head-of-group company whose activity is limited to holding shares in companies over which it intends to exercise control (Steichen, p. 443).

**Types (Steichen, p. 443):**
- **Holdings pures (Pure holdings):** Only hold participations in other companies
- **Holdings mixtes (Mixed holdings):** Also provide services to subsidiaries ("animatrices")

**Distinction from Societe de participations financieres (Art. 31 par. 2 L19.12.2002):**
Companies whose sole object is taking participations and their management and enhancement, without direct or indirect interference in the management of those companies (Steichen, p. 443).

**Historical Note:** The holding company 1929 regime (fiscal advantages) was abolished at end of 2010 following EU state aid rules. The SPF (Societe de gestion de Patrimoine Familial) was instituted in 2007 as replacement vehicle (Steichen, p. 444).

### E. Joint Venture (Filiale commune)

A particular form of corporate group characterized by equal participation between two shareholders, often themselves two competing or complementary groups (Steichen, p. 442).

**Challenges:**
- Neither party wants minority position
- Essential to avoid deadlocks
- Often resolved through shareholders' agreements (conventions de vote) (Steichen, p. 442)

## 3. Constitution of Groups

### A. Acquisition of Listed Securities

#### 1. Offre Publique d'Achat (Public Takeover Bid)

**Definition:** A person publicly announces intention to acquire securities of a listed company at a price determined in advance and payable in cash (or shares for OPE) (Steichen, p. 444).

**Regulatory Framework:** Directive 2004/25/CE transposed by Law of 19 May 2006 (L19.5.2006), supervised by CSSF (Steichen, p. 445).

**Types:**
- **OPA amicale (Friendly):** Results from negotiation/acceptance by target management
- **OPA sauvage (Hostile):** Implemented suddenly, often meets opposition from target management
- **OPA volontaire (Voluntary):** Bidder freely decides to acquire target securities
- **OPA obligatoire (Mandatory):** Bidder has obtained control and must offer to purchase remaining securities

**Three Fundamental Principles:**

**1. Transparency (Steichen, pp. 445-446):**
- Decision to launch OPA must be made public without delay after informing CSSF (Art. 6 al. 1 L19.5.2006)
- Bidder must prepare **document d'offre** (offer document) approved by CSSF
- Target management must issue reasoned opinion on offer after consulting employee representatives (Art. 10 al. 5 L19.5.2006)

**2. Shareholder Protection (Steichen, pp. 446-447):**
- OPA becomes **mandatory** when control threshold crossed (Art. 5(1) L19.5.2006)
- Control threshold in Luxembourg: **33 1/3%** of voting rights (Art. 5(3) L19.5.2006)
- Must offer **juste prix** (fair price) = highest price paid in preceding 12 months (Art. 5(4) L19.5.2006)
- **Principe d'egalite de traitement**: All holders of same class must receive equivalent treatment

**Squeeze-out (Retrait obligatoire):**
- Available when bidder holds **95%+** of capital and voting rights post-OPA (Art. 15 al. 2 L19.5.2006)
- Exercisable within 3 months after offer acceptance period
- At **juste prix** (Art. 15 al. 5 L19.5.2006) (Steichen, p. 447)

**Sell-out (Rachat obligatoire):**
- Minority shareholders can oblige majority to buy their shares
- Available when majority holds **90%+** of voting rights (Art. 16 L19.5.2006)
- Same price as squeeze-out (Steichen, p. 448)

**3. Liberal Conception/Neutrality (Steichen, pp. 448-449):**
- Management must obtain prior shareholder authorization before taking action that could frustrate offer (Art. 10 al. 2 L19.5.2006)
- BUT: This is opt-in (shareholders must vote for it in EGM)
- **Reciprocity rule**: Neutrality does not apply if bidder would not apply same rule (Art. 9 al. 3 L19.5.2006)

#### 2. Notification of Major Holdings

**Framework:** Transparency Law of 11 January 2008 (Steichen, p. 449).

**Triggers:** Acquisitions of **voting rights** in **listed companies** reaching thresholds of 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25%, 33 1/3%, 50%, 66 2/3% (Steichen, p. 449).

**Obligation:** Notify company and CSSF within **7 days** of threshold crossing (Art. 11 Loi Transparence) (Steichen, p. 450).

**Sanction:** Voting rights suspended until notification made (Art. 28(1) Loi Transparence); assembly decisions may be annulled judicially (Art. 28(2) Loi Transparence) (Steichen, p. 450).

### B. Acquisition of Non-Listed Securities

Not regulated. Acquisition may occur through amicable transfer at conditions freely and secretly agreed between parties (Steichen, p. 450).

## 4. Functioning of Groups

### A. Interet de groupe (Group Interest)

The interest of the group may not coincide with the interest of individual member companies. The law has created protective provisions for various stakeholders (Steichen, p. 440).

### B. Impermeabilite juridique (Legal Impermeability)

**Key Principle:** Each group company remains legally independent. The debts of one company do not become the debts of others, absent specific arrangements (guarantees, etc.) (Steichen, p. 438).

## 5. Banking and Finance Application

### Regulatory Considerations

**Prudential Supervision:**
- Groups containing credit institutions subject to consolidated supervision by CSSF
- Capital adequacy requirements at group level (CRR/CRD)
- Internal governance requirements for groups

**Qualifying Holdings:**
- Acquisition of qualifying holdings (10%+) in credit institutions/PSF requires prior CSSF authorization
- Change of control notifications mandatory

### Holding Structures

**Luxembourg Holding Companies commonly used for:**
- Group restructuring and simplification
- Tax-efficient dividend flows (participation exemption)
- Capital gains optimization on disposals
- Financing platforms (intragroup loans)

**SOPARFI (Societe de Participations Financieres):**
- Subject to corporate income tax but benefits from participation exemption regime
- Commonly used as intermediate holding company

### Intragroup Transactions

**Transfer Pricing:**
- Arm's length principle applies
- Documentation requirements for significant transactions
- Advance pricing agreements available from Luxembourg tax authorities

**Cash Pooling:**
- Physical and notional pooling structures common
- Upstream guarantees and loans subject to corporate benefit analysis
- Financial assistance rules to be observed
