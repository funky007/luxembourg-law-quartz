# 27 - Restructurations

## Overview

This note covers corporate restructuring operations under Luxembourg company law (Loi du 10 aout 1915 sur les societes commerciales, as amended). Restructuring encompasses changes limited to a single company (modifications des statuts, transformation) and operations involving multiple companies (fusions, scissions, apports partiels d'actifs).

---

## 1. Modifications des statuts (Statute Amendments)

### A. The Competent Body and Majority Rules

The statutes constitute the fundamental charter of the company. Only the general assembly of shareholders (assemblee generale) is competent to modify the statutes, as they represent the collective expression of shareholder will (Steichen, p. 404).

**Majority Requirements:**
- In **partnerships (societes de personnes)**: unanimity is required in principle, unless the statutes provide otherwise (Steichen, p. 404)
- In **corporations (societes de capitaux)**: reinforced quorum and majority conditions apply, but unanimity is not required - hence the term "assemblee generale extraordinaire" (Steichen, p. 404)

**Key Principle:** Statutes may require stricter quorum or majority requirements than those provided by law, but may not relax the legal requirements (Steichen, p. 404).

**Essential Elements Protection (Art. 450-3 par. 1 L1915):**
- The only remaining essential element requiring unanimous consent is the extent of shareholder commitments (etendue des engagements des actionnaires)
- Nationality was removed as an essential element by the law of August 10, 2016 (Steichen, p. 404)

### B. Opposability of Statutory Modifications

Under Art. 19-3 L27.05.2016: "Acts or extracts of acts are only opposable to third parties from the day of their publication in the Memorial (...), unless the company proves that such third parties had prior knowledge" (Steichen, p. 405).

The opposability referred to is that provided in contract law under Article 1165 C.civ. (effet relatif des contrats) - contracts only bind the contracting parties but third parties must recognize the effects a contract may have for them (principe d'opposabilite) (Steichen, p. 405).

## 2. Transformation de la societe

### A. Definition and Scope

**Transformation** is the operation by which a company changes its legal form while maintaining its legal personality (Art. 100-3 al. 3 L1915) (Steichen, p. 406).

**Scope (Art. 100-3 L1915):**
- Any of the six main commercial company types may transform into another type
- Transformation to or from a societe civile is possible
- Transformation to or from a GIE (Groupement d'interet economique) is permitted
- Transformation into a SE (Societe Europeenne) is not permitted (Steichen, p. 407)
- Transformation into a SARL simplifiee is not possible (Art. 100-3 al. 4, 6 and 8 L1915) (Steichen, p. 408)

**Key Legal Effect:** "Les transformations visees au present article ne donnent lieu ni a dissolution ni a creation d'une personnalite juridique nouvelle" (Art. 100-3 avant-dernier alinea L1915) (Steichen, p. 411)

### B. Conditions of Validity

Three principles must be combined (Steichen, pp. 408-410):

**First Principle (Effets induits):** Transformation requires amendment of statutes, thus conditions for statute modification apply:
- In partnerships: unanimity in principle (Art. 1010-7 par. 1 no. 1 L1915)
- In corporations: reinforced quorum and majority (Art. 1010-7 L1915)

**Second Principle (Conditions cumulatives):** When transforming into an SA, the conditions required for constitution of the new form must be observed - including minimum capital requirements and adequate capitaux propres (Steichen, p. 408-409).

**Third Principle (Augmentation des engagements):** If transformation increases shareholder commitments, unanimity is required (Art. 1010-7 par. 4 L1915) (Steichen, p. 409):
- Transformation from SARL to SNC (increased liability) requires unanimity
- Transformation from SNC to SECS requires unanimity (unlimited liability disappears)
- Consent of future commandites is required when they will be exposed to unlimited liability

**Procedural Requirements (Arts. 1010-1 to 1010-9 L1915):**
1. Management body must prepare a written report (rapport ecrit) on transformation (Art. 1010-4 L1915)
2. Balance sheet (bilan) not older than 6 months (Art. 1010-2 L1915)
3. Reviseur d'entreprises agrees report on the balance sheet (Art. 1010-3 L1915) - unless shareholders unanimously waive
4. Transformation act must be a notarial deed (acte notarie) (Art. 1010-9 L1915)

### C. Effects of Transformation

**On Associates:**
- Substitution of old rights by new rights, by real subrogation (subrogation reelle)
- Shareholder status is maintained
- Obligations may change depending on departure and arrival forms (Steichen, p. 412)

**On Corporate Bodies:**
- If transformation causes structural modification, new bodies are put in place and old bodies cease functions
- If status and functions remain sufficiently similar, no termination of functions (Steichen, p. 412)
- Auditor functions continue unless they change from reviseur to commissaire or vice versa (Steichen, p. 413)

**External Effects:**
- Creditors retain their rights - limitation of liability only applies for the future (Art. 100-3 dernier alinea L1915: "les droits des tiers sont reserves") (Steichen, p. 413)
- Pre-transformation creditors may invoke the new legal form if it increases shareholder obligations

**Tax Implications:**
- Homogeneous transformations (transparent to transparent, opaque to opaque): no tax consequences (Steichen, p. 414)
- Passage to opacity: assimilated to business cessation with taxation of latent gains, unless opting for fiscal neutrality
- Passage to transparency: taxation of all latent gains at IRC level (Steichen, p. 414)

## 3. Fusions (Mergers)

### A. Types of Mergers

**Fusion-absorption:** Company A absorbs Company B; A survives, B disappears (Steichen, p. 415)

**Fusion-confusion:** Both companies dissolve into a new company C (Steichen, p. 415)

**Fusion-combinaison:** Special variant combining elements of both (Steichen, p. 415)

### B. Merger Procedure

**Preparatory Phase:**
1. **Evaluation of exchange ratio** (parite d'echange) - fundamental for protecting shareholders
2. **Projet de fusion** (merger plan) - detailed document setting forth merger terms (Art. 1020-1 L1915)
3. **Expert reports** - independent expert assessment of exchange ratio
4. **Management reports** - explaining and justifying the merger (Art. 1021-1 to 1021-19 L1915)

**Decision Phase:**
1. Approval by extraordinary general assemblies of each company
2. Conditions of quorum and majority for statute modifications apply
3. Publication requirements in Memorial

**Key Articles:** Arts. 1020-1 to 1023-4 L1915

### C. Effects of Merger

**Transmission universelle du patrimoine:** The absorbed company transfers its entire patrimony (assets and liabilities) to the absorbing company by operation of law, without need for individual transfer formalities (Steichen, p. 416).

**Dissolution without liquidation:** The absorbed company is dissolved but not liquidated (dissolution sans liquidation) (Steichen, p. 416)

**Creditor Protection (Droit d'opposition):**
- Creditors may oppose the merger before competent courts
- Purpose is to obtain guarantees for their claims
- Does not prevent merger completion but provides security (Art. 1030-1 to 1033-1 L1915)

**Nullity:** Merger may only be declared null on grounds specifically enumerated in law, and action must be brought within 6 months (Art. 1023-4 L1915)

## 4. Scissions (Demergers)

### A. Types of Demergers

**Scission-absorption:** Company A splits its assets among existing companies B and C; A disappears (Steichen, p. 417)

**Scission-constitution:** Company A splits its assets to create new companies B and C; A disappears (Steichen, p. 417)

**Key Articles:** Arts. 1040-3, 1040-4, 1050-1 L1915

### B. Apport partiel d'actifs (Partial Asset Contribution)

When only part of a company's activities is transferred to another entity, the contributing company continues to exist. This is not strictly a demerger but follows similar principles (Steichen, p. 414).

**Branche d'activite:** A branch of activity (branche d'activite) is an autonomous ensemble of assets capable of independent operation (Steichen, p. 417).

### C. Transfert de patrimoine professionnel

Allows transfer of a professional patrimony as a universality, simplifying certain restructuring operations (Steichen, p. 417).

## 5. Banking and Finance Application

### Regulatory Considerations

Financial sector entities (etablissements de credit, PSF) face additional requirements:
- Prior CSSF authorization may be required for certain restructurings
- Specific capital adequacy requirements must be maintained post-restructuring
- Change of control notifications to CSSF
- Continuity of regulatory licenses must be ensured

### Holding Company Restructurings

Luxembourg holding companies frequently use:
- **Transformation:** SARL to SA for increased flexibility or access to capital markets
- **Fusion-absorption:** Consolidating group structures, simplifying chains of ownership
- **TUP (Transmission universelle de patrimoine):** Simplified dissolution for wholly-owned subsidiaries

### Documentation Requirements

For regulated entities:
- Detailed restructuring memorandum for CSSF
- Pro forma financial statements post-restructuring
- Legal opinion on continuity of licenses and authorizations
- Tax ruling requests where applicable
