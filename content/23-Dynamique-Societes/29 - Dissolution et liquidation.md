# 29 - Dissolution et liquidation

## Overview

This note covers the disappearance of companies under Luxembourg law (Loi du 10 aout 1915 sur les societes commerciales). Whatever its origin, the dissolution of a company leads to its liquidation with a view to sharing any net assets (Steichen, Chapter 4, pp. 472-503).

---

## 1. La dissolution de la societe (Dissolution)

### A. Dissolution voulue par les associes (Voluntary Dissolution)

**Unanimous Consent (Mutuus dissensus):**
The will of the shareholders creates the company; the contrary will dissolves it. All shareholders acting unanimously have the power to decide on early termination of the company contract (Steichen, p. 473).

**Key Principle (Art. 1134 al. 2 C.civ.):** "Les conventions ne peuvent etre revoquees que de leur consentement mutuel, ou pour les causes que la loi autorise" - contracts may only be revoked by mutual consent or for causes authorized by law (Steichen, p. 473).

**Majority Dissolution:**
In company law, the assembly may vote dissolution even without unanimity. This is a significant departure from general contract law principles (Steichen, pp. 473-474):
- The decision always has a collective character but need not be unanimous
- Deciding to terminate the contract early amounts to modifying the duration (duree) of the contract
- Falls within the competence of the assemblee generale extraordinaire (Steichen, p. 474)

**Unilateral Renunciation (Art. 1865 no. 5 C.civ.):**
A company may be dissolved "by the will of one or more persons to no longer be in partnership" (Steichen, p. 474).

**Limitations:**
- This rule is limited to **societes a duree illimitee** (unlimited duration companies) (Art. 1869 C.civ.)
- Formally excluded from SA, SAS, and SCA (Art. 480-1 al. 3 L1915) (Steichen, p. 474)
- Also excluded from SARL (Art. 710-3 L1915) (Steichen, p. 474)
- The renunciation must be made in **good faith** and not **a contretemps** (inopportune timing) (Art. 1870 C.civ.) (Steichen, p. 475)

**Statutory Causes:**
Nothing prevents shareholders from providing in the statutes for additional causes of dissolution specific to the company (Steichen, p. 475).

### B. Dissolution imposee aux associes (Involuntary Dissolution)

#### 1. Dissolution de plein droit (Automatic Dissolution)

**Expiration of Term (Art. 1865 C.civ.):**
- "La societe finit par l'expiration du terme pour laquelle elle a ete contractee"
- Dissolution is **automatique** (Steichen, p. 476)
- Shareholders may avoid dissolution by **prorogation** (extension) before term expires
- Prorogation must be voted before expiration; afterwards, the company is dissolved and a new company must be constituted (Steichen, p. 477)
- **Tacit renewal** clauses are permissible (Steichen, p. 477)
- **Late prorogation** (after term expiration) has no effect - the company is dissolved without possibility of revival (Steichen, p. 477)

**Realization or Extinction of Corporate Purpose (Art. 1865 no. 2 C.civ.):**
- A company that is merely **en sommeil** (dormant) is not dissolved - dormancy does not prevent later resumption of activities (Steichen, p. 478)
- Assessment is made by reference to the **statutory object** as stated in the statutes, not actual activity (Steichen, p. 478)
- **Consommation de l'objet** (achievement of purpose) - e.g., construction project completed, building sold (Steichen, p. 478)
- **Extinction de l'objet** (extinction of purpose) - e.g., activity becomes prohibited (Steichen, p. 478-479)

**Death, Interdiction, or Deconfiture of a Partner (Art. 1865 nos. 3 and 4 C.civ.):**
- Applies to **societes de personnes** where the identity of partners matters (Steichen, p. 479)
- In societes de capitaux, death or bankruptcy of a shareholder has no effect on the company's existence (Steichen, p. 479)
- Statutes may provide for continuation with heirs (Art. 1868 C.civ.) (Steichen, p. 479)

**Loss of Contributions (Art. 1867 C.civ.):**
- Only applies to **apports en propriete** (not apports en jouissance)
- Loss before contribution: dissolution (Art. 1867 al. 1 C.civ.)
- Loss after contribution: risk for the company, no dissolution unless the thing was the essential element of the company (Steichen, p. 479)

#### 2. Dissolution judiciaire (Judicial Dissolution)

**Reunion of All Shares in Single Hand (Art. 1865bis C.civ.):**
- "La reunion de toutes les parts sociales en une seule main n'entraine pas la dissolution de la societe" (Steichen, p. 480)
- For companies other than SA, SAS, and SARL: **regularization** required within one year
- After one year, any interested party (including creditors) may request judicial dissolution
- Tribunal may grant additional 6-month delay for regularization (Steichen, p. 480)
- Sole shareholder may dissolve the company at any time (Steichen, p. 480)

**Dissolution for Just Cause (Art. 1871 C.civ.):**
"La dissolution des societes a terme ne peut etre demandee par l'un des associes avant le terme convenu, qu'autant qu'il y en a de justes motifs" (Steichen, p. 481).

**Application:**
- Originally limited to societes de personnes
- Extended to SA and SCA (Art. 480-1 al. 4 L1915) (Steichen, p. 481)
- Extended to SARL (Art. 710-3 L1915) (Steichen, p. 481)
- Extended to cooperatives (Art. 811-4 L1915) (Steichen, p. 481)

**Examples of Just Causes:**
- Partner fails to perform obligations (e.g., fails to make contribution in industry) (Steichen, p. 481)
- Habitual infirmity making partner unfit for company business (Steichen, p. 481)
- Disappearance of co-partner prevents company functioning (Steichen, p. 481)

**Mesintelligence entre associes:**
- Simple "mauvais rapports" (bad relations) are insufficient - they are part of company life (Steichen, p. 481)
- **Mesentente grave** (serious disagreement) affecting management and functioning is required (Steichen, pp. 481-482)
- **Paralysie** of the company - immobilization of corporate bodies unable to react or make decisions (Steichen, p. 482)
- Blockage may be legal (assemblies unable to pass resolutions) or economic (business completely obstructed) (Steichen, p. 482)

**Procedure:**
- In temporary crisis: tribunal may grant **sursis** (stay) or appoint **administrateur provisoire** (Steichen, p. 482)
- In permanent paralysis: dissolution justified to avoid company sinking into endless disputes (Steichen, p. 482)
- Action belongs exclusively to shareholders - creditors and third parties excluded (Steichen, p. 482)
- The shareholder responsible for the dispute cannot bring the action - **nemo auditur propriam turpitudinem allegans** (Steichen, p. 482)
- Effect is **constitutive** and from date of judgment (Steichen, p. 483)

**Dissolution for Serious Legal Infractions (Art. 1200-1 L1915):**
The commercial tribunal may order liquidation of any company "qui poursuit des activites contraires a la loi penale ou qui contrevient gravement aux dispositions de la presente loi" (Steichen, p. 483).

**Purpose:** To maintain a healthy financial center by dissolving shell companies, companies committing serious and repeated violations of company law, and companies engaged in illegal activities (Steichen, p. 483).

**Procedure:**
- **Procureur d'Etat** has monopoly of judicial request - interested parties cannot act directly (Steichen, p. 483)
- No prior notice to the company required (Steichen, p. 483)
- Regularization does not automatically end the action (Steichen, p. 483)
- Gravity of infractions is a matter for sovereign judicial assessment (Steichen, pp. 483-484)

## 2. Effets de la dissolution (Effects of Dissolution)

### A. Principal Effects

**Survival of Legal Personality (Art. 1100-1 par. 1 L1915):**
"Les societes civiles et commerciales (...) sont, apres leur dissolution, reputees exister pour leur liquidation" (Steichen, p. 485).

**Consequences:**
- Company retains its name and domicile (Steichen, p. 485)
- Company may sue and be sued alone before commercial courts (Steichen, p. 485)
- Corporate patrimony subsists as creditors' security - cannot be seized by shareholders' personal creditors (Steichen, p. 485)
- Company may perform any operation useful for its liquidation (Steichen, p. 485)
- Contracts in progress continue (Steichen, p. 485)
- Company must not yet be struck off the RCS (Steichen, p. 486)
- Statutes may be modified within limits of liquidation needs (Steichen, p. 486)

**Limitations:**
- Company cannot transform or extend beyond its term (Steichen, p. 486)
- Company cannot undertake entirely new operations (Steichen, p. 486)

**Cessation of Corporate Bodies:**
- Dissolution implies appointment of liquidator
- Management bodies, auditors, and statutory commissaires cease their functions (Steichen, p. 486)
- Former directors must account for period from start of financial year to dissolution date (Steichen, p. 486)

### B. Secondary Effects

**Revocability of Dissolution:**
- If dissolution results from causes beyond shareholders' control (term, achievement of purpose, judicial dissolution): **irrevocable** (Steichen, p. 486-487)
- If dissolution decided by shareholders (contractual termination): shareholders may unanimously decide to revive the company (Steichen, p. 487)

**Publication (Art. 100-13 par. 2 L1915):**
- Dissolution must be **publiee** (published) (Steichen, p. 487)
- Effects towards third parties only from publication date (Steichen, p. 487)
- Must indicate **appointment of liquidator** (Steichen, p. 487)

**Bankruptcy:**
- Dissolution does not prevent bankruptcy proceedings (Steichen, p. 487)
- If during liquidation the assets appear insufficient to cover liabilities, the liquidator must declare bankruptcy (Steichen, p. 487)
- Bankruptcy may be declared up to 6 months and 10 days following closure of liquidation (Steichen, p. 488)

## 3. Dissolution-confusion (TUP - Transmission universelle de patrimoine)

### A. Definition and Option

When all shares are held by a single shareholder who decides to dissolve the company, there is dissolution without liquidation by way of **transmission universelle de patrimoine** (Art. 1865bis C.civ.) (Steichen, p. 489).

**Key Feature:** This is an **option** for the sole shareholder - they may choose standard liquidation instead (Steichen, p. 489).

### B. Effects

**Immediate Disappearance:**
- Company ceases to exist immediately
- No liquidation takes place
- Only hypothesis in Luxembourg law of "dissolution seche" or "dissolution simplifiee" (Steichen, p. 489)

**Universal Transfer:**
- Entire patrimony (assets and liabilities) automatically and immediately transfers to sole shareholder as **universalite** (Steichen, p. 489)
- Operates by operation of law, without novation
- No need to comply with formalities for specific transfers (e.g., Art. 1690 C.civ. for assignment of claims) (Steichen, p. 490)

### C. Creditor Protection

**Right to Request Guarantees (Art. 1865bis al. 4 C.civ.):**
- Creditors cannot oppose dissolution
- Within 30 days of Memorial publication, creditors may request from tribunal president the **constitution de suretes** (provision of security) (Steichen, p. 490)
- If shareholder's patrimony appears insufficient relative to debts assumed
- Tribunal president decides on the merits but according to summary procedure (Steichen, p. 490)

**Required Certificates (Art. 1100-1 par. 2 L1915):**
The dissolution deed must be accompanied, under penalty of nullity, by certificates from (Steichen, p. 490):
1. Centre commun de la securite sociale (social security)
2. Administration des contributions directes (direct taxes)
3. Administration de l'enregistrement et des domaines (registration and domains)

## 4. La liquidation de la societe (Liquidation)

### A. Le liquidateur (The Liquidator)

#### 1. Appointment

**Statutory Liquidator:**
Statutes may designate the liquidator in advance (liquidateur statutaire) - either by name or by position (e.g., "the managing directors") (Steichen, p. 491).

**Appointment by Assembly (Art. 1100-2 al. 1 L1915):**
"A defaut de convention contraire, (...) les liquidateurs sont nommes par l'assemblee generale des associes" (Steichen, p. 491).

**Majorities:**
- SNC and SARL: majority of half the shareholders holding three-quarters of capital (Art. 1100-2 al. 1 L1915) (Steichen, p. 492)
- SECS: three-quarters of partnership interests (Steichen, p. 492)
- These rules are suppletive (Steichen, p. 492)

**Judicial Appointment:**
Tribunal appoints liquidator when (Art. 1100-2 L1915) (Steichen, p. 492):
- Shareholders cannot agree on a person
- Tribunal has itself ordered the liquidation (Art. 100-20 L1915 for nullity)
- Judicial appointment is subsidiary; shareholders may later agree to substitute their chosen liquidator (Steichen, p. 492)

**Legal Person as Liquidator (Art. 1100-2 al. 3 L1915):**
- Permitted - natural person representative must be designated
- Same publication requirements as for directors (Steichen, p. 491)

#### 2. Powers

**Status of Liquidator:**
- Not "mandataire" of the company but an **organe** (organ) of the company (Steichen, p. 494)
- Represents the company, not individual shareholders (Steichen, p. 494)
- Not representative of creditors (unlike curateur in bankruptcy) (Steichen, p. 494)

**General Powers (Art. 1100-4 L1915):**
Powers sufficient to realize assets, including (Steichen, p. 495):
- Bringing and defending lawsuits
- Receiving all payments, giving releases
- Realizing securities
- Endorsing negotiable instruments
- Transacting or compromising on disputes

**Restrictions (suppletive - Art. 1100-4 and 1100-5 L1915):**

| Action | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| Sale of immovables | Assembly authorization (Art. 1100-5 L1915) (Steichen, p. 495) |
| New borrowings | Assembly authorization (Art. 1100-5 L1915) (Steichen, p. 495) |
| Pledging/mortgaging assets | Assembly authorization (Steichen, p. 495) |
| Contribution of assets to another company | Assembly authorization (Art. 1100-5 L1915) (Steichen, p. 496) |

#### 3. Termination of Functions

**Automatic Termination:**
- Death, interdiction, or deconfiture of liquidator (Art. 2003 C.civ.) (Steichen, p. 493)
- Bankruptcy of company (curateur takes over) (Steichen, p. 493)
- Expiration of limited term (Steichen, p. 493)

**Revocation:**
- Statutory liquidator: cannot be unilaterally revoked except for contractual non-performance (Steichen, p. 493)
- Assembly-appointed liquidator: same rules as for directors (Steichen, p. 493)
- Judicially-appointed liquidator: only by tribunal (Steichen, p. 493)

### B. Payment of Creditors

**Priority Rules (Art. 1100-8 L1915):**
- Secured creditors (privilegies, hypothecaires) are paid first (Steichen, p. 497)
- Unsecured creditors paid proportionally (principe d'egalite des creanciers) (Steichen, p. 497)
- Term debts become immediately due (with discount for early payment) (Steichen, p. 497)

**Insufficiency of Assets:**
If assets insufficient (Steichen, p. 497):
1. Call for unpaid capital from shareholders (Art. 1100-6 L1915)
2. Exercise action against unlimited liability partners
3. Declare bankruptcy if still insufficient

### C. Distribution to Shareholders

**Reimbursement of Contributions (Art. 1100-9 al. 1 L1915):**
- After payment or consignment of amounts for creditors
- In principle, nominal value of shares (Steichen, p. 497)
- May be in kind if the contributed asset remains in the estate (Art. 1872 C.civ. referencing succession rules) (Steichen, p. 498)

**Boni de liquidation:**
If funds remain after paying creditors and reimbursing contributions, this "liquidation bonus" is distributed to shareholders proportionally to their contributions (unless statutes provide otherwise) (Steichen, p. 498).

### D. Closure of Liquidation

**Annual Reporting (Art. 1100-14 L1915):**
Each year, liquidation results submitted to general assembly with indication of causes preventing completion (Steichen, p. 499).

**Commissaire a la liquidation (Art. 1100-15 L1915):**
When liquidation is complete, assembly appoints commissaire(s) to examine documents. Must be:
- Independent of liquidator (Steichen, p. 500)
- Reviseur d'entreprises agree if company previously required statutory audit (Steichen, p. 500)

**Final Assembly:**
- No quorum required (Steichen, p. 501)
- Decisions by ordinary majority (Steichen, p. 501)
- Votes on liquidator's report and discharge (decharge) (Steichen, p. 501)
- If assembly refuses to approve accounts: tribunal decides (Steichen, p. 501)

**Publication (Art. 1100-15 al. 2 L1915):**
Closure published in Memorial indicating:
- Location where books/documents will be kept for at least 5 years
- Measures taken for consignment of amounts due to creditors/shareholders (Steichen, p. 501)

**Radiation from RCS:**
Must occur within one month of closure (Art. 19-1 L27.05.2016) (Steichen, p. 501).

**Passive Survival (Art. 1400-6 L1915):**
Company continues to exist for 5 years after publication of closure to respond to creditors' actions - but only passively (cannot sue as plaintiff) (Steichen, p. 501).

## 5. Banking and Finance Application

### Regulated Entity Dissolution

**CSSF Notification:**
- Prior notification to CSSF required for dissolution of credit institutions and PSF
- CSSF may impose conditions or objections
- Liquidator appointment may require CSSF approval

**Protected Deposits:**
For credit institutions, depositor protection scheme (Fonds de garantie des depots Luxembourg) considerations apply.

### Fund Liquidation

Investment funds (OPC/OPCVM) follow specific liquidation procedures:
- CSSF notification and supervision
- Specific timelines for NAV calculation and distribution
- Investor protection provisions

### Intra-Group Liquidations

**TUP for Subsidiaries:**
Commonly used to simplify group structures:
- Parent company becomes sole shareholder
- Dissolves subsidiary without liquidation
- Universal transfer of assets and liabilities to parent
- Tax considerations: potential application of participation exemption on capital gains

**Documentation:**
- Board resolutions of both entities
- Shareholder decisions (notarial deed)
- Tax certificates
- CSSF notification where applicable
