# Societe en commandite simple (SCS) - Limited Partnership

> **Type**: Mixed Partnership / Societe de personnes et de capitaux
> **Source**: Steichen, *Precis de droit des societes*, Chapter 3, Part II (pp. 538-551)
> **Key Legislation**: Loi du 10 aout 1915, arts. 310-1 et seq. (reformed 2013)

---

## 1. Overview & Characteristics

### 1.1 Definition

**Steichen, n 711**: Art. 310-1 L1915 defines the *societe en commandite* as "celle que contractent (...) un ou plusieurs associes commandites indefiniment et solidairement responsables des engagements sociaux, avec un ou plusieurs associes commanditaires qui n'engagent qu'une mise determinee."

The limited partnership is contracted between:
- **Commandites** (general partners): unlimitedly and jointly liable
- **Commanditaires** (limited partners): liable only to extent of their contribution

### 1.2 Historical Background

**Steichen, n 710**: The *commandite* derives from the *contrat de commande*, widely used in maritime commerce and later in land commerce. This contract allowed nobles and robed professionals to clandestinely participate in commercial affairs (the financier remaining unknown to third parties).

### 1.3 Key Features

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Commandite Liability** | Unlimited and joint (*indefinie et solidaire*) |
| **Commanditaire Liability** | Limited to contribution (*mise determinee*) |
| **Minimum Capital** | None required |
| **Minimum Associates** | 1 commandite + 1 commanditaire (may be same person for different interests) |
| **Legal Personality** | Yes (SCS); No (SECsp - special variant) |
| **Commercial Character** | Commandites are merchants; commanditaires are not |

### 1.4 Two Variants Since 2013 Reform

**Steichen, n 712**:

| Variant | Legal Personality | Abbreviation |
|---------|-------------------|--------------|
| *Societe en commandite simple* | Yes | SCS / SECS |
| *Societe en commandite speciale* | No | SECsp |

**Steichen, n 713**: The SECsp can behave *as if* it had legal personality: has domicile, acts through management organ, issues partnership interests, can be liquidated. Only exceptions: cannot participate in mergers/demergers, no criminal liability.

## 2. Uses and Advantages

### 2.1 Traditional Uses

**Steichen, n 710-711**:

1. **Inventor + Financier Combination**
   - Inventor with expertise but limited capital (commandite)
   - Financier with capital (commanditaire)
   - Combines "finance" and "know-how"

2. **Continuation After Death**
   - SNC where associate dies leaving minor heirs
   - Transform to SCS with minors as commanditaires

3. **GmbH & Co KG Structure**
   - German-inspired fiscal structure
   - SARL as commandite, investors as commanditaires
   - Combines fiscal transparency with limited liability

### 2.2 Modern Investment Fund Use

**Steichen, n 711**: Since 2013 reform (implementing AIFMD directive), the SCS has seen renewed interest:
- Fund operators familiar with Anglo-Saxon *limited partnership*
- Deeply modernized legislation
- Strong contractual freedom (*primaute du contrat*)
- Tailored rules for dissolution conditions

## 3. Formation

### 3.1 Essential Characteristics

**Steichen, n 714**:

| Characteristic | Description |
|----------------|-------------|
| **Dual Categories** | Commandites + Commanditaires (distinct from *commandite par actions*) |
| **Parts d'interet** | Partnership interests (not freely negotiable like shares) |
| **Minimum Composition** | At least 1 commandite + 1 commanditaire (distinct persons) |
| **Cumulation** | Same person may be both, but must always have at least one distinct of each |

### 3.2 Formation Requirements

**Steichen, n 715**:

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Capital** | No minimum imposed by law |
| **Contributions** | Cash, in-kind, or industry (savoir-faire); per contract terms |
| **Register** | Must maintain register at registered office |
| **Minors** | May be commanditaires (not merchants) |

### 3.3 Limited Publication

**Steichen, n 715**: Statutes need NOT be fully published (*confidentialite des affaires*). Publication limited to:
- Designation of commandites
- Company name or denomination
- Corporate purpose and seat
- Designation of managers
- Duration

### 3.4 Corporate Name

**Steichen, n 715**: Since 2013 reform:
- SCS operates with *denomination sociale* (not *raison sociale*)
- Commanditaire name may appear in corporate name
- This alone does NOT create unlimited liability
- Unlimited liability requires *immixtion* in management

## 4. Governance

### 4.1 Management Structure

**Steichen, n 716**: "La gerance appartient a un ou plusieurs gerants, associes commandites ou non" (Art. 310-2 al. 1er L1915).

| Manager Type | Liability |
|--------------|-----------|
| **Commandite-Manager** | Unlimited personal liability |
| **Third-Party Manager** | Liability per SA director rules (Art. 441-9 L1915) |

**Steichen, n 716**: If no managers appointed, all commandites are deemed managers, each able to exercise full management powers alone.

### 4.2 Delegation of Powers

**Steichen, n 716**: Managers may delegate all or part of management powers to third parties. Delegation may cover entire management, not limited to specific operations (Art. 310-2 al. 3 L1915).

### 4.3 Powers vis-a-vis Third Parties

**Steichen, n 717**: Unlike typical partnerships:
- Manager acts bind company (like limited liability companies)
- Statutory restrictions on powers NOT opposable to third parties
- Company bound even if act exceeds corporate purpose (unless third party knew/should have known)

### 4.4 Decision-Making

**Steichen, n 718**:

| Decision Type | Default Rule |
|---------------|--------------|
| **Fundamental Changes** | 3/4 of partnership interests + all commandites |
| **Other Decisions** | Majority of votes cast |
| **Voting Basis** | Per partnership interests (not per capita), unless statutes provide otherwise |

**Fundamental Changes** (Art. 310-5 al. 2 L1915):
- Modification of corporate purpose
- Change of nationality
- Transformation
- Liquidation

## 5. Commanditaires (Limited Partners)

### 5.1 Prohibition on Management

**Steichen, n 719**: The commanditaire cannot perform *acts of management vis-a-vis third parties* (Art. 310-3 L1915).

**Dual Justification**:
1. **Protection of Third Parties**: Prevents confusion about who bears unlimited liability
2. **Protection of Commandites**: Prevents reckless acts by those not personally at risk

### 5.2 Permitted Activities

**Steichen, n 719-720**: The following are NOT management acts (permitted):

| Activity | Reference |
|----------|-----------|
| Exercise of associate prerogatives | Art. 310-3 L1915 |
| Advice and counsel to company/managers | Art. 310-3 L1915 |
| Control and supervision acts | Art. 310-3 L1915 |
| Granting loans, guarantees, security | Art. 310-3 L1915 |
| Authorizations to managers per statutes | Art. 310-3 L1915 |
| Acting as agent with disclosed capacity | Art. 310-3 dernier al. L1915 |

**Steichen, n 720**: Distinction is between:
- *Actes de gestion externe* (external management): PROHIBITED
- *Actes de gestion interne* (internal management): PERMITTED

### 5.3 Consequences of Prohibited Acts

**Steichen, n 721**:

| Violation | Consequence |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Occasional Acts** | Unlimited joint liability for those specific acts only (*responsabilite ponctuelle*) |
| **Habitual Acts** | Unlimited joint liability for ALL company debts (*responsabilite generale*) - even prior debts |

**Steichen, n 722**: This liability is exorbitant but operates ONLY vis-a-vis third parties. Internally, commanditaire retains limited partner status and may seek recourse for amounts exceeding contribution.

### 5.4 Transfer of Interests

**Steichen, n 725-726**:

| Default Rule | Modification |
|--------------|--------------|
| Consent of commandites required | Statutes may provide free transferability |
| On death: transfer without consent | Statutes may provide restrictions |
| May be freely negotiable (like SA shares) | Company may acquire own interests |

### 5.5 Limited Liability

**Steichen, n 727**: Commanditaires only liable to extent of contributions:
- No further contribution to losses beyond investment
- Excess losses borne by commandites alone
- Not merchants (no bankruptcy from company's insolvency)

---

## 6. Commandites (General Partners)

### 6.1 Status

**Steichen, n 723**: Commandites are in same position as SNC associates:
- Unlimited joint liability
- Merchant status
- Management rights (unless delegated)
- Subject to SNC dissolution rules

### 6.2 Events Affecting Commandites

**Steichen, n 724**: Events causing SNC dissolution have same effect when affecting commandite:
- Death
- Incapacity
- Bankruptcy
- Unless continuation clause exists

---

## 7. Profit Distribution and Losses

### 7.1 Profit Distribution

**Steichen, n 723**: Per *contractual freedom*, drafters may adopt any distribution key:
- Commandites often favored (greater risk)
- Commanditaires may be privileged
- Different treatment among commanditaires permitted
- Subject to prohibition of *clause leonine*

### 7.2 Loss Allocation

**Steichen, n 723**:

| Category | Loss Allocation |
|----------|-----------------|
| **Commanditaires** | Limited to contribution amount |
| **Commandites** | Bear remaining losses (unlimited) |

**Example** (Steichen, n 723): SCS with A, B (commandites, 40% each) and C (commanditaire, 20%, contribution 300). Losses of 3,000: C's theoretical share = 600, but capped at 300. A and B share remaining 2,700 (= 1,350 each).

## 8. Banking & Finance Application

### 8.1 Primary Use: Investment Funds

The SCS (and SECsp) have become the **vehicle of choice** for alternative investment funds in Luxembourg:

1. **AIFMD-Compliant Structures**
   - Fund manager as commandite (or manager)
   - Investors as commanditaires
   - Mirrors Anglo-Saxon limited partnership

2. **Private Equity Funds**
   - General partner with unlimited liability (often SARL)
   - Limited partners with capped exposure
   - Carried interest structures

3. **Real Estate Funds**
   - Property holding through SCS
   - Fiscal transparency available
   - Flexible governance

### 8.2 Key Advantages for Funds

| Advantage | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Contractual Freedom** | Nearly unlimited structuring flexibility |
| **No Minimum Capital** | Low formation cost |
| **Tax Transparency** | Pass-through treatment possible |
| **Limited Publication** | Confidentiality of investor details |
| **Familiar Structure** | Similar to common law LP |

### 8.3 Structuring Considerations

When advising on SCS:
- Identify who bears unlimited liability (commandite)
- Often use SARL/SA as commandite to limit exposure
- Draft statutes carefully re: management boundaries
- Define commanditaire activities precisely to avoid *immixtion*
- Consider SECsp if legal personality not needed

### 8.4 Regulatory Aspects

SCS may be used as:
- Regulated AIF (RAIF)
- Part II UCI
- SIF
- SICAR

## 9. SCS vs. SCSp: Comparative Analysis

### 9.1 The 2013 Reform

**Law of 12 July 2013** created the SCSp alongside the traditional SCS, implementing AIFMD while preserving contractual freedom.

> "La commandite spéciale constitue un cadre juridique parfaitement adapté aux véhicules de capital-investissement de type limited partnership."

**Source**: C. Duro, "La société en commandite simple et la société en commandite spéciale après la loi du 12 juillet 2013", ACE 2013/9, p. 3

### 9.2 Fundamental Distinctions

| Feature | SCS | SCSp |
|---------|-----|------|
| **Legal Personality** | Yes (Art. 310-1 L1915) | No (Art. 320-1 L1915) |
| **Patrimony** | Owns assets directly | Gesamthand (indivision spéciale) |
| **RCS Registration** | Required | Not registered |
| **Capital Structure** | Capital social | Parts d'intérêts only |
| **Capital Maintenance** | Rules apply | No capital fixity |
| **Creditor Protection** | Corporate veil | Partner liability rules |
| **Tax Treatment** | Corporate or transparent | Generally transparent |

**Source**: D. Boone & A. Trotska, "Le renouveau de la commandite (2ème partie)", JurisNews Vol. 5 No 4/2012, pp. 120-125

### 9.3 The Gesamthand Doctrine

The SCSp's absence of legal personality raised questions about asset holding. Luxembourg adopted the German **Gesamthand** (joint-hand) concept:

**Art. 320-2 L1915**: "Les biens meubles et immeubles de la société en commandite spéciale forment un patrimoine séparé de celui de chaque associé."

> "Le législateur a voulu doter la société en commandite spéciale d'un patrimoine propre et distinct du patrimoine personnel des associés, mais aussi du patrimoine de toute autre société en commandite spéciale dans laquelle l'un ou l'autre des associés aurait participé."

**Key Characteristics**:
- **Separate patrimony** distinct from each partner's personal assets
- **Protection from personal creditors** of partners
- **No need for trust structures** (unlike common law LP)
- **Asset segregation** without corporate veil

**Source**: P. Schleimer, "Réflexions sur le régime patrimonial de la société en commandite spéciale", Larcier 2013, pp. 49-65

### 9.4 Practical Choice: SCS or SCSp?

| Consideration | Prefer SCS | Prefer SCSp |
|---------------|------------|-------------|
| **Real estate holding** | Clearer title registration | Possible but complex |
| **Banking relationships** | Easier account opening | May require explanation |
| **Tax transparency** | Requires election | Default treatment |
| **Capital distribution** | Subject to rules | Free distribution |
| **Merger/demerger** | Eligible | Not eligible |
| **Litigation capacity** | Clear standing | Acts through gérant |
| **Confidentiality** | RCS publication | Greater privacy |
| **Fund structures** | Both suitable | Often preferred |

**Source**: D. Boone & A. Trotska, "Le renouveau de la commandite (3ème partie)", JurisNews Vol. 6 No 1-2/2013, p. 210

### 9.5 Parts d'Intérêts Regime

Both forms issue parts d'intérêts, but the regime differs:

**SCS**: Parts d'intérêts represent capital contributions and are subject to capital maintenance principles.

**SCSp**: Parts d'intérêts are purely contractual, with maximum flexibility:
- No nominal value requirement
- No equal value requirement
- Free redemption/withdrawal per contrat social
- Carried interest structures facilitated

> "Les modalités d'émission des parts d'intérêts sont laissées à la libre discrétion des associés, qui les définissent dans le contrat social."

**Source**: C. Duro, op. cit., pp. 12-13

### 9.6 International Recognition

The SCSp presents unique challenges:

| Jurisdiction | Recognition |
|--------------|-------------|
| **Common Law** | Generally recognized as partnership |
| **Civil Law** | May be treated as contract or entity |
| **Tax Treaties** | Requires analysis per treaty |
| **Security Interests** | Gesamthand assets vs. personal property |

**Banking Consideration**: When taking security over SCSp assets or parts d'intérêts, careful analysis of the Gesamthand nature is required. See [[Gage sur engagements investisseurs]].

**Source**: P. Schleimer, op. cit., pp. 58-62
