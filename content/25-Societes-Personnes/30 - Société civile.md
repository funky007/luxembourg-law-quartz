# Societe civile (Civil Partnership)

> **Type**: Partnership / Societe de personnes
> **Source**: Steichen, *Precis de droit des societes*, Chapter 1, Part II (pp. 511-527)
> **Key Legislation**: Code civil, arts. 1832-1873

---

## 1. Overview & Characteristics

### 1.1 Definition

**Steichen, n 661**: The *societe civile* (civil partnership) is defined residually: only those companies that are neither commercial by their object nor their form are civil companies. Civil partnerships with a commercial form are governed by the rules applicable to the commercial form they have adopted.

**Steichen, n 662**: Luxembourg continues to apply the Civil Code in its 1805 version to civil partnerships. The legal provisions are largely archaic but leave great freedom to contractual will (*volonte contractuelle*).

### 1.2 Key Features

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Liability** | Unlimited (*infinie*) but without solidarity (*sans solidarite*) - Art. 1862-1863 C.civ. |
| **Minimum Capital** | None required |
| **Minimum Associates** | 2 (no maximum) |
| **Legal Personality** | Yes (since 1933 law) |
| **Share Transferability** | Incessible by default (Art. 1861 C.civ.) |
| **Decision-making** | Unanimity required unless statutes provide otherwise |

### 1.3 Distinguishing Characteristics

**Steichen, n 661**: The civil partnership is marked by:
- Strong *intuitu personae* character
- Great importance of the will of the associates
- Parts are non-transferable by default
- Death or withdrawal of an associate dissolves the company
- Unlimited liability of associates (but not joint and several)
- Statutory changes require unanimity unless otherwise provided

## 2. Practical Uses

### 2.1 Common Applications

**Steichen, n 662**: Civil partnerships are frequently encountered in:

1. **Professional Activities** (*profession civile*)
   - Agricultural activities
   - Liberal professions
   - Intellectual professions

2. **Real Estate Management** (*patrimoine immobilier*)
   - Family real estate management
   - Advantages for estate planning and succession
   - Separation of real estate from business assets

3. **Business Real Estate Structures** (*societe civile immobiliere* - SCI)
   - Land and buildings held by SCI
   - Business (*fonds de commerce*) held by commercial company (typically SARL)
   - Associates of SCI remain outside commercial risks
   - Real estate protected from business creditors

**Steichen, n 662**: This separation places the real estate out of reach of business creditors (*hors d'atteinte des creanciers de l'entreprise*).

## 3. Formation

### 3.1 Requirements for Associates

**Steichen, n 664**: No particular capacity is required of associates despite their unlimited liability:
- Civil capacity suffices (commercial capacity not required)
- Minors may participate
- Incapacitated adults may participate
- Natural persons or legal entities (public or private law)
- Commercial companies may be associates
- Minimum: 2 associates; no maximum limit

### 3.2 Capital Requirements

**Steichen, n 664**: No minimum capital is required:
- Associates may freely determine capital amount
- May determine portion not immediately released
- May determine timing of release
- Unlimited liability serves as sufficient guarantee for creditors
- Possible to form a civil partnership without capital (only industry contributions)

### 3.3 Formalities

**Steichen, n 665**:

| Requirement | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| **Written Form** | Notarial deed or private agreement (Art. 100-4 L1915) |
| **Originals** | Only 2 originals required (even with more associates) |
| **Publication** | Full statutes published at RESA (Art. 100-10 L1915) |
| **Registration** | Mandatory registration at RCS (Art. 8 LRCS) |

**Exception for Family Companies** (*societe familiale*): Only extract publication required under 1933 law when 9/10 of shares held by family members.

### 3.4 Legal Personality

**Steichen, n 665**: Luxembourg jurisprudence early recognized civil partnerships as legal persons (*etres moraux*). The 1933 law confirmed that civil partnerships have "a legal individuality distinct from that of their associates" (*une individualite juridique distincte de celle de ses associes*).

## 4. Object and Duration

### 4.1 Civil Object Requirement

**Steichen, n 666**: The object must be civil. Civil activities are defined by exclusion from commercial acts listed in the Commercial Code:
- No repeated speculation (purchase for resale with profit)
- Commercial operations may be accessory if they complement the civil activity

**Steichen, n 666**: If commercial acts are performed regularly, the company is deemed to have *erroneously* (*par erreur*) taken the civil form. It will be treated as a *societe en nom collectif* (general partnership).

### 4.2 Duration

**Steichen, n 667**: In the absence of statutory provisions:
- Duration limited to the enterprise for which formed; OR
- Duration for the life of associates, with each having unilateral dissolution right

## 5. Governance

### 5.1 Management Structure

**Steichen, n 675**: The Code civil only provides suppletive rules; parties are entirely free to derogate in statutes.

**Two Management Modes** (Art. 1859 C.civ.):

| Mode | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **With Manager** (*gerant*) | Named manager has exclusive management powers |
| **Without Manager** | Associates deemed to have given each other reciprocal power to administer |

### 5.2 Manager Qualifications

**Steichen, n 676**: No particular legal capacity required:
- May be natural person or legal entity
- If legal entity, represented by natural person
- Managers typically chosen from associates (but not obligatory)
- Statutes freely fix number of managers

### 5.3 Types of Managers

**Steichen, n 678-679**:

| Type | Characteristics | Revocation |
|------|-----------------|------------|
| **Statutory Manager** (*gerant statutaire*) | Named in statutes | Requires unanimity OR "legitimate cause" (*cause legitime*) via court |
| **Ordinary Manager** (*gerant ordinaire*) | Named by separate act | Revocable at will (*ad nutum*); unanimity in silence of statutes |

### 5.4 Manager Powers

**Steichen, n 680-681**: Powers depend on statutes or nomination act. In silence:
- Managers have power for all acts related to company's object
- This includes acts of disposition (*actes de disposition*) if conforming to corporate purpose
- Acts beyond corporate purpose require unanimous consent

**Steichen, n 681**: Statutory limitations on manager powers are opposable to third parties (unlike in limited liability companies).

---

## 6. Associates

### 6.1 Rights and Obligations

**Steichen, n 689**: Liability of associates:
- **Unlimited** (*indefinie*): extends to entire personal patrimony
- **Not joint and several** (*sans solidarite*): each associate liable only for their share
- Division of liability: proportional to share in company (Art. 1863 C.civ.)

### 6.2 Share Transfers

**Steichen, n 691**:

| Transfer Type | Rule |
|---------------|------|
| **To Third Parties** | Requires unanimous consent (Art. 1861 C.civ.) |
| **Between Associates** | May be facilitated by statutes |
| **Pledging** | Possible with creditor's agreement |

### 6.3 Decision-Making

**Steichen, n 687**:
- Unanimity required for statutory modifications (unless statutes provide otherwise)
- Statutes may provide for reinforced majority for certain matters
- Unanimous decision cannot alter essence of partnership contract

---

## 7. Dissolution

### 7.1 Causes of Dissolution

**Steichen, n 669**: No specific causes beyond general dissolution causes:
- Death of an associate (unless contrary clause)
- Insolvency (*deconfiture*) of an associate
- Arrival of term
- Achievement or impossibility of corporate purpose
- Judicial dissolution for just cause

**Steichen, n 669**: Revocation of associate-manager does NOT cause dissolution.

### 7.2 Transformation

**Steichen, n 668**: Civil partnership may transform into:
- Commercial company (limited or unlimited liability)
- Decision valid with 60% of associates (regardless of capital percentage)
- No creation of new legal person
- Unless prohibited by statutes

### 7.3 Liquidation

**Steichen, n 670**: Following 2016 law reform:
- Legal personality survives dissolution
- Associates do not fall into co-ownership (*indivision*)
- Standard liquidation rules apply (Art. 1100-1 L1915)

## 8. Banking & Finance Application

### 8.1 Use in Wealth Structuring

The *societe civile immobiliere* (SCI) is commonly used in banking and wealth management contexts:

1. **Family Wealth Planning**
   - Real estate held separately from operating businesses
   - Facilitates succession planning through share transfers
   - Protects family assets from business creditors

2. **Real Estate Investment**
   - Holding structure for real estate portfolios
   - Rental income generation
   - Capital gains planning

3. **Professional Practices**
   - Law firms, accounting firms, medical practices
   - Liberal professionals organizing their activities

### 8.2 Tax Considerations

**Steichen, n 662-663**: Important fiscal implications:
- If civil partnership performs commercial acts regularly, treated as commercial for tax
- Transfer of shares in real estate SCI subject to proportional registration duties (since 2001 law)
- "Business split" (*scission d'entreprise*) doctrine may apply when SCI and operating company have same associates

### 8.3 Limitations in Banking

Civil partnerships are **not suitable** for:
- Banking activities (regulated entities)
- Insurance activities
- Activities requiring minimum capital
- Public offerings
