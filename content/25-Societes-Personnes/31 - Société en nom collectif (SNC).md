# Societe en nom collectif (SNC) - General Partnership

> **Type**: Commercial Partnership / Societe de personnes
> **Source**: Steichen, *Precis de droit des societes*, Chapter 2, Part II (pp. 528-538)
> **Key Legislation**: Loi du 10 aout 1915, arts. 200-1 et seq.

---

## 1. Overview & Characteristics

### 1.1 Definition

**Steichen, n 695**: "La societe en nom collectif est celle dans laquelle tous les associes sont indefiniment et solidairement tenus de tous les engagements de la societe" (Art. 200-1 L1915).

The *societe en nom collectif* (general partnership) is the company in which **all associates are unlimitedly and jointly liable for all company obligations**.

### 1.2 Historical Background

**Steichen, n 695**: The SNC constitutes the archetype of partnerships (*societe de personnes*). Already recognized in the *Ordonnance de Colbert* under the qualifier "general company," it owes its name to POTHIER and SAVARY who wrote that associates conducted their commerce "under their collective name."

Since the 2016 law, the SNC no longer has a *raison sociale* (firm name); it operates, like all companies, with a *denomination sociale* (corporate name).

### 1.3 Key Features

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Liability** | Unlimited AND joint and several (*indefinie et solidaire*) |
| **Minimum Capital** | None required (symbolic 1 EUR suffices) |
| **Minimum Associates** | 2 |
| **Legal Personality** | Yes |
| **Commercial Status** | Associates are merchants (*commercants*) |
| **Decision-making** | Unanimity for modifications (unless statutes provide otherwise) |
| **Tax Treatment** | Fiscally transparent |

### 1.4 Default Company Form

**Steichen, n 695**: The SNC is the *societe de droit commun* (default company) - the form retained by courts if associates conduct commerce without adopting a specific legal form, or if they use a company type unknown in Luxembourg law.

## 2. Advantages and Uses

### 2.1 Practical Advantages

**Steichen, n 696**:

1. **Control over Membership**
   - Unanimous consent required for share transfers
   - Excludes unwanted third parties (*tiers juges indesirables*)

2. **Confidentiality** (*discretion*)
   - No obligation to publish annual accounts (exception: if all associates are limited liability companies)
   - No requirement for auditor (*commissaire* or *reviseur d'entreprises*)

3. **Fiscal Transparency**
   - Immediate taxation of profits in associates' hands (even without distribution)
   - Immediate pass-through of tax losses to associates
   - Attractive for start-up phase with initial losses

## 3. Formation

### 3.1 Requirements for Associates

**Steichen, n 697**:

| Requirement | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| **Capacity** | Must have commercial capacity (*capacite commerciale*) |
| **Excluded** | Minors (even emancipated), adults under guardianship/curatorship, persons disqualified from commerce |
| **Legal Entities** | Permitted; civil companies cannot be associates |
| **Multiple Memberships** | Associates may participate in other SNCs |

**Steichen, n 697**: Associates acquire merchant status (*qualite de commercant*) by operation of law. However, they need not individually register at RCS or maintain personal commercial accounts - only the company exercises commercial activity.

### 3.2 Capital and Contributions

**Steichen, n 697**:

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Minimum Capital** | None (symbolic 1 EUR possible) |
| **Contributions in Kind** | Valued in deed; no auditor report required |
| **Industry Contributions** | Permitted but not part of capital |
| **Founders Liability** | No special "founders" concept or liability |

### 3.3 Formalities

**Steichen, n 698**:

| Requirement | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| **Form** | Notarial deed OR private agreement (Art. 100-4 L1915) |
| **Originals** | As many originals as parties with distinct interests |
| **Publication** | Extract at RESA signed by all associates |
| **Content** | Must identify all associates precisely (names, addresses, etc.) |

### 3.4 Amendments

**Steichen, n 698**: Modifications to articles of association require:
- Unanimity of associates (default rule)
- May be amended by reinforced majority if statutes permit
- No parallelism of forms: notarial deed may be amended by private agreement
- Changes must be published

## 4. Dissolution

### 4.1 Personal Causes

**Steichen, n 699**: The SNC, more than any other commercial company, is affected by events concerning individual associates:

| Event | Effect |
|-------|--------|
| **Interdiction** (*mise en tutelle*) | Dissolution |
| **Death** | Dissolution |
| **Bankruptcy** | Dissolution |
| **Loss of Confidence** | Dissolution |

### 4.2 Continuation Clauses

**Steichen, n 699**: Associates may mitigate these effects:
- **Continuation between survivors**: Heirs receive cash value, not share in company
- This constitutes an *exceptionally valid pact on future succession* (Art. 1868 C.civ.)
- Heirs have no company rights, only monetary claim

**Steichen, n 700**: If heir is minor, company must transform into *commandite simple* with minor as *commanditaire*.

### 4.3 Duration-Related Dissolution

**Steichen, n 702**:

| Duration | Withdrawal Right |
|----------|------------------|
| **Indefinite** | Any associate may withdraw (good faith, not untimely) |
| **Fixed Term** | No withdrawal before term; no transfer to third parties |

### 4.4 Croupier Arrangement

**Steichen, n 702**: An associate may privately share their interest with a *croupier* (silent participant). This creates a small company between associate and croupier, without affecting the SNC itself.

## 5. Governance

### 5.1 Management Structure

**Steichen, n 703**: The 1915 law provides no specific rules for SNC management. Civil partnership rules apply by reference.

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Manager** (*gerant*) | One or more; natural or legal person |
| **Selection** | Usually from associates; third parties rare |
| **Representation** | Company acts through representative organ |
| **Court Actions** | Company name only; manager names not required |

### 5.2 Third-Party Managers

**Steichen, n 704**: Third-party managers are rare because associates dislike having their unlimited liability engaged by someone who (absent personal fault) would not be personally liable for obligations incurred on behalf of the company.

### 5.3 Manager Powers

**Steichen, n 704-705**:

| Relationship | Rules |
|--------------|-------|
| **Internal** | Per statutes; otherwise civil partnership rules |
| **External** | Managers bind company within corporate purpose |
| **Object Limitation** | Acts within corporate purpose; otherwise unanimous consent |
| **Statutory Limits** | Limits are opposable to third parties |

**Steichen, n 705**: Managers have power for all *acts of administration* - not to be taken literally; includes acts of disposition necessary to achieve corporate purpose.

### 5.4 Manager Liability

**Steichen, n 705**: Managers are subject to same obligations as directors of companies generally.

## 6. Associates

### 6.1 Role in Management

**Steichen, n 704**: When a manager is appointed, non-manager associates have no role in management. The manager has exclusive management powers (*exclusivite de la gestion sociale*).

### 6.2 Assembly of Associates

**Steichen, n 706**: In absence of specific rules:
- Apply "deliberating assemblies" common law
- Each associate has one vote
- Ordinary decisions: absolute majority
- Extraordinary decisions (statutory amendments): unanimity
- Unless statutes provide otherwise

**Steichen, n 706**: The assembly may:
- Control management closely
- Deliberate on acts exceeding manager powers
- Approve accounts
- Ratify acts beyond powers (unanimous consent)
- Prohibit specific acts

### 6.3 Liability of Associates

**Steichen, n 707-709**: This is the essential characteristic of the SNC.

| Aspect | Rule |
|--------|------|
| **Indefinite** | Associates liable with entire personal patrimony |
| **Solidary** | Creditor may pursue any associate for full debt |
| **Public Order** | Cannot be limited by statutes |
| **Benefit of Division** | Does NOT apply |

**Steichen, n 708**: The solidarity is "*parfaite*" (perfect) in civil law sense:
- Actions against one associate interrupt prescription for all
- Fault of one produces effect against all
- Associate who pays may recover from others proportionally

### 6.4 Subsidiary Liability

**Steichen, n 710**: Associates are "*debiteurs subsidiaires*" (subsidiary debtors):
- Not co-debtors with company, nor guarantors
- Called to pay only if company fails to satisfy obligations
- Creditors can pursue company directly
- Also pursue associates who are personally liable

**Steichen, n 710**: The question is whether actions must be sequential or may be concurrent - must the creditor first pursue the company? The answer is:
- Creditors may pursue the company
- Creditors may also pursue associates
- Associates must be warned of company insolvency first

### 6.5 Bankruptcy Consequences

**Steichen, n 709**: Bankruptcy of the SNC automatically entails bankruptcy of all associates (*faillite entrainera d'elle-meme la faillite des associes*):
- Associates being merchants, they are bankrupt
- Company bankruptcy must be prior to associate bankruptcies
- Separate bankruptcy estates for company and each associate
- Inverse NOT true: associate bankruptcy merely causes company dissolution

## 7. Banking & Finance Application

### 7.1 Uses in Finance

The SNC has limited direct application in mainstream banking due to unlimited liability, but is used in:

1. **Tax Planning Structures**
   - Pass-through taxation advantageous for certain investors
   - Loss pass-through for start-up investments
   - Often with SARL as sole associate (*GmbH & Co KG* model via SCS)

2. **Professional Services**
   - Law firms (though civil partnerships more common)
   - Accounting partnerships
   - Where personal commitment signaled

3. **Joint Ventures**
   - Where partners want deep commitment
   - Control over membership changes

### 7.2 Limitations

The SNC is **not suitable** for:
- Banking activities (capital requirements, regulation)
- Insurance activities
- Activities requiring limited liability
- Large-scale operations with multiple investors

### 7.3 Structuring Considerations

When advising on SNCs:
- Associates must understand unlimited joint liability
- Bankruptcy of company triggers associate bankruptcies
- No protection from creditors
- Best used with creditworthy associates
- Consider SCS if some investors need limited liability
