# Societes cooperatives - Cooperative Companies

> **Type**: Hybrid Form / Forme sui generis
> **Source**: Steichen, *Precis de droit des societes*, Chapter 4, Part II (pp. 551-562)
> **Key Legislation**: Loi du 10 aout 1915, arts. 811-1 et seq.

---

## 1. Overview & Characteristics

### 1.1 Definition

**Steichen, n 729**: Art. 811-1 L1915 defines the cooperative as: "celle qui se compose d'associes dont le nombre ou les apports sont variables et dont les parts sont incessibles a des tiers. Elle est a responsabilite illimitee ou limitee."

The cooperative is characterized by:
- Variable number of associates
- Variable contributions
- Non-transferable shares (to third parties)
- Choice of unlimited or limited liability

### 1.2 Historical Background

**Steichen, n 728**: The 1808 Commercial Code only knew three company types (SNC, commandite, SA). The cooperative was merely a specific application of existing forms. With the 1915 law, the cooperative became a distinct type (*forme propre*).

### 1.3 Key Features

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Liability** | Limited OR unlimited (choice in statutes) |
| **Capital** | Variable (*capital variable*) |
| **Associates** | Variable number; minimum 3 (since 2016) |
| **Shares** | Non-transferable to third parties (*incessibles aux tiers*) |
| **Character** | Hybrid between capital and personal companies |

### 1.4 Essential Characteristics

**Steichen, n 730-731**:

| Characteristic | Description |
|----------------|-------------|
| **Variable Capital** | Open-door technique (*porte ouverte*) |
| **Non-Transferability** | Mandatory (*d'ordre public*); excludes speculation |
| **Dual Relationship** | Associates are both members AND clients/suppliers |

## 2. Cooperative Principles

### 2.1 The Cooperative Ideal

**Steichen, n 732**: The cooperative emerged from the 18th-century cooperative movement in Great Britain as popular associations. Goals:
- Improve conditions of workers and middle classes
- Eliminate intermediaries and employers
- Eliminate capitalist profit

### 2.2 The Ristourne Concept

**Steichen, n 732**: The *ristourne* (rebate) exemplifies cooperative philosophy:

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Consumer Cooperative** | Sells products to members at margin; margin redistributed proportionally to purchases |
| **Producer Cooperative** | Buys from members at favorable prices; distributes surplus |

**Steichen, n 732**: The ristourne represents refund of "excess collected" - seemingly eliminating profit. But it enables associates to save, which is a form of benefit sharing.

### 2.3 Distinction from Associations

**Steichen, n 733**: Though close to associations in some respects, cooperatives differ:
- Perform commercial activities (production, distribution, credit)
- These activities cannot be performed by associations
- Best viewed as a "*categorie sui generis*"

## 3. Formation

### 3.1 Conditions of Substance

**Steichen, n 734**:

| Requirement | Rule |
|-------------|------|
| **Minimum Associates** | 3 (reduced from 7 by 2016 law) |
| **Associate Capacity** | Civil capacity suffices (not commercial) |
| **Capital** | Must be fully subscribed; no minimum; no immediate release requirement |
| **Liability** | If limited: capital split into fixed and variable portions |

### 3.2 Formal Requirements

**Steichen, n 735**:

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Form** | Notarial deed OR private agreement |
| **Originals** | Only 2 required (even with more associates) |
| **Publication** | Full statutes published at Memorial |
| **Content** | Must state object (commercial), duration |

### 3.3 Object Requirement

**Steichen, n 734**: Object must be commercial (Art. 811-3 n 2 L1915).

### 3.4 Duration

**Steichen, n 735**:
- Freely fixed in statutes
- May be unlimited
- If unlimited: dissolution by majority vote (SA rules) or just cause

## 4. Governance

### 4.1 Management Structure

**Steichen, n 736**:

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Administration** | Per statutes; collégiale or single manager |
| **Open to** | Associates only OR third parties |
| **Default Rule** | If silent: SA rules apply (single administrator) |
| **Publication** | Extract of nomination deed at RCS |

### 4.2 Assembly of Associates

**Steichen, n 736**:

| Element | Statutory Freedom | Default Rule |
|---------|-------------------|--------------|
| **Voting Rights** | Statutes may exclude some; multi-vote shares permitted | All associates vote equally |
| **Convocation** | Per statutes | Registered letter |
| **Decisions** | Unanimity, majority, or less than 50% | SA assembly rules |

### 4.3 Control

**Steichen, n 737**:

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Auditor** (*commissaire*) | Per statutes; default: one auditor (SA rules) |
| **Powers** | Surveillance only (cannot convene assembly unless statutes permit) |

### 4.4 Corporate Name

**Steichen, n 737**: Cooperatives operate with *denomination sociale* (not *raison sociale*):
- All documents must include "Societe cooperative"
- Violation: personal liability of interveners

## 5. Associates

### 5.1 Choice of Liability Regime

**Steichen, n 738**: Associates choose in statutes:

| Regime | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| **Unlimited Joint** | Like SNC associates |
| **Unlimited Divisible** | Like civil partnership associates |
| **Limited** | Like capital company shareholders |
| **Default** | Unlimited joint (Art. 811-5 n 6 L1915) |

**Steichen, n 738**: Error on liability regime may annul subscription if it was the "determining cause."

### 5.2 Share Register

**Steichen, n 739**:

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Form** | Nominative shares only |
| **Register** | Names, residence, capital contributed/withdrawn |
| **Update** | Every 6 months |
| **Deposit** | List filed at tribunal (enables third-party verification) |
| **Certificate** | Associates receive representative (not constitutive) certificate |

### 5.3 Profit and Loss Sharing

**Steichen, n 740**:

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Profit Share** | Per statutes; default: 1/2 equal, 1/2 per contribution |
| **Loss Contribution** | Per statutes |
| **On Withdrawal** | Only entitled to reimbursement of contribution (no surplus) |

### 5.4 Entry and Exit

**Steichen, n 731**:

| Movement | Mechanism |
|----------|-----------|
| **Entry** | Subscription (*adhesion*) - capital increase |
| **Exit** | Reimbursement (*retrait*) or exclusion |
| **No Direct Transfer** | No juridical link between incoming and outgoing associate |

### 5.5 Limited Transferability

**Steichen, n 731**:
- Transfer to third parties: **PROHIBITED** (public order)
- Transfer to existing associates: possible if statutes permit
- Rationale: eliminate speculation; preserve worker-oriented character

## 6. Cooperative SA (CoopSA)

### 6.1 Introduction

**Steichen, n 742-743**: A 1999 law created the "cooperative organized as a societe anonyme" to serve pension funds (SEPCAV and ASSEP). This hybrid combines:
- Cooperative characteristics (variable capital, non-transferability)
- SA governance and structure

### 6.2 Dual Regime

**Steichen, n 743**: Art. 820-1 L1915:

| Applicable Rules | Modifications |
|------------------|---------------|
| Cooperative provisions | With adaptations |
| SA provisions | With adaptations |

**Steichen, n 743**: In case of conflict, cooperative rules prevail (SA rules are suppletive).

### 6.3 Key Differences from Classic Cooperative

**Steichen, n 744-745**:

| Element | Classic Cooperative | CoopSA |
|---------|---------------------|--------|
| **Administration** | Flexible | Board of directors + general meeting |
| **Auditor** | Single auditor | Commissaire required |
| **Assembly Rules** | Cooperative rules | More flexible cooperative rules |
| **Shares** | Certificats | Nominative or dematerialized shares |

### 6.4 Variable Capital Mechanism

**Steichen, n 747**: The CoopSA maintains variable capital:
- No formal increase/decrease procedures
- Capital fluctuates with entries/exits
- Must distinguish fixed and variable portions

## 7. European Cooperative Society (SCE)

### 7.1 Overview

**Steichen, n 748-749**: The *Societe cooperative europeenne* (SCE) is based on EU Regulation 1435/2003.

| Element | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Purpose** | Enable cooperatives from different member states to unite |
| **Foundation** | Cross-border merger or formation |
| **Minimum Capital** | EUR 30,000 |
| **Seat** | Must be in member state where head office located |

### 7.2 Characteristics

**Steichen, n 750**:
- Resident in multiple member states (minimum 2)
- Variable membership and capital
- Members liable only to capital subscribed (unless statutes provide otherwise)

### 7.3 Limited Relevance

**Steichen, n 748**: The SCE has limited practical interest in Luxembourg due to complexity and limited cross-border cooperative activity.

## 8. Banking & Finance Application

### 8.1 Primary Uses

Cooperatives have specific applications in finance:

1. **Pension Funds** (SEPCAV/ASSEP)
   - CoopSA form specifically designed
   - Variable capital suits pension contributions
   - Associates are both members and beneficiaries

2. **Credit Unions** (*caisses de credit*)
   - Traditional cooperative banking
   - Members are depositors and borrowers

3. **Agricultural Cooperatives**
   - Banking services for members
   - Cooperative credit arrangements

### 8.2 Advantages in Finance

| Advantage | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Variable Capital** | Accommodates ongoing subscriptions/redemptions |
| **Non-Speculation** | Aligns with pension fund/mutual philosophy |
| **Dual Relationship** | Members as investors and beneficiaries |
| **Flexibility** | Liability regime choice |

### 8.3 Limitations

Cooperatives are **less suitable** for:
- Traditional banking (capital requirements)
- Investment funds (alternative structures preferred)
- For-profit ventures (non-speculation principle)

### 8.4 Regulatory Considerations

When advising on cooperatives:
- SEPCAV/ASSEP subject to CAA supervision
- Credit cooperatives subject to CSSF
- Consider liability regime carefully
- Exit mechanisms important for pension structures
