# Clause pénale (art. 1152)

> **Type**: Contractual damages clause  
> **Article**: 1152 Code civil  
> **Effect**: Pre-agreed compensation for breach

---

## Definition

Clause by which parties **pre-determine** the compensation due in case of breach.

Two functions:
1. **Indemnitaire**: Compensate for breach
2. **Comminatoire**: Pressure debtor to perform

---

## Two Types

### 1. Clause Pénale Compensatoire

Compensates for **total non-performance** or defective performance.

**Replaces** damages assessment by judge.

### 2. Clause Pénale Moratoire

Compensates for **delay** (retard) in performance.

**Cumulates** with performance of main obligation.

---

## Effects

### Binding on Judge

> **Art. 1152 al. 1**: Judge must apply the agreed penalty — cannot substitute own assessment.

### Exception: Judicial Modification

> **Art. 1152 al. 2**: Judge may **reduce or increase** penalty if it is "manifestement excessive ou dérisoire."

**Test**: Manifest disproportion — not mere difference from actual damage.

---

## Survival After Resolution

### Clause Pénale Compensatoire

> **Poelmans §217**: Survives resolution — designed to compensate consequences of contract's failure.

### Clause Pénale Moratoire

> Does **NOT** survive resolution — purpose (sanctioning delay) incompatible with termination.

**Rationale**: Moratoire clause contemplates eventual performance; resolution ends that possibility.

---

## Indivisibility and Clause Pénale

> **Art. 1232 C.civ.**: When indivisible obligation has clause pénale, and one co-debtor defaults:

- Penalty may be demanded **in full** from defaulting debtor
- Or **proportionally** from each co-debtor
- Innocent co-debtors have recourse against defaulter

**Case** (Poelmans): Spouses sold apartment; one refused to sign notarial deed. Court held: obligation with clause pénale is indivisible; refusing spouse liable for full penalty.

---

## Conditions for Application

### 1. Valid Contract

Clause pénale accessory to main obligation.

### 2. Breach

Non-performance, defective performance, or delay.

### 3. Imputable to Debtor

[[Force majeure (arts. 1147-1148)]] exonerates from penalty too.

### 4. Mise en Demeure

Generally required before penalty accrues.

---

## Distinction from Related Clauses

| Clause | Function |
|--------|----------|
| **Clause pénale** | Pre-agreed damages |
| **Clause limitative** | Caps liability |
| **Clause exonératoire** | Excludes liability |
| **Astreinte** | Judicial pressure measure |

---

## Banking Application

### Late Payment Penalties

Common in loan agreements:
- Default interest (moratoire)
- Break costs (compensatoire)

### Prepayment Penalties

May be characterized as clause pénale → subject to judicial review if excessive.

### Guarantees

Penalty clauses in guarantees:
- Subject to same rules
- May be reduced if manifestly excessive
