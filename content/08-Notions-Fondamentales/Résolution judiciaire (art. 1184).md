# Résolution judiciaire (art. 1184)

> **Type**: Remedy for non-performance  
> **Article**: 1184 Code civil  
> **Nature**: Implied in synallagmatic contracts

---

## The Principle

> **Art. 1184**: "La condition résolutoire est toujours sous-entendue dans les contrats synallagmatiques, pour le cas où l'une des deux parties ne satisfera point à son engagement."

Resolution is **implied** in every synallagmatic contract.

---

## Creditor's Choice

The innocent party may choose between:
1. **Exécution forcée** — compel performance
2. **Résolution** — terminate + damages

> Option may be changed during proceedings until final judgment.

---

## Conditions

### 1. Synallagmatic Contract

**Excluded contracts** (Poelmans §211):
- **Partage** (partition) — declaratory effect precludes resolution
- **Rente viagère** (art. 1978) — aleatory character

**Included**:
- **Transaction** — can be resolved for breach (despite debate)
- **Donation with charge** (art. 953) — application of art. 1184

### 2. Creditor of Unperformed Obligation

Claimant must be the party whose obligation was not performed.

> Debtor who didn't perform cannot seek resolution to escape own obligations.

### 3. Mise en Demeure

Generally required before seeking resolution.

### 4. Judicial Pronouncement

> **Principle**: Contract not resolved *de plein droit* — must be requested from court.

**Case**: Buyer's letter declaring contract resolved ≠ valid resolution — must go to court.

### 5. Sufficient Gravity

> **Poelmans §215**: Judge resolves only if breach so serious that victim would not have contracted had they foreseen it.

Partial breach may suffice if it affects essential obligations.

---

## Exception: Résolution Unilatérale

Despite art. 1184's judicial requirement, jurisprudence admits unilateral resolution:

### Standard Procedure

1. **Mise en demeure** to debtor
2. **Reasonable time** to perform
3. If still no performance → **notify resolution**

### Urgency Exception

When waiting would cause **constant worsening** of prejudice:
- No mise en demeure required
- Immediate notification permitted

### Safeguards

> Creditor must preserve debtor's defense rights for *a posteriori* judicial control.

**Risk**: If court finds resolution unjustified → resolving party liable.

---

## Effects

### Retroactive

Contract deemed **never to have existed**.

### Restitutions

Parties return what they received:
- In kind if possible
- By equivalent if not

### Exception: Successive Contracts

For ongoing performance contracts → **ex nunc** only (prospective).

### Damages

Innocent party may claim damages for prejudice caused by breach.

---

## Waiver

> **Poelmans §220**: Right to resolution is **not ordre public**.

Parties may contractually waive right to resolution.
