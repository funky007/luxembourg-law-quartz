# Obligations indivisibles

> **Type**: Obligations à sujets multiples  
> **Articles**: 1217-1225 Code civil  
> **Feature**: Cannot be divided by nature or intention

---

## Definition

> An obligation is **indivisible** when it cannot be performed in parts, either by its **nature** or by parties' **intention**.

---

## Sources of Indivisibility

### 1. Natural Indivisibility (art. 1217)

Object **physically cannot** be divided:
- Deliver specific unique item
- Create specific work of art
- Grant a servitude

### 2. Conventional Indivisibility (art. 1218)

Parties **intended** obligation to be indivisible:
- Even if object could be divided
- Express or implied from contract

---

## Comparison with Solidarité

| Aspect | Solidarité | Indivisibilité |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| **Source** | Express/legal | Nature/intention |
| **Basis** | Persons | Object |
| **Survives to heirs** | Divides among heirs | Passes indivisibly to heirs |
| **Representative effects** | Yes | Limited |

**Key difference**: Indivisibility **survives death** — heirs remain bound for whole.

---

## Effects — Passive Indivisibility

### Multiple Debtors

Each debtor liable for **whole** obligation.

### Creditor's Rights

- Demand full performance from any debtor
- Single performance liberates all

### Performance by One

> **Poelmans**: Does **not** necessarily liberate others — depends on object.

If obligation is **ne pas faire** (not to do):
- One debtor's breach ≠ others' breach
- But performance by one satisfies creditor

---

## Effects — Active Indivisibility

### Multiple Creditors

Each creditor can demand **whole** performance.

### Debtor's Protection

Payment to one creditor liberates debtor toward all.

---

## Clause Pénale and Indivisibility

> **Art. 1232**: When indivisible obligation has clause pénale:

| Scenario | Effect |
|----------|--------|
| One co-debtor defaults | Penalty may be claimed in full from defaulter |
| | Or proportionally from each co-debtor |
| | Innocent co-debtors have recourse |

**Case** (Poelmans): Spouses selling apartment — one refused to sign. Court held obligation with clause pénale indivisible; refusing spouse liable for full penalty.

---

## Recourse Among Co-Debtors

### After Payment

Paying debtor has **recourse** against co-debtors:
- For their share of the debt
- According to internal agreement or interests

### Division for Recourse

Even though indivisible toward creditor, debt **divides internally** among co-debtors.

---

## Resolution and Indivisibility

> Non-performance by one co-debtor allows creditor to seek resolution against **all**.

But: Only defaulting co-debtor liable for **damages** (others' fault not presumed).

---

## Banking Application

### Joint Borrowers

If obligation characterized as indivisible:
- Each borrower liable for whole
- Survives to heirs (unlike mere solidarity)

### Security Interests

Indivisibility often stipulated:
- Mortgage indivisible (each heir's property remains fully charged)
- Pledge cannot be divided

### Guarantee Obligations

Careful drafting:
- Solidarité for personal liability
- Indivisibility for inheritance protection
