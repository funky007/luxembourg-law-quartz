# Non-cumul des responsabilités

> **Type**: Fundamental principle  
> **Effect**: Cannot combine contractual and delictual liability  
> **Scope**: Between contracting parties

---

## The Principle

> **Fundamental Rule**: Contractual and extra-contractual liability **cannot be combined** (*non-cumul*).

If a contract exists between parties, the liability regime is **necessarily contractual**.

---

## Rationale

### Contract as Complete Regime

The contract:
- Allocates risks between parties
- Defines obligations and remedies
- Parties' agreement governs their relationship

### Preventing Forum Shopping

Without non-cumul, victims could choose more favorable regime:
- Longer prescription
- Better damages rules
- Easier proof

---

## Practical Consequences

| Aspect | Contractual (art. 1147) | Delictual (art. 1382) |
|--------|-------------------------|----------------------|
| **Basis** | Contract breach | General fault |
| **Prescription** | Per contract/law | 30 years (commercial: 10) |
| **Damages** | Foreseeable only (art. 1150) | All direct damage |
| **Proof** | Depends on obligation type | Claimant proves fault |

### Damages Limitation

> **Art. 1150**: Contractual damages limited to **foreseeable** harm (unless dol).

Delictual damages: all **direct** harm, foreseeable or not.

### Prescription Differences

Commercial context:
- Contractual: Often 10 years (art. 189 C.com)
- Delictual: Also 10 years for commercial matters

---

## Application

### Step 1: Is There a Contract?

If yes → contractual regime applies exclusively.

### Step 2: What Obligation Was Breached?

- Express terms
- Implied terms (art. 1135)
- Good faith duties (art. 1134 al. 3)

### Step 3: Was Breach Proven?

Depends on obligation type:
- **Moyens**: Creditor proves fault
- **Résultat**: Failure = breach (debtor must prove exoneration)

---

## Exceptions

### Concurrent Victims

If same act harms:
- **Contractual party**: Contractual regime
- **Third party**: Delictual regime

Each victim governed by their applicable regime.

### Criminal Fault

Criminal conduct may open delictual avenue even between contractors.

### Separate Relationships

If damage relates to relationship **outside** the contract, delictual rules may apply.

### Personal Injury

Some systems allow delictual action for personal injury even in contractual context — less clear in Luxembourg.

---

## Banking Application

### Bank-Client Relationship

Contract exists → all claims governed by contractual liability:
- Investment advice failures
- Payment order errors
- Information duty breaches

### Bank-Third Party Relationship

No contract → delictual liability:
- Fraudulent transfers affecting third parties
- Negligent statements relied on by non-clients
- Director liability to creditors

### Professional Liability

Directors toward company: Contractual (mandat)
Directors toward shareholders: Potentially delictual (no direct contract)

---

## Key Point

> **Always determine**: Is there a contract between these parties?

If yes → you are in contractual territory, with its rules on:
- Proof
- Prescription  
- Damages limitations
- Available defenses
