# Cause (arts. 1131-1133)

> **Type**: Condition de validité (art. 1108)  
> **Articles**: 1131-1133 Code civil  
> **Luxembourg Specificity**: Cause doctrine **retained** (unlike France 2016)

---

## Luxembourg Retains Cause

> **CRITICAL**: Unlike France (which abolished cause in the 2016 reform), Luxembourg **retains the cause doctrine**.

This is fundamental for Luxembourg contract law.

---

## Definition

> **Cause** = the *pourquoi de l'opération juridique* — why the party contracted.

Not the motive, but the direct reason for undertaking the obligation.

---

## Two Requirements

### 1. Cause Must Exist (art. 1131)

> "L'obligation sans cause, ou sur une fausse cause... ne peut avoir aucun effet."

| Situation | Sanction |
|-----------|----------|
| **Absence of cause** | Nullité relative |
| **False cause** | Nullité relative |

### 2. Cause Must Be Lawful (art. 1133)

> "La cause est illicite, quand elle est prohibée par la loi, quand elle est contraire aux bonnes mœurs ou à l'ordre public."

| Situation | Sanction |
|-----------|----------|
| **Illicit cause** | Nullité absolue |

---

## Absence vs. Illicit Cause

| Aspect | Absence de cause | Cause illicite |
|--------|------------------|----------------|
| **Nature** | Formation defect | Content defect |
| **Sanction** | Nullité relative | Nullité absolue |
| **Prescription** | 5 years | 30 years |
| **Who invokes** | Protected party | Any party |

---

## Common Illicit Motive

> **Poelmans**: No need for **common** illicit motive. If one party knows of other's illicit purpose, cause is illicit.

**Example**: Loan granted knowing borrower will use funds for illegal purpose.

---

## Cause in Different Contracts

### Synallagmatic Contracts

Cause of each party's obligation = other party's obligation.

- In sale: buyer's cause = obtaining thing; seller's cause = receiving price

### Unilateral Contracts

Cause = antecedent relationship or gratuitous intent.

### Gratuitous Contracts

Cause = *intention libérale* (intention to benefit).

---

## Banking Application — Corporate Benefit

### The Problem

When company guarantees another's debt (especially parent's), what is the **cause**?

### Corporate Benefit Doctrine

For cause to exist, guarantor company must receive **real benefit**:
- Direct benefit (fee, access to group financing)
- Indirect benefit (group stability, continued support)

### Upstream Guarantees

Subsidiary guaranteeing parent's debt:
- **Challenge**: What does subsidiary receive?
- **Analysis**: Must demonstrate corporate benefit
- **Risk**: Nullity if purely gratuitous to detriment of company

### Key Considerations

| Factor | Analysis |
|--------|----------|
| **Group membership** | Generally recognized benefit |
| **Financial interdependence** | Supports cause |
| **Proportionality** | Guarantee amount vs. company assets |
| **Directors' duties** | Must act in company's interest |

---

## Proof

| Element | Presumption |
|---------|-------------|
| **Existence of cause** | Presumed from contract |
| **Illicit cause** | Must be proven by challenger |

---

## Distinction from Objet

| Aspect | Objet | Cause |
|--------|-------|-------|
| **Question** | What is promised? | Why is it promised? |
| **Focus** | The prestation | The purpose |
| **Sanction** | Nullité absolue (illicit objet) | Depends on issue |
