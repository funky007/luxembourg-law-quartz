# Clause résolutoire

> **Type**: Contractual resolution mechanism  
> **Effect**: Resolution upon specified breach  
> **Key Rule**: Strictly interpreted; mise en demeure usually required

---

## Definition

Contractual clause providing for **automatic resolution** upon specified breach, without need for judicial intervention.

Also called: *pacte commissoire exprès*

---

## Operation (Poelmans §218)

### Principle

Clause allows resolution **de plein droit** (automatically) when:
- Specified breach occurs
- Procedural requirements met (typically mise en demeure)

### Strict Interpretation

> Clause résolutoire is **exception** to judicial resolution → interpreted **restrictively**.

Party invoking must **scrupulously comply** with clause conditions.

---

## Mise en Demeure Requirement

### General Rule

Even with clause résolutoire, **mise en demeure still required** unless clause expressly dispenses.

### Purpose of Mise en Demeure

Two possible purposes (per Poelmans):
1. **Traditional**: Give debtor last chance to perform
2. **Alternative**: Simply notify debtor that clause is being invoked

### Clause Must Be Clear

If clause says "sommation de payer" → must demand payment first.
If clause says "notification of resolution" → different effect.

---

## Key Case — Bank Loan (Poelmans)

**Facts**: Bank's clause required mise en demeure before resolution. Bank sent demand for full balance without first demanding arrears.

**Held**: 
- No valid resolution — clause not properly invoked
- Clause ambiguous → interpreted in favor of debtor (borrower)
- Bank retained right to seek judicial resolution under art. 1184

---

## Special Rule: Bail (Lease)

> **Art. 1762-2 C.civ.**: Clause résolutoire in lease "reste soumise à l'appréciation du juge compétent."

**Effect**: Even with clause, lease resolution **requires judicial intervention**.

**Case**: Landlord tried to resolve commercial lease by letter based on clause → invalid; must go to court.

---

## Relationship with Art. 1184

### Not Mutually Exclusive

> Existence of clause résolutoire does **not** deprive creditor of right to judicial resolution under art. 1184.

Unless creditor **expressly and unequivocally waived** judicial resolution.

### Strategic Choice

Creditor may choose:
- Invoke clause (if conditions met)
- Seek judicial resolution (if clause fails or prefer judicial process)

---

## Consumer Protection

> **Art. L.211-3.4 Code conso.**: Abusive if professional reserves right to terminate unilaterally without **specific valid reason** in contract.

Clause must specify grounds precisely.

---

## Waiver of Resolution Right

> **Poelmans §220**: Right to resolution is **not ordre public**.

Parties may contractually waive right to resolution entirely.

---

## Drafting Considerations

### Essential Elements

1. **Triggering events**: Specify which breaches activate clause
2. **Procedure**: Mise en demeure? Notice period?
3. **Automatic vs. optional**: Does clause self-execute or require invocation?
4. **Consequences**: What happens upon resolution?

### Banking Standard

Typical loan clause:
> "Upon occurrence of an Event of Default, the Lender may, by notice to the Borrower, declare all amounts immediately due and payable..."
