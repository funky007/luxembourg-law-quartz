# Droit de rétention (Right of Retention)

> **Type**: Quasi-security / Exclusivity-conferring guarantee
> **Source**: Westendorf, T.1, §§810-862
> **Key Articles**: Arts. 570, 863, 1612, 1653, 1673, 1948, 2082, 2087, 2280 C.civ.
> **Nature**: NOT a true sûreté - confers only right to block, not preference or pursuit

---

## Definition and Legal Nature (§810)

The creditor retains the debtor's property (*chose*) until payment of the obligation owed. The right approaches but does not constitute a true security (*sûreté*).

**Westendorf, T.1, §810**:
> The creditor has no direct right reinforcing his right to the value of the property. He has neither preference right (*droit de préférence*) nor right of pursuit (*droit de suite*). He has only a simple right to retain the property and block its return.

**Luxembourg jurisprudence**:
- **Cour d'appel, 21 juin 2006, n° 29.783**: "Like the exception of non-performance, the right of retention permits a creditor to refuse to return property to his debtor as long as the latter has not performed his obligations."
- **Trib. Luxembourg (référé), 26 septembre 2006, n° 618/2006**: "The right of retention may be defined as the right by virtue of which a person holding property belonging to another is entitled to defer restitution until payment of what is owed to him, in connection with that property, by its owner."

**Cass. fr. com., 20 mai 1997**: The right of retention is NOT a sûreté.

---

## Generalization of the Right (§811)

The 1804 legislator did not provide a general principle. Case law developed the theory from specific Code civil provisions, achieving legislative recognition in Belgium (Art. 73, Chapitre 3, Titre XVII, Livre III C.civ.) and France (Art. 2286 C.civ.).

**Code civil provisions recognizing retention**:
- Art. 570, 863, 1612, 1653, 1673, 1948, 2082, 2087, 2280 (direct references)
- Arts. 545, 555 (indirect)

**JP Diekirch, 5 mars 1990, n° 152/90**:
> "[In the absence of specific legal text] when certain conditions are met, the creditor enjoys a right of retention by analogy to cases for which the legislator expressly provides a right of retention."

---

## Conditions of Existence (§812)

Three cumulative requirements per **Cour d'appel (référé), 20 mars 2019, n° 42/19**:
1. **Existence of a claim** (*créance*) - certain, liquid, and exigible
2. **Detention of property** (*détention d'une chose*)
3. **Link of connexity** (*lien de connexité*) between claim and detention

---

## §1. The Secured Claim (*La créance garantie*)

### Object of the Claim (§813)

Generally secures a **monetary claim** (*créance de somme d'argent*), but may secure an obligation to do (*faire*) or not do (*ne pas faire*).

### Certainty of the Claim (§814)

**The claim must exist** - a merely doubtful claim cannot support retention.

**JP Diekirch, 5 mars 1990, n° 152/90**:
> "A claim, to be certain, must truly and surely exist at the moment the right of retention is invoked."

**However**: The claim may be contested - "principe certain de créance" suffices.

**Trib. Luxembourg, 2 mars 2011, n° 44/11**:
> "It matters not that the claim is not yet liquid or certain because contested in principle or amount. In this case, retention is at the risk and peril of the retainer who must assume, if necessary, the negative decision of the judge."

**Consequences of uncertain claim**: French case law sanctioned as *abus de confiance* (criminal breach of trust) a holder exercising retention on an uncertain claim.

### Exigibility of the Claim (§815)

**The claim must be due** (*exigible*) - retention is a means of constraint for immediate payment.

**Consequences**:
- Natural obligation cannot give rise to retention
- Suspensive term (*terme suspensif*) prevents retention
- Retention cannot be invoked by anticipation
- Grace period (*délai de grâce*) does NOT prevent retention

**Cour d'appel, 22 septembre 2016, n° 125/16**:
> "The right of retention can only apply if there exists an exigible claim... The simple risk that in the future the co-contractor might not pay his invoices is not a legitimate reason to refuse contract performance."

**In bankruptcy**: French case law holds exigibility is no longer required.

### Liquidity of the Claim (§816)

**Liquidity not strictly required** if creditor seeks judicial liquidation.

**Cour d'appel (référé), 20 mars 2019, n° 42/19**:
> "As for liquidity of the claim, it is considered not required due to the conservatory character of the right of retention."

**However**: Creditor preventing liquidation cannot invoke retention (**Trib. Diekirch, 22 juin 2021, n° 2021TADCH01/65**).

---

## §2. Detention (*La détention*)

### Effective Detention (§818)

**Simple precarious detention** (*détention précaire*) is necessary and sufficient, provided the retainer remains conscious of the obligation to return.

**Trib. Luxembourg, 25 mai 2018, n° 131/2018**:
> "True possession with *animus domini* is not necessary, simple precarious detention suffices. Thus, a mandatary, depositary, lessee may invoke the right of retention."

### Detention Through Third Party (§819)

Retention may be exercised through a third party holding for the creditor's account (*détention par entiercement*).

### Regular Detention (§820-821)

The retainer must have obtained detention in a **"normal and lawful"** manner.

**Excluded**:
- Detention obtained by fraud
- Detention by a retainer in bad faith (*mauvaise foi*)

**Bad faith assessed morally** (dishonest, suspect, disloyal conditions) - **Trib. Luxembourg, 30 avril 2014, n° 481/2014**.

**Technical bad faith** (knowledge of not being owner) does NOT prevent retention for improvements.

**Cass. belge, 27 avril 2006**:
> "The creditor is in good faith when he could believe at the time of receiving the goods that his debtor was the owner of the property, or at least was competent to conclude agreements likely to give rise to a right of retention."

### Object of Retention (§822-824)

**Originally**: Only corporeal movables (*biens corporels*).

**Modern evolution**: Extends to incorporeal assets (*biens incorporels*) where a blocking power exists.

**Specific objects recognized**:
- Financial assets (loi 5 août 2005, Art. 5(5))
- Vehicle registration cards (*carte grise*)
- Accounting documents
- Customs documents
- Keys of an apartment

**Value of object**: Objects of minimal value may be retained if causing significant inconvenience (*gêne*).

**NOT retainable**:
- Objects not in commerce (notarial minutes)
- Children
- Human body
- Counterfeit goods (**Cass. fr. com., 26 octobre 1999**)

### Immovable Property (§823)

**French position**: Retention opposable to debtor even without land registry formalities, but NOT opposable to:
- Hypothecary creditor
- Liquidator

### Accessory Character (§825)

The right of retention is **accessory to the claim** - it follows the claim. Transfer of claim requires transfer of detention.

---

## §3. The Link of Connexity (*Le lien de connexité*)

### Necessity (§826)

Case law requires connexity between claim and detention, unless retention results from a sûreté.

**Trib. Luxembourg, 2 février 2018, n° 2018TALCH02/00167**:
> "If the debt and the creditor's effective power over the property derive from two distinct contracts, the second cannot be the object of a right of retention exercised to guarantee the first."

### Material Connexity (§827)

The claim arose **from the detention of the property** without contractual relationship:
- Conservation expenses (*frais de conservation*)
- Improvement expenses (*frais d'amélioration*)
- Acquisition expenses

### Juridical Connexity (§828)

The claim need not arise from the property itself - sufficient that it arises from the **contract or quasi-contract** pursuant to which the property was delivered.

**Examples**:
- Mandatary (Art. 1948 C.civ.)
- Notary
- Accountant

**Luxembourg jurisprudence on accountants** - **Cour d'appel (référé), 27 janvier 2004, n° 28.061**:
> "Accountants have been recognized the right to retain, until fully paid their fees, not only accounting documents they prepared for their client, but all documents they hold, including those belonging to their client delivered in communication."

### Conventional Connexity (§829-831)

**Westendorf, T.1, §829** (citing L. Aynès):
> "There is conventional connexity when a debtor delivers property to his creditor to guarantee payment of his debt without giving that property as a pledge."

**Luxembourg recognition**: **Cour d'appel, 14 octobre 2015, n° 39.083**:
The court recognized conventional retention where the document sufficient under Art. 2074 C.civ. contained no mention exteriorizing intent to constitute a pledge.

**Multiple tribunal decisions** recognize conventional retention in service contracts:
- Trib. Luxembourg (référé), 23 novembre 2012, n° 801/2012
- Trib. Luxembourg (référé), 22 juillet 2013, n° 466/2013

**Conditions for conventional retention**:
1. Certainty of will to create such right
2. Delivery of the thing
3. Written agreement (*a priori*)

**Advantage**: Can cover objects of no value that cannot constitute pledge collateral.

### Insufficient Connexity (§832-833)

**Even with connexity, retention denied**:
- Syndic retaining condominium archives
- Dentist retaining dental prosthesis
- Objects not in commerce

**Lawyers**: May retain procedure documents and documents obtained through work - **Trib. Diekirch, 15 avril 1880**:
> "The lawyer has a right of retention on all procedure acts made by him and on all documents obtained through his work and at his expense, until payment of what is owed to him."

## Section 2. Regime (§835)

### §1. Effects

#### A. The Blocking Right (*Droit de blocage*)

**"Gel et gêne"** (freeze and inconvenience) - **Westendorf, T.1, §837**.

The retainer "adopts a passive position" - blocking is the essence of the right.

##### I. Indivisibility (§838)

**The right of retention is indivisible** - even partial payment does not require partial release.

**JP Luxembourg, 13 juillet 2022, Rép. fisc. 2071**:
> "The right of retention is in principle indivisible. Article 1948 of the Civil Code specifies that the depositary may block the property until full payment. Partial payment does not in principle permit release of the property."

##### Impossibility of Abuse (§839-841)

**Trib. Luxembourg (référé), 4 juillet 1994, n° 945/94**:
> "The right of retention is not susceptible of abuse; by its very essence it is exercised with the intention to harm the debtor, its effectiveness depends on the prejudice it causes."

**French Cass. fr. civ. I, 24 septembre 2009**: Courts have overturned lower decisions finding abusive retention.

**However**: Art. 6-1 C.civ. applies - **exceptionally** abusive if:
- Exercised with malevolence
- Without interest for retainer
- Exercised unjustifiably

**Consumer protection** (Art. L. 212-13 Code de la consommation): Repairer cannot retain if "characterized disproportion" between property value and amount owed.

##### II. Opposability (§844-849)

**Opposable to debtor** - even benefiting from provisional suspension of proceedings.

**Opposable to universal successors** of debtor.

**Opposable to other creditors** - **Cour d'appel, 14 octobre 2015, n° 39.083**:
> "The creditor holds the property in 'hostage', putting him in a position of strength when the property has value exceeding his claim."

**In bankruptcy**: Opposable to the estate (*masse*) and curator. The curator must satisfy the creditor to recover the property. No declaration of claim required (**Cass. belge, 16 janvier 2020**).

**Opposable to particular successors** - Art. 2279 C.civ. protects good faith acquirers, but case law generally recognizes opposability.

**Opposable to non-debtor owner**: Belgian and French case law recognize opposability to sub-acquirer, even in good faith, where material connexity exists.

#### B. Absence of Legal Prerogatives (§850-852)

**No right of pursuit** (*droit de suite*) - loss of property extinguishes retention.

**No right of preference** (*droit de préférence*) - in forced sale at creditor's initiative, retainer loses right and ranks according to general rules.

**No judicial attribution** (*attribution judiciaire*) - **Cass. fr. com., 9 juin 1998**: Retainer cannot request judicial attribution.

---

### §2. Extinction (§853-862)

#### A. Causes of Extinction

##### I. Accessory Extinction (§855-857)

**Extinction of claim** → extinction of retention.

**Consignation** of corresponding sum also extinguishes.

**Exception 1** - Art. 2082(2) C.civ.: Pledge retention covers subsequent debt between same parties.

**Exception 2** - Retention **interrupts prescription** of secured claim - **Cass. fr. com., 31 octobre 2006**:
> "Maintenance of the pledge in the hands of the creditor, in that it implies tacit permanent recognition of the creditor's right by the debtor, interrupts prescription."

##### II. Principal Extinction (§858-860)

**Disappearance of property** - NO real subrogation to indemnity (unless directly paid to retainer).

**Express renunciation** of right of retention.

**Voluntary release** (*dessaisissement volontaire*) - **Trib. Luxembourg, 30 juillet 1925, Pas., 11, p. 470**.

**Exception**: No extinction if release for expert examination - **Cass. fr. civ. III, 13 juillet 1994**.

#### B. Revival of Retention (§861)

**Successive deliveries**: Creditor releasing property loses retention. Right cannot revive for original claim if property delivered under new contract.

**Exception**: If delivery under **single framework contract** (*convention-cadre*), successive claims become articles of an account whose balance is secured by retention.

| Feature | Droit de rétention | Possessory Lien (Common Law) |
|---------|-------------------|------------------------------|
| Nature | Not a security interest | Not a security interest |

## Banking Practice

### Practical Applications

1. **Accountant retention** of client documents - widely recognized
2. **Lawyer retention** of case files (Art. 2.4.3.1 Règlement Intérieur)
3. **Depositary/warehouse** retention of goods
4. **Garage** retention of vehicles

### Due Diligence Checklist

1. Verify claim is certain (or principally certain), liquid, exigible
2. Confirm lawful detention
3. Establish connexity:
   - Material (conservation/improvement costs)?
   - Juridical (same contract)?
   - Conventional (express provision)?
4. Document express retention clause if relying on conventional connexity
5. Consider consumer protection limits (disproportion)

### Drafting Considerations

**Conventional retention clause example**:
> "The Service Provider shall have the right to retain all documents, files and materials delivered by or on behalf of the Client until full payment of all sums due hereunder, whether or not such documents relate to the unpaid invoices."

### Limitations

- No active realization right - must wait for payment or third-party initiative
- Lost on voluntary release (unless for limited purpose)
- Consumer protection restrictions
- Cannot cover unrelated claims absent framework agreement
