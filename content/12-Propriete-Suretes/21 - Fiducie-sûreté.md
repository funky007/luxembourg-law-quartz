# Fiducie-sûreté (Security Trust)

> **Type**: Property-based security / Ownership transfer as guarantee
> **Source**: Westendorf, T.1, §§1000-1059
> **Key Legislation**: Loi du 27 juillet 2003 sur le trust et les contrats fiduciaires
> **Nature**: TRUE sûreté - transfer of ownership into separate patrimony

---

## Introduction and Historical Background (§§1000-1003)

### Ancient Origins, Modern Revival (§1000)

**Westendorf, T.1, §1000**:
Transfer of ownership as security is both ancient (Roman law) and very recent. History showed this form of security was "too heavy in consequences" (*trop lourd de conséquences*), leading to its replacement by pledge (with dispossession but without transfer) or hypothec.

### Contemporary Origins (§1001)

The modern concept originated in **Germany** (second half of 19th century) where validity was recognized due to practical needs:
- Dispossession requirement in traditional movable securities hindered credit development
- Persons seeking funds needed to use as collateral the very assets needed for production
- Doctrine turned to Roman *fiducia*, rediscovered and adapted by practice

### Development in Luxembourg (§1003)

**Westendorf, T.1, §1003**:
In Luxembourg, the appearance of these securities is due to:
1. Practice of transfer of ownership as security established on **international financial markets**
2. Since **1983**, Luxembourg has had fiduciary regulation (*Loi sur la fiducie*) initially for trust-like management purposes
3. Nothing prevented use as *fiducie-sûreté* — practice has adopted it

## Section 1. Transfer of Ownership WITHOUT Fiduciary Patrimony (§§1010-1016)

### Recognition of Certain Applications (§1011)

**Westendorf, T.1, §1011**:
In French-inspired legal systems, certain fiducie-sûretés were recognized by case law, primarily those involving **sums of money** (*gage-espèces*).

In the context of cash collateral, admission of transfer of ownership as security is not in doubt.

### Hostile Luxembourg Jurisprudence (§1014)

**Westendorf, T.1, §1014**:
> "Contrary to the wishes of doctrine, Luxembourg jurisprudence, which has apparently pronounced only twice on the question, has not favorably received the fiducie-sûreté outside the statutory texts."

**Cour d'appel, 22 mars 2000, n° 21.710 du rôle**:
A cession of claim granted as security constitutes in reality a *nantissement* and not a true cession, since it does not transfer ownership of the claim.

**Trib. Luxembourg, 7 mars 2006, nos 73.447, 88.338, 93.059 et 94.285 du rôle**:
The court qualified as pledge (*gage*) a purported "transfer of ownership as security" of a suitcase containing securities, since it fell neither within the Loi du 1er août 2001 nor the règlement grand-ducal du 19 juillet 1983.

### Arguments for Generalized Admission (§1015)

**Three arguments** (citing P. Crocq):

1. **No numerus clausus of translative contracts** — the transfer of ownership as security has a cause (the security purpose) despite absence of price, and does not create a prohibited inalienability

2. **Numerus clausus of real rights** only opposes the creation of **new perpetual dismemberments** of ownership, not a new temporary use of undivided ownership

3. **Numerus clausus of real guarantees** requires that the secured property be **in the grantor's patrimony** — this principle cannot be invoked for proprietary security where the property is in the **creditor's** patrimony

**Westendorf, T.1, §1016**:
> "Despite these very convincing arguments, only the legislator could truly impose general recognition of the fiducie-sûreté."

## Section 2. Fiducie-sûreté under the Loi du 27 juillet 2003 (§§1017-1059)

### Legislative Evolution (§1017)

| Text | Date | Contribution |
|------|------|--------------|
| Arrêté grand-ducal du 22 décembre 1972 | 1972 | Fiduciary representation (limited scope) |
| Règlement grand-ducal du 19 juillet 1983 | 1983 | First true fiduciary contract regime |
| **Loi du 27 juillet 2003** | 2003 | Current regime — expressly recognizes fiducie-sûreté |

**Loi du 27 juillet 2003**:
Expressly recognizes "fiducie conclue à des fins de garantie" and regulates certain aspects when fiducie is used as security.

## §1. Formation (§§1019-1033)

### A. Bankruptcy Rules (§1019)

**Suspect period nullities** (Art. 445, al. 4 C. com.):
A fiducie-sûreté constituted during the suspect period (or 10 days prior) **in guarantee of a pre-existing debt** is null.

**Exception**: Fiducie-sûreté constituted before this period is unassailable except for proven fraud (Art. 448 C. com.).

**Dessaisissement** (Art. 444 C. com.):
From the declaratory judgment of bankruptcy, the debtor cannot constitute further fiducie-sûretés.

### B. Scope of Application (§1020)

**Art. 1 Loi 2003 — Limited Personal Scope**:
The law applies only if the fiduciary is:
- Credit institution (*établissement de crédit*)
- Investment firm (*entreprise d'investissement*)
- SICAV or SICAF
- Securitization company (*société de titrisation*)
- Management company of FCP or securitization fund
- Pension fund (*fonds de pension*)
- Insurance or reinsurance undertaking
- National or international public body in the financial sector

**Consequence**: A fiduciary contract with other persons is NOT null — the law simply does not apply.

**Cour d'appel, 12 juillet 2017, n° 130/17, n° 42.068 du rôle**:
> "Neither the Loi du 31 mai 1999 nor the Loi du 27 juillet 2003 provide that contracts concluded with persons lacking the required authorization are void."

**Beneficiary ≠ Fiduciary**:
The fiduciary may hold the security for the account of a third-party beneficiary. The beneficiary need not be the fiduciary.

### C. Secured Claims (§1022)

**Art. 8(1) Loi 2003**:
> "Le contrat fiduciaire peut être conclu pour garantir des créances nées ou à naître."

*Translation*: The fiduciary contract may secure claims that have arisen or will arise.

### D. Object of Security (§1022)

**Any property in commerce** (*tout bien dans le commerce*) may form the collateral base, without other limitation.

The security may simultaneously cover assets of **very different natures** (e.g., shares, receivables, equipment).

In particular, fiducie could cover elements of a *fonds de commerce* or stocks — putting the creditor in a preferable position to a pledgee under the *gage sur fonds de commerce*.

### E. Conditions of Validity (§§1023-1024)

**General contract law** applies (*droit commun des contrats*).

**Trib. Luxembourg, 7 avril 2017, n° 80/2017**:
The fiduciary contract is a **synallagmatic contract**. By contrast to the Anglo-Saxon trust (unilateral declaration), the fiduciary contract proceeds from a contract.

### F. No Dispossession Required (§§1025-1026)

**Westendorf, T.1, §1025**:
> "As for the transfer of ownership resulting from a fiduciary contract, it does not necessarily give rise to dispossession of the transferred property."

The 2003 law **does not impose dispossession** and does not refer to pledge rules.

**However** (§1026):
Dispossession protects the beneficiary against actions of the fiduciant who might transfer goods to a good-faith third party protected by **Art. 2279 C.civ.** In case of misappropriation, the fiduciant is guilty of *abus de confiance*, but these remedies may not replace the property.

### G. Evolution of Collateral (§§1027-1028)

**Art. 8(1) Loi 2003**:
> "Les parties peuvent convenir que le patrimoine fiduciaire évoluera en fonction des engagements garantis ou d'autres facteurs de leur choix."

*Translation*: Parties may agree that the fiduciary patrimony will evolve according to the secured obligations or other factors of their choice.

**Westendorf, T.1, §1027**:
This is a particularly interesting and derogatory text, allowing not only substitution of assets but also **modification of collateral value** according to the parties' needs.

**Consequence for suspect period** (§1028):
Additional transfers made pursuant to a contractual evolution clause do NOT constitute new securities during the suspect period. *A contrario*, transfers not provided for by the contract fall under suspect period nullities.

### H. Proof (§1029)

**Art. 9(1) Loi 2003**:
> "La preuve du contrat fiduciaire doit être rapportée par écrit."

*Translation*: Proof of the fiduciary contract must be established in writing.

**Trib. Luxembourg, 7 avril 2017, n° 80/2017**:
The writing is required *ad probationem* and not *ad validitatem* — the contract is valid by the mere meeting of wills.

### I. Opposability to Third Parties (§§1030-1033)

**Art. 9(2) Loi 2003**:
The fiduciary contract is **opposable to third parties upon conclusion**.

**Westendorf, T.1, §1031**:
This principle applies regardless of the good or bad faith of third parties — their knowledge of the contract does not matter.

**Exceptions** (§1032):

1. **Rules of form and opposability** applicable by reason of the nature of transferred assets (e.g., immovables, S.àr.l. shares under Art. 710-12 LSC)

2. **Limitations on fiduciary's powers** (Art. 7(3)) — opposable to third parties only if the third party had knowledge

**Opposability to assigned debtor** (§1033):
For transferred receivables, Art. 9(3) provides immediate opposability, but:
> "Le débiteur se libère valablement entre les mains du fiduciant tant qu'il n'a pas connaissance du transfert."

*Translation*: The debtor validly discharges between the hands of the fiduciant until knowledge of the transfer.

## §2. Effects (§§1035-1059)

### A. Common Effects to All Fiducies

#### I. Real Effects: Transfer into Separate Patrimony (§§1037-1042)

**Art. 5 Loi 2003**:
The fiduciary becomes **owner** of the property forming the **fiduciary patrimony** (*patrimoine fiduciaire*).

**Trib. Luxembourg, 1er juillet 2020, n° 2020TALCH15/00895**:
> "The fiduciary is thus made owner of property forming a fiduciary patrimony. The transfer of this fiduciary property takes place in a patrimony of affectation distinct from the personal patrimony of the fiduciary and from any other patrimony."

**Nature of ownership** (§1037):
This is **ordinary ownership** (*propriété de droit commun*) — no dismemberment occurs. However:
- The property is **finalized** (must serve beneficiary's interests)
- The property is **temporary** (fiduciary must transmit to beneficiary at end)

**Separate patrimony** (§1040):

**Art. 6 Loi 2003**:
> "Le patrimoine fiduciaire est distinct du patrimoine personnel du fiduciaire, comme de tout autre patrimoine fiduciaire."

*Translation*: The fiduciary patrimony is distinct from the personal patrimony of the fiduciary, as from any other fiduciary patrimony.

**Consequence — "Impregnable citadel"** (§1041):
- Creditors of the fiduciary **cannot seize** assets in the fiduciary patrimony
- Creditors of the fiduciant **cannot seize** assets in the fiduciary patrimony
- Only creditors whose rights arose **from** the fiduciary patrimony may seize

**Trib. Luxembourg, 1er avril 2015, n° 162.933 du rôle**:
> "The fiduciary patrimony defined as a distinct mass of property, burdened with its own liabilities: only creditors whose rights arose from the conservation or management of these assets could seize them."

**End of fiducie — real effects** (§1042):
At the end of the contract, the fiduciary patrimony disappears and ownership of the property is retransferred to the fiduciant or transferred to the third-party beneficiary, **automatically** in principle.

**Trib. Luxembourg, 4 mai 2018, n° 2018TALCH02/00778**:
> "The specificity of the fiducie, which lies in the non-definitive character of the transfer of ownership, implies by definition that ownership of the concerned property returns, in time, to the fiduciant or his successors. The return of ownership into the constituant's patrimony operates by operation of law."

#### II. Personal Effects (§§1043-1052)

**Art. 7(1) Loi 2003**:
> "Les règles du mandat, à l'exclusion de celles reposant sur la représentation, sont applicables aux relations entre le fiduciant et le fiduciaire."

*Translation*: The rules of mandate, excluding those based on representation, apply to relations between fiduciant and fiduciary.

**Conservation obligation** (§1043):
The obligation to transfer assets at the end of the fiducie necessarily implies a conservation obligation (*obligation de conservation*).

**Nuances**:

| Issue | Default Rule | Exception |
|-------|--------------|-----------|
| Representation (§1045) | Not applicable | May be contractually conferred |
| Instructions (§1046) | Fiduciant may instruct | Art. 7(4): fiduciant may renounce |
| Unilateral termination (§1047) | Fixed-term: **NOT** resiliable | Parties may agree otherwise |
| Death/incapacity (§1047A) | Art. 2003 C.civ. may apply | Parties may derogate |

**Art. 7(5) Loi 2003**:
> "Sauf convention contraire, ni le fiduciant, ni le fiduciaire ne peuvent mettre fin unilatéralement au contrat fiduciaire conclu pour une durée déterminée."

**Replacement of fiduciary**:

**Art. 7(6) Loi 2003**:
The fiduciant, fiduciary, or third-party beneficiary may request in court, for **grave reasons** (*motifs graves*):
- Provisional or definitive replacement of the fiduciary, OR
- Anticipated extinction of the fiduciary contract

**Examples of grave reasons** (Doc. parl. 4721/00):
- Confusion of fiduciary and personal patrimony
- Dissipation of entrusted assets
- Seriously reprehensible conduct regarding financial legislation
- Grave disagreement with meddling fiduciant

### B. Particular Effects of Fiducie-sûreté (§§1053-1059)

#### I. Effects upon Default (§§1054-1058)

**Mise en demeure** (§1054):
A demand to the debtor to pay appears indispensable before realization.

**No prohibition of pacte commissoire** (§1054):
Since this is not a pledge, pledge rules (including prohibition of *pacte commissoire*) do not apply.

**Definitive transfer** (§1054):
If the creditor is also fiduciary, it is understood that the fiduciary obtains free disposition of the collateral assets. This implies transfer from the fiduciary patrimony to the fiduciary's personal patrimony.

**No enrichment — mandatory soulte** (§1055):

**Art. 8(2) Loi 2003**:
> "Est nulle toute stipulation ayant pour objet ou pour effet de dispenser le fiduciaire de verser au fiduciant ou au tiers bénéficiaire le solde net résultant de la différence entre la valeur, au jour de la réalisation, des biens constituant la garantie et le montant des créances garanties."

*Translation*: Any stipulation having as its object or effect to dispense the fiduciary from paying the net balance (difference between collateral value at realization and secured claims amount) is **null**.

**Westendorf, T.1, §1055**:
> "[A] sûreté ne peut jamais avoir pour finalité l'enrichissement de son bénéficiaire."

*Translation*: A security can never have as its purpose the enrichment of its beneficiary.

**Evaluation** (§§1056-1058):
- Evaluation must be made at the **day of realization**
- Parties should specify evaluation methods in the contract
- For listed assets: specify opening/closing price or average
- For unlisted assets: specify detailed valuation methodology

**Realization methods** (§1056):
- Assets remain with creditor/beneficiary (attribution)
- Sale by creditor
- Attribution with expert valuation

#### II. Accessory Character (§1059)

**Westendorf, T.1, §1059**:
The fiducie-sûreté is a **structurally accessory** security (*sûreté structurellement accessoire*):

**Consequences**:
1. **Extinction of secured claim** → disappearance of fiducie-sûreté
   - Exception: parties may provide that fiducie continues as fiducie-gestion

2. **Transfer of secured claim** → transfer of fiducie-sûreté
   - If creditor is also fiduciary: transfer of ownership to new creditor becomes necessary
   - OR: fiduciary remains owner but acts for benefit of new creditor

## Banking Practice

### Advantages

- **Strongest form of security** — creditor is (via fiduciary) owner
- **Insolvency-remote** — assets not in debtor's or creditor's estate
- **Flexible collateral** — any assets in commerce
- **No dispossession** — debtor may retain use of assets
- **Evolution clause** — collateral may fluctuate with obligations
- **Immediate opposability** — effective upon conclusion
- **No prohibition of pacte commissoire** — streamlined realization

### Risks

- **Limited circle of fiduciaries** — must be regulated financial institution
- **Art. 2279 risk** — if no dispossession, good faith acquirer protected
- **Complexity** — separate accounting for fiduciary patrimony required
- **Cost** — justified only for significant operations
- **No registration** — third parties may lack notice

### Due Diligence Checklist

1. Verify fiduciary has required status (credit institution, etc.)
2. Ensure contract clearly designates fiducie as security (*à des fins de garantie*)
3. Define collateral precisely (or provide evolution clause)
4. Specify evaluation methodology for realization
5. Consider dispossession for tangible movables (Art. 2279 protection)
6. Document separate fiduciary patrimony
7. Verify no suspect period issues if grantor financially distressed

### Drafting Considerations

**Security purpose clause**:
> "Le présent contrat fiduciaire est conclu à des fins de garantie au sens de l'article 8 de la loi du 27 juillet 2003 relative au trust et aux contrats fiduciaires."

**Evolution clause**:
> "Le patrimoine fiduciaire évoluera en fonction du montant des créances garanties. En cas d'augmentation de la créance garantie ou de diminution de la valeur des biens constituant le patrimoine fiduciaire, le fiduciant s'engage à transférer des biens supplémentaires."

**Realization clause**:
> "En cas de défaut d'exécution de l'obligation garantie à l'échéance, le fiduciaire pourra, à son choix: (a) conserver les biens du patrimoine fiduciaire en pleine propriété, sous réserve du versement de la soulte prévue à l'article 8(2) de la loi; ou (b) procéder à la vente des biens dans les conditions les plus favorables."

**Evaluation methodology**:
> "La valeur des biens sera déterminée au jour de la réalisation par référence à [méthode de valorisation]. En cas de désaccord, un expert indépendant sera désigné d'un commun accord ou, à défaut, par le président du Tribunal d'arrondissement."
