# Appropriation (Pacte commissoire)

> **Type**: Enforcement mechanism / Self-help remedy
> **Source**: Westendorf, T.1, §230-235, §536-539
> **Key Articles**: Art. 2078 C.civ. (prohibition); Art. 11(1)(a) loi 5 août 2005 (authorization)
> **Key Cases**: CA, 22/01/2020, no CAL-2017-00004
> **Related**: [[11 - Gage civil (droit commun)]], [[13 - Contrat de garantie financière]], [[Financial collateral (loi 5 août 2005)]]

---

## Definition

The *pacte commissoire* (forfeiture clause) is an agreement whereby the creditor may appropriate the pledged asset in satisfaction of the debt upon default, without judicial intervention. In modern usage, *appropriation* refers to the pledgee becoming owner of the collateral through self-help enforcement.

---

## The Traditional Prohibition

### Civil Code Rule

**Art. 2078 C.civ.**:
> "Le créancier ne peut, à défaut de paiement, disposer du gage, sauf à lui à faire ordonner en justice que ce gage lui demeurera en paiement et jusqu'à due concurrence, d'après une estimation faite par experts, ou qu'il sera vendu aux enchères."

*Translation*: "The creditor may not, in default of payment, dispose of the pledge, except to have it judicially ordered that the pledge remain his in payment up to due concurrence, according to an expert valuation, or that it be sold at auction."

**Westendorf, T.1, §230**:
> "Toute clause qui autoriserait le créancier à s'approprier le gage est nulle."

### Rationale for Prohibition

1. **Debtor protection**: Prevents creditor from acquiring valuable asset for minor debt
2. **Other creditors**: Preserves orderly distribution of debtor's assets
3. **Market integrity**: Ensures fair valuation through judicial process

---

## Financial Collateral Exception

### Authorization Under Loi 5 août 2005

**Art. 11(1)(a) loi 5 août 2005**:

The creditor may "s'approprier les avoirs nantis ou les faire approprier par un tiers qu'il désigne à cet effet" — appropriate the pledged assets or have them appropriated by a designated third party.

**Westendorf, T.1, §536-539**:
> The law creates an exception to Art. 2078 for financial collateral, enabling self-help appropriation without court authorization.

### Conditions

For appropriation under the CGF regime:

1. **Triggering event** — Default or contractually agreed event
2. **No prior formalities** — No mise en demeure required
3. **Valuation** — Must account for any surplus value
4. **Good faith** — Cannot appropriate abusively

### Surplus Value

**Westendorf, T.1, §538**:
If the collateral value exceeds the secured debt, the pledgee must account to the grantor for the surplus (*soulte*). A security cannot be a source of enrichment beyond securing payment.

---

## Appropriation by Third Party

**Art. 11(1)(a) loi 5 août 2005** (as amended by loi 20 mai 2011):

**Westendorf, T.1, §539**:
> Appropriation may be made not only by the pledgee but by a third party designated by the pledgee — for example, a special purpose vehicle set up to acquire the assets.

This enables structured enforcement solutions in complex transactions.

---

## Distinction: Appropriation vs. Judicial Attribution

| Appropriation (Art. 11(1)(a)) | Judicial Attribution (Art. 11(1)(c)) |
|------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Self-help remedy | Court-ordered |
| No prior authorization | Requires court order |
| Only under CGF regime | Available under civil code too |
| Faster, private | Public, judicial |

---

## Comparative Note

**French law reform (2006)**: The *ordonnance* of 23 March 2006 reformed French security law, authorizing *pacte commissoire* for pledge (new Art. 2348 C.civ. fr.). Luxembourg law had already achieved this for financial collateral via the 2005 law.

**English law**: Appropriation is permitted under the Financial Collateral Arrangements Regulations 2003, implementing Directive 2002/47/CE.
