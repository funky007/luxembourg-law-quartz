# Right to Follow (Droit de suite)

> **Type**: Characteristic of real rights / Real securities
> **Source**: Westendorf, T.1, §31, §210
> **Key Articles**: Arts. 2073, 2076, 2279 C.civ.
> **Related**: [[Droit préférentiel]], [[Dépossession]]

---

## Definition

The *droit de suite* (right to follow) is the entitlement of a holder of a real right to pursue the asset into whosever hands it passes. For security interests, it means the security follows the asset even if ownership changes.

**General principle**: A real right (*droit réel*) is enforceable *erga omnes* and follows the thing itself, not merely the person of the original owner.

---

## The Pledge's Limited Droit de Suite

### The Problem

Unlike mortgage (*hypothèque*) which confers a complete *droit de suite* over immovables, the pledge over movables has a **limited or incomplete** droit de suite.

**Westendorf, T.1, §31**:
> "Le gage, 'affecte de façon substantielle les qualités que l'on peut attendre [d']un bien' et, contrairement à l'hypothèque, ne confère pas au créancier de droit de suite complet."

*Translation*: "The pledge 'substantially affects the qualities one can expect from an asset' and, unlike the mortgage, does not confer on the creditor a complete right to follow."

### Why Limited?

For movables, Art. 2279 C.civ. provides that "en fait de meubles, la possession vaut titre" (possession is equivalent to title). A third party who acquires possession of a movable in good faith obtains good title, defeating the pledgee's rights.

**Consequence**: The pledgee's real right can be defeated by a good faith acquirer — hence the crucial importance of **dispossession** to protect the creditor.

---

## Role of Dispossession

Since the pledge lacks a complete *droit de suite*, **dispossession** serves as a substitute protection mechanism.

**Westendorf, T.1, §31**:
> "En l'absence de [droit de suite complet], il ne peut pas y avoir de protection efficace du créancier à l'encontre des agissements du débiteur et des tiers s'il n'existe pas de publicité, sauf à remplacer cette publicité par une dépossession au profit du créancier."

*Translation*: "In the absence of [a complete right to follow], there can be no effective protection of the creditor against the actions of the debtor and third parties if there is no publicity, unless this publicity is replaced by dispossession in favor of the creditor."

### The Trade-off

| With Dispossession | Without Dispossession |
|--------------------|----------------------|
| Creditor holds asset | Debtor retains asset |
| Third parties cannot acquire in good faith | Third parties may acquire good title |
| Privilege protected | Privilege lost (pledge "inoperative") |

---

## Comparison: Pledge vs. Mortgage

| Feature | Gage (Pledge) | Hypothèque (Mortgage) |
|---------|---------------|----------------------|
| Subject matter | Movables | Immovables |
| Droit de suite | Limited/incomplete | Complete |
| Publicity mechanism | Dispossession | Registration (publicité foncière) |
| Protection against third parties | Via possession | Via registration |

**Why the difference?**
- Immovable property has a registration system providing publicity
- Movable property traditionally has no general registration (Luxembourg still lacks one)
- Possession is the only visible sign of rights over movables

---

## Exception: Financial Collateral

The Loi du 5 août 2005 sur les contrats de garantie financière modifies this analysis for financial collateral:

1. **Fictive dispossession** permitted (e.g., control over account)
2. **Enhanced protection** against third parties
3. **Special enforcement** rules apply

However, even here, some form of "control" (*maîtrise*) is required as a substitute for physical dispossession.

## Luxembourg Jurisprudence

**Cour d'appel, 8 février 2006, n° 30.012 du rôle, BIJ 7/2006, p. 174**:
> "En achetant sans le savoir un véhicule gagé, l'acquéreur se méprend de façon substantielle sur l'étendue de son droit de jouissance."

*Translation*: "By unknowingly buying a pledged vehicle, the purchaser substantially misunderstands the extent of his right of enjoyment."

**Context**: This case involved a French pledge without dispossession (*gage sans dépossession*) on a vehicle later registered in Luxembourg. The buyer could invoke error (*erreur*) because:
- Effective dispossession would have made the error inexcusable
- Without dispossession, the buyer could not know of the encumbrance

## Implications for Security Structuring

### For Pledgees

1. **Maintain possession**: Loss of possession may defeat the security
2. **Third-party custody**: Use *tiers convenu* (agreed third party) if direct possession impractical
3. **Financial collateral**: Consider the CGF regime for financial assets

### For Third Parties

1. **Possessor presumed owner**: Art. 2279 protects good faith acquirers
2. **Due diligence**: No general register to check for movable security in Luxembourg
3. **Exception**: Financial instruments may be subject to CGF rights

---

> **Common Law Comparison**
>
> English law distinguishes:
> - **Legal interests**: Generally enforceable against the world
> - **Equitable interests**: May be defeated by a bona fide purchaser for value without notice
>
> The **nemo dat quod non habet** rule (no one can give what they do not have) is subject to exceptions protecting good faith purchasers, similar to Art. 2279 C.civ.
>
> Under UCC Article 9, the secured party's right to follow collateral depends on proper **perfection** (usually by filing). A buyer in ordinary course of business takes free of security interests even if perfected.

## Banking Application

### Security Over Movables

When taking security over movable assets:

1. **Prefer dispossession**: Provides best protection
2. **If dispossession impractical**: Consider:
   - Retention of title (*réserve de propriété*) — ownership-based security
   - Financial collateral arrangements — for eligible assets
   - Fiduciary security (*fiducie-sûreté*) — ownership transfer
3. **Monitor the asset**: Ensure no unauthorized disposition

### Enforcement Considerations

The limited *droit de suite* means:
- Act quickly if debtor threatens to dispose of asset
- Consider contractual restrictions on disposition
- In financial collateral: special enforcement rights may apply
