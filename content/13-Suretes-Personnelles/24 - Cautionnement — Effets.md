# Cautionnement — Effets (Effects)

> **Type**: Personal Security (*sûreté personnelle*)
> **Source**: Westendorf, T.3, §§700-899
> **Key Articles**: C.civ. arts. 2011, 2016, 2021-2032

---

## Overview

The effects of suretyship (*cautionnement*) operate within a triangular relationship (*relation triangulaire*) between the surety (*caution*), creditor (*créancier*), and principal debtor (*débiteur principal*). The effects differ depending on which relationship is considered (Westendorf, T.3, §700).

---

## Effects Between Surety and Creditor

### Creditor's Obligations

#### 1. Good Faith (*Bonne foi*)

The creditor must act in good faith throughout the relationship. Even though suretyship is a unilateral contract benefiting the creditor, the creditor cannot abuse their position (Westendorf, T.3, §702).

#### 2. Annual Information Duty (Art. 2016 al. 2)

Since the 2013 Over-indebtedness Law, when a suretyship is granted by a natural person, the creditor must inform the surety annually of the evolution of the guaranteed debt and its accessories (Westendorf, T.3, §§704-710).

> **Art. 2016 al. 2 C.civ.**: "Lorsque le cautionnement est contracté par une personne physique, celle-ci est informée par le créancier de l'évolution du montant de la créance garantie et de ses accessoires au moins annuellement..."
>
> *Translation*: "When the suretyship is contracted by a natural person, they shall be informed by the creditor of the evolution of the guaranteed debt and its accessories at least annually..."

**Key points**:
- Applies to indefinite guarantees of determined/determinable debts
- Information must be provided at the agreed date or contract anniversary
- **Sanction**: Forfeiture (*déchéance*) of all accessories, fees, and penalties
- Burden of proof on creditor to demonstrate compliance
- Applies to all creditors (professional or private)
- Director-sureties benefit from this protection

**Temporal application**: The Court of Appeal holds that Art. 2016 al. 2 does not apply to guarantees signed before its entry into force (February 2014), though this position is debated (Westendorf, T.3, §705).

### Creditor's Rights

#### Right to Pursue the Surety

Upon the principal debtor's default, the creditor may pursue the surety for payment. However, the surety has defenses that may delay or limit this right.

### Surety's Defenses

#### Bénéfice de Discussion (Art. 2021-2024)

The surety may require the creditor to first pursue the principal debtor's assets before seeking payment from the surety. Requirements:
- Must be raised at first demand (*in limine litis*)
- Surety must identify seizable debtor assets
- Surety must advance execution costs

> **Banking Practice**: Systematically waived in commercial guarantees and whenever the surety is bound jointly and severally (*cautionnement solidaire*).

#### Bénéfice de Division (Art. 2025-2026)

When multiple sureties guarantee the same debt, each may require the creditor to divide their claim proportionately among solvent co-sureties.

> **Banking Practice**: Routinely waived through solidarity clauses.

#### Opposability of Exceptions

The surety may generally raise against the creditor all defenses that the principal debtor could raise, including:
- Nullity of the principal obligation
- Payment, set-off, or novation
- Prescription of the principal debt
- Force majeure affecting the debtor

**Exception**: Personal defenses of the debtor (e.g., incapacity) cannot be invoked by the surety.

## Effects Between Surety and Debtor

### Before Payment

The surety has limited rights before actually paying the creditor. However, in certain circumstances, the surety may act to protect their position.

#### Recourse Before Payment (Art. 2032)

The surety may proceed against the debtor even before payment if:
1. The surety is sued by the creditor
2. The debtor is bankrupt or insolvent
3. The debtor committed to relieve the surety by a certain date
4. The debt has become due
5. Ten years have elapsed (for indefinite-term obligations)

### After Payment

Upon paying the creditor, the surety acquires two distinct actions against the principal debtor.

#### 1. Personal Action (Art. 2028)

The surety has a direct personal action against the debtor for:
- Principal amount paid
- Interest from date of payment
- Costs incurred
- Damages suffered

This action exists whether the suretyship was given with or without the debtor's knowledge.

#### 2. Subrogation (Art. 1251)

The surety is legally subrogated to all rights of the creditor against the debtor, including:
- The claim itself
- All securities and privileges
- Priority ranking
- Any judgments obtained

> **Banking Implication**: A corporate guarantor who pays may be subrogated to the bank's mortgage over the debtor's assets.

#### Notice Requirement (Art. 2031)

The surety should notify the debtor before paying. If the surety pays without notice and the debtor subsequently pays the same debt (or had defenses), the surety loses recourse against the debtor but may recover from the creditor (*répétition de l'indu*).

## Effects Between Co-Sureties

### Contribution Rights

When multiple persons guarantee the same debt, a surety who pays more than their share may seek contribution from co-sureties (Westendorf, T.3, §§850-899).

The paying surety may:
1. Exercise a direct action based on the relationship between co-sureties
2. Exercise subrogated rights in the creditor's position

**Calculation**: Each co-surety's share is determined proportionately, excluding insolvent co-sureties.

---

## Transmission of the Guarantee

### Assignment of the Guaranteed Claim

When the creditor assigns the guaranteed claim, the suretyship follows as an accessory right. The surety may oppose to the assignee all defenses they could have raised against the original creditor.

### Death of the Surety

The surety's obligation transmits to their heirs (*héritiers*), who are bound in proportion to their inheritance shares. Heirs are not jointly and severally liable unless the original guarantee so provided.

---

> **Common Law Comparison**
>
> | Aspect | Luxembourg | Common Law |
> |--------|------------|------------|
> | Default position | Surety may require discussion first | Creditor may pursue surety directly |
> | Information duty | Statutory annual information (natural persons) | No general statutory duty |
> | Subrogation | Legal subrogation automatic | Equitable subrogation available |
> | Co-surety contribution | Right to proportionate contribution | Contribution in equity |
>
> **Key Difference**: Luxembourg's default rules provide more protection to sureties through discussion and division benefits; common law defaults favor creditors.

## Banking Application

### Facility Documentation

**Standard clauses** in bank guarantees typically include:
- Express waiver of *bénéfice de discussion* and *bénéfice de division*
- Joint and several liability (*solidarité*) with debtor
- Acknowledgment of receipt of annual information (or waiver where permitted)
- Authorization for creditor to grant extensions without surety consent

### Subordination

In structured finance transactions, guarantee subordination provisions typically require:
- Subordination of surety's subrogation claims to senior debt
- Turnover of any amounts recovered via subrogation
- Standstill on enforcement of contribution rights

### Insolvency Considerations

When the principal debtor enters insolvency:
- The guarantee becomes immediately enforceable
- The surety cannot benefit from any moratorium granted to the debtor
- Payment by the surety creates a claim in the insolvency (but subordinated to the creditor's remaining claim)
