# Cautionnement — Extinction

> **Type**: Personal Security (*sûreté personnelle*)
> **Source**: Westendorf, T.3, §§900-1100
> **Key Articles**: C.civ. arts. 2034-2039

---

## Overview

The surety's obligation may be extinguished through two principal routes:
1. **Accessory extinction** (*extinction par voie accessoire*): The guarantee terminates because the principal obligation terminates
2. **Principal extinction** (*extinction par voie principale*): The guarantee terminates independently of the principal obligation

This dual structure reflects the accessory yet autonomous nature of suretyship (Westendorf, T.3, §907).

---

## The Dual Obligation Structure

### Coverage Obligation (*Obligation de couverture*)

The obligation to guarantee payment of debts arising during a specified period. This obligation frames the temporal scope of the guarantee.

### Settlement Obligation (*Obligation de règlement*)

The obligation to pay, upon the debtor's default, debts that arose during the coverage period. This obligation persists even after coverage ends.

> **Key Principle**: After the guarantee's term expires, the surety remains liable for debts that:
> - Arose and became due during the coverage period, AND
> - Arose during the coverage period but became due afterward
>
> (Cour d'appel, 8 juin 2023, n° 98/23)

---

## Extinction by Accessory Route

The surety's obligation extinguishes when the principal obligation extinguishes, regardless of the cause.

### 1. Payment (*Paiement*)

Payment by the principal debtor extinguishes both the principal debt and the guarantee. Payment by the surety extinguishes the guarantee but creates recourse rights against the debtor.

### 2. Novation (Art. 2037 implied)

Novation of the principal obligation extinguishes the suretyship unless the creditor expressly reserves the guarantee for the new obligation. The surety must consent to guarantee the novated obligation.

### 3. Set-off (*Compensation*)

Set-off between creditor and principal debtor extinguishes the principal debt and consequently the guarantee.

### 4. Release of the Debtor (*Remise de dette*)

If the creditor releases the principal debtor, the guarantee is extinguished. However, release of the surety does not discharge the debtor.

### 5. Confusion (*Confusion*)

If the creditor and debtor become the same person (e.g., through inheritance), the obligation is extinguished and so is the guarantee.

### 6. Prescription of Principal Debt

Prescription of the principal debt extinguishes the guarantee. The surety may invoke prescription even if the debtor does not.

---

## Extinction by Principal Route

The guarantee may terminate independently of the principal obligation.

### 1. Direct Causes

#### Payment by Surety

Payment by the surety extinguishes the guarantee while preserving recourse rights against the debtor.

#### Prescription of the Guarantee

The guarantee itself may prescribe independently. However, given its accessory nature, this is rare in practice as the principal debt usually prescribes first.

#### Release of Surety (*Remise de cautionnement*)

The creditor may release the surety without releasing the debtor. This may occur as part of restructuring negotiations.

#### Term or Condition

If the guarantee is subject to a term (*terme*) or condition (*condition résolutoire*), its occurrence terminates the guarantee.

### 2. Unilateral Termination (*Résiliation unilatérale*)

#### Indefinite-Term Guarantees

For guarantees of indefinite duration (*cautionnement à durée indéterminée*), the surety may terminate unilaterally with notice.

**Effect**: Termination ends the coverage obligation prospectively. The surety remains liable for debts arising before termination (settlement obligation continues).

> **Banking Practice**: Guarantee forms typically specify notice periods and procedures for termination. Banks may require replacement security before releasing a terminating surety.

### 3. Creditor's Fault (Art. 2037)

A critical protection: the surety is discharged if the creditor's fault prevents subrogation into the creditor's rights and securities.

> **Art. 2037 C.civ.**: "La caution est déchargée, lorsque la subrogation aux droits, hypothèques et privilèges du créancier ne peut plus, par le fait de ce créancier, s'opérer en faveur de la caution."
>
> *Translation*: "The surety is discharged when subrogation into the creditor's rights, mortgages, and privileges can no longer, by the creditor's act, operate in favor of the surety."

#### Examples of Creditor Fault

- Releasing security without surety consent
- Allowing security registrations to lapse
- Granting subordination or priority waivers
- Releasing a co-surety
- Failing to perfect a security interest

#### Extent of Discharge

The discharge is proportionate to the prejudice suffered by the surety. If the lost security was worth 50% of the debt, the surety is discharged to that extent.

#### Waiver

Standard bank guarantees typically include waiver of Art. 2037 rights, permitting the bank to manage securities freely without affecting the guarantee's validity.

## Special Rules for Omnibus Guarantees

### Continuing Guarantees (*Cautionnement omnibus*)

For guarantees covering present and future debts (common in banking):
- Coverage obligation continues until termination
- Each new debt falls within the guarantee as it arises
- Termination stops new debts from being covered
- Pre-existing debts remain guaranteed

### Death of Surety

Upon the surety's death:
- Coverage obligation typically terminates for future debts
- Settlement obligation transmits to heirs for existing debts
- Heirs are liable in proportion to their inheritance shares

---

## Debtor's Insolvency

### Effect on Guarantee

The debtor's insolvency (*faillite*) does not extinguish the guarantee. Rather:
- The guarantee becomes immediately callable
- The surety cannot invoke moratoria or arrangements granted to the debtor
- Payment by the surety creates an insolvency claim

### Surety's Filing

If the surety pays, they may file a claim in the debtor's insolvency. However, this claim is typically subordinated to any remaining claim of the guaranteed creditor until the creditor is paid in full.

---

## Practical Considerations

### Evidence of Extinction

The surety bears the burden of proving the guarantee has been extinguished. Documentation requirements:
- Proof of debtor's payment
- Evidence of creditor's release
- Termination notices with delivery proof
- Evidence of creditor's fault under Art. 2037

### Continuing Enforceability

Even after guarantee termination:
- Accrued obligations remain enforceable
- Interest continues to accrue on unpaid amounts
- Enforcement proceedings may continue for pre-termination debts

---

> **Common Law Comparison**
>
> | Extinction Cause | Luxembourg | Common Law |
> |------------------|------------|------------|
> | Payment of principal | Extinguishes guarantee | Extinguishes guarantee |
> | Release of debtor | Extinguishes guarantee | May extinguish (varies by jurisdiction) |
> | Creditor prejudices surety | Proportionate discharge (Art. 2037) | Similar equitable defense |
> | Unilateral termination | Available for indefinite guarantees | Generally available with notice |
> | Material alteration | May discharge if prejudicial | Discharges surety (rule in *Holme v Brunskill*) |
>
> **Key Similarity**: Both systems recognize that creditor conduct prejudicing the surety may discharge the guarantee.

## Banking Application

### Documentation

**Termination clauses** should address:
- Notice period requirements (typically 30-90 days)
- Form of notice (written, registered mail)
- Effect on outstanding obligations
- Replacement security requirements
- Survival of settlement obligation

**Art. 2037 waivers** are standard but should be carefully drafted:
- Scope of permitted creditor actions
- Reservation of surety rights in extreme cases
- Disclosure requirements for material changes

### Restructuring Transactions

When restructuring a debtor's facilities:
- Obtain surety consent to material changes
- Consider whether novation terminates existing guarantees
- Document continuing guarantee obligations clearly
- Release old guarantees only upon receipt of replacement security

### Enforcement Considerations

Before enforcing against a surety:
- Verify guarantee remains in force
- Confirm no Art. 2037 discharge events
- Check annual information compliance (Art. 2016)
- Review termination notices received
- Assess proportionality defenses
