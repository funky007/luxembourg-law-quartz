## 1. Modes of Contract Termination

```
DISSOLUTION DES CONTRATS
        │
        ├─→ RÉSILIATION (consensual/unilateral)
        │       ├─ Mutuus dissensus
        │       └─ Unilateral (CDI, specific contracts)
        │
        ├─→ RÉSOLUTION (for breach)
        │       ├─ Judiciaire (art. 1184)
        │       ├─ Unilatérale (urgence)
        │       └─ Conventionnelle (clause résolutoire)
        │
        ├─→ NULLITÉ (formation defect)
        │       ├─ Absolue
        │       └─ Relative
        │
        └─→ CADUCITÉ (supervening impossibility)
```

## 2. Résiliation (Termination)

### 2.1 Definition

> **Poelmans §221**: Résiliation occurs when parties undo their contract, either unilaterally or by mutual agreement.

Contrast with *résolution* (termination for breach).

### 2.2 Mutuus Dissensus (art. 1134 al. 2)

**Principle**: Parties may revoke contract by mutual consent.

**Form**: No formalities required; may be tacit if certain.

**Effects**:
- Contract ends entirely
- Parties must restore what they received
- If contract not yet executed → simply ceases to exist

> **Exception for successive contracts**: Mutuus dissensus operates **ex nunc** only — cannot undo past performance.

### 2.3 Unilateral Résiliation

**Permitted for**:
- Contrats à durée indéterminée (CDI)
- Specific contracts (e.g., mandat — revocable at will)
- When clause permits

**Requirements**:
- Reasonable notice (*préavis*)
- Good faith
- No abuse of right

## 3. Nullité (Nullity)

**See full note**: [[Nullité]]

### 3.1 Comparative Table

| Aspect | Nullité Absolue | Nullité Relative |
|--------|-----------------|------------------|
| **Grounds** | Ordre public, illicit object/cause | Vices du consentement, incapacity |
| **Who invokes** | Any interested party | Protected party only |
| **Judge ex officio** | Yes | No |
| **Prescription** | 30 years | 5 years (1 year lésion) |
| **Exception de nullité** | Imprescriptible | Imprescriptible |
| **Confirmation** | Impossible | Possible |

### 3.2 Exception de Nullité

> **Poelmans §232**: The right to oppose nullity as a defense against enforcement action is imprescriptible — for both absolute and relative nullity.

### 3.3 Confirmation (arts. 1337 et s.)

**Definition**: Act by which protected party renounces right to invoke nullity.

**Requirements**:
- Can only be done **after** right arises
- Must indicate grounds of nullity
- Must show intention to repair defect

**Forms**:
- **Express**: Written acknowledgment
- **Tacit**: Voluntary execution or conduct showing clear intent

> **Case** (Poelmans §232): Buyers' letter demanding construction start within 15 days ≠ confirmation of defective sale contract; no intent to repair vice.

### 3.4 Effects of Nullity

**General effect**: Contract deemed never to have existed (*ab initio*).

**Consequences**:
- Restitution of prestations
- May be total or partial (if severable)
- Damages possible if one party at fault

---

## 4. Caducité (Lapse)

### 4.1 Definition

Contract becomes *caduc* when essential element disappears **after** valid formation.

### 4.2 Examples

- Object of contract destroyed
- Condition defective (third party refuses to determine price)
- Death terminates *intuitu personae* contracts
- Purpose achieved or impossible

### 4.3 Effect

Contract ceases to exist **ex nunc** (from moment of lapse).

## 5. Comparison Table

| Mode | Cause | Timing | Effect |
|------|-------|--------|--------|
| **Nullité** | Formation defect | Ab initio | Retroactive |
| **Résolution** | Non-performance | After formation | Retroactive |
| **Résiliation** | Agreement/right | After formation | Prospective |
| **Caducité** | Supervening event | After formation | Prospective |
