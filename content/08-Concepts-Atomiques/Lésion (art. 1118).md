# Lésion (art. 1118)

> **Type**: Vice du consentement  
> **Article**: 1118 Code civil (reformed 2011)  
> **Sanction**: Nullité relative (rescision or reduction)  
> **⚠️ Prescription**: **1 YEAR** from contract conclusion

---

## Definition

> **Lésion** = gross disproportion in prestations exploiting party's weakness.

Since 2011 reform, Luxembourg applies a **dual test** (objective + subjective).

---

## The Dual Test (art. 1118)

### 1. Objective Element

**Serious disproportion** (*disproportion grave*) between prestations at time of contract.

No fixed quantum — assessed by court considering:
- Nature of contract
- Market conditions
- Parties' circumstances

### 2. Subjective Element

The disproportion must result from **exploitation** of:
- **Faiblesse** (weakness)
- **Ignorance** (lack of knowledge)
- **Situation précaire** (precarious situation)

---

## Key Case (Poelmans §75)

**Facts**: Sale of company shares for €225,000 when company had **zero value** (heavily indebted).

**Held**: Contract annulled for lésion — manifest disproportion exploiting seller's situation.

---

## ⚠️ CRITICAL — Prescription

> **Art. 1118 al. 3**: Action must be brought within **1 YEAR** from contract **conclusion**.

| Vice | Prescription | Starting Point |
|------|--------------|----------------|
| Erreur | 5 years | Discovery |
| Dol | 5 years | Discovery |
| Violence | 5 years | Cessation |
| **Lésion** | **1 year** | **Conclusion** |

This is a major trap — much shorter than other vices.

---

## Remedies (art. 1118 al. 2)

Victim may choose:
1. **Rescision** — contract annulled
2. **Reduction** — obligations reduced to eliminate disproportion

---

## Specific Lésion Regimes

Article 1118 is the **general** lésion. Other specific regimes exist:

| Article | Application | Threshold |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| **1674** | Sale of immovables | < 5/12 of value |
| **887** | Partition (partage) | > 1/4 prejudice |
| **1305** | Minors | Any prejudice (no quantum) |
| **1855** | Pacte léonin (companies) | Excessive imbalance in profit/loss |

### Immovable Sales (art. 1674)

**Rescision pour lésion**: If seller received less than 5/12 of immovable's value.

**Buyer's option** (art. 1681): Can avoid rescission by paying supplement to just price.

**Method**: *Réévaluation proportionnelle* — assess disproportion at time of sale, calculate supplement at current value.

---

## Transaction and Lésion (Poelmans §76)

**General rule**: Transaction cannot be attacked for lésion (art. 2052).

**Exception**: If transaction relates to partition operations (*opérations mêmes du partage*), lésion applies.

### Key Case

**Facts**: Transaction presented as settlement showed extreme asymmetry — employee gave up significant claims for minimal concession.

**Held**: Transaction annulled as *léonine* due to abnormal financial asymmetry.

---

## Pacte Léonin (art. 1855)

**Definition**: Company clauses creating excessive imbalance in:
- Profit distribution
- Loss allocation

**Prohibited** if they destroy *affectio societatis*.

**Examples**:
- Fixed attribution of profits (= mere remuneration, not partnership)
- Portage agreements destroying partnership character

---

## Banking Application

### Loan Agreements

- Excessive interest may be challenged
- Consumer protection provides additional limits
- Usury laws apply

### Guarantees

- Disproportionate guarantee vs. guarantor's means
- Consider at time of formation
- Related to [[Cause (arts. 1131-1133)]] analysis

### Investment Products

- Manifestly unsuitable products
- Related to duty to inform
