# Nullité

> **Type**: Sanction of formation defects  
> **Two regimes**: Absolue vs. Relative  
> **Key principle**: Exception de nullité is imprescriptible

---

## Comparative Table

| Aspect | Nullité Absolue | Nullité Relative |
|--------|-----------------|------------------|
| **Protects** | General interest | Private interest |
| **Grounds** | Ordre public, illicit objet/cause | Vices du consentement, incapacity |
| **Who invokes** | Any interested party | Protected party only |
| **Ex officio** | Yes, by judge | No |
| **Prescription** | 30 years | 5 years (1 year for lésion) |
| **Exception de nullité** | Imprescriptible | Imprescriptible |
| **Confirmation** | Impossible | Possible |

---

## Nullité Absolue

### Grounds

- **Ordre public** violation (*sensu stricto*)
- **Illicit object** (art. 1128)
- **Illicit cause** (art. 1133)
- **Impossible object**
- **Object hors commerce**
- Absence of required **form** (solemnities)

### Characteristics

| Feature | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Who may invoke** | Any interested party |
| **Judge ex officio** | Yes — must raise if apparent |
| **Prescription** | 30 years |
| **Confirmation** | Impossible |
| **Renunciation** | Impossible in advance |

---

## Nullité Relative

### Grounds

- **Vices du consentement**: [[Erreur (arts. 1110-1117)|Erreur]], [[Dol (art. 1116)|Dol]], [[Violence (arts. 1111-1114)|Violence]], [[Lésion (art. 1118)|Lésion]]
- **Incapacity**: [[Capacité (arts. 1123-1125)|Minors, protected adults]]
- **Ordre public de protection** (protecting private interest)

### Characteristics

| Feature | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Who may invoke** | Protected party only |
| **Judge ex officio** | No |
| **Prescription** | 5 years (art. 1304) — **1 year for lésion** |
| **Starting point** | Discovery (erreur/dol), cessation (violence), conclusion (lésion) |
| **Confirmation** | Possible (arts. 1337 et s.) |

---

## Exception de Nullité

### Principle

> **Imprescriptible**: The right to oppose nullity as a **defense** against enforcement action never prescribes.

### Scope

Applies to **both** absolute and relative nullity.

### Practical Effect

Even after prescription of affirmative action:
- Cannot sue to annul contract
- **But** can defend against enforcement by invoking nullity

---

## Confirmation (arts. 1337 et s.)

### Definition

Act by which protected party **renounces** right to invoke nullity.

### Requirements (Poelmans §232)

| Requirement | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Timing** | Only after right has arisen (not in advance) |
| **Knowledge** | Must know grounds of nullity |
| **Intent** | Must show intention to repair the vice |

### Forms

- **Express**: Written acknowledgment
- **Tacit**: Voluntary execution showing non-equivocal intent to repair

### What Does NOT Constitute Confirmation

**Case** (Poelmans): Buyers' letter demanding construction start ≠ confirmation — no intent to repair vice apparent.

---

## Effects of Nullity

### Retroactive Annihilation

Contract deemed **never to have existed** (*ab initio*).

### Restitutions

Parties must return what they received:
- In kind if possible
- By equivalent if not

### Exceptions

- **Successive contracts**: Often only prospective effect
- **Partial nullity**: If clause severable

---

## Ordre Public Sensu Stricto vs. De Protection

> **Poelmans §117**: Must distinguish type of ordre public rule.

| Type | Purpose | Sanction |
|------|---------|----------|
| **Sensu stricto** | General interest (political, moral, social, economic) | Nullité absolue |
| **De protection** | Protect weaker party, preserve contract balance | Nullité relative |

### Examples of Ordre Public de Protection

- **Pacte commissoire** prohibition — protects debtor
- Consumer protection — protects consumer
- Labor law protections — protects employee
