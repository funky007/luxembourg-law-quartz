# Force Majeure (arts. 1147-1148)

> **Type**: Defense to contractual liability  
> **Articles**: 1147-1148 Code civil  
> **Effect**: Exonerates debtor from liability

---

## Definition

Event that prevents performance and exonerates debtor from liability for non-performance.

---

## Three Cumulative Conditions

> **Luxembourg applies strict conception** — all three must be met.

| Condition | Test |
|-----------|------|
| **1. Extériorité** | External to debtor's sphere |
| **2. Imprévisibilité** | Unforeseeable at contract formation |
| **3. Irrésistibilité** | Insurmountable — prevents performance |

### 1. Extériorité

Event must be **outside debtor's control**.

**Not external**: Debtor's illness (generally), employee's fault, equipment failure.

### 2. Imprévisibilité

Event **could not be foreseen** at time of contracting.

**Test**: Would reasonable parties have anticipated this risk?

**Not unforeseeable**: Known seasonal patterns, foreseeable economic conditions.

### 3. Irrésistibilité

Event makes performance **impossible**, not merely more difficult or expensive.

**Mere difficulty ≠ force majeure**: Increased costs, changed circumstances generally insufficient.

---

## Effects

### Temporary Impossibility

- Obligation **suspended**
- Resumes when event ends
- Unless delay makes performance useless

### Permanent Impossibility

- Obligation **extinguished**
- Debtor exonerated from liability
- Risk allocation depends on contract type

---

## Risk Allocation

### Obligations de Donner (art. 1138)

**Res perit domino**: Risk passes to owner/creditor from contract formation.

### Obligations de Faire

**Res perit debitori**: Risk generally on debtor until performance.

---

## Partial Force Majeure

If event only partially prevents performance:
- Partial exoneration
- Debtor must perform what remains possible

---

## Contractual Modifications

### Force Majeure Clauses

Parties may:
- **Define** what constitutes force majeure
- **Exclude** certain events
- **Allocate** risk differently
- **Limit** effects

### Validity

Generally enforceable unless:
- Contrary to ordre public
- Excludes liability for dol or faute lourde

---

## Banking Application

### Payment Obligations

Force majeure **rarely** excuses payment obligations:
- Money is fungible
- Alternative sources exist
- Mere financial difficulty insufficient

### Service Obligations

May apply to:
- System failures (if truly external)
- Regulatory changes preventing performance
- War, terrorism, natural disasters

### Pandemic Considerations

COVID-19 raised force majeure issues:
- Generally recognized in some contexts
- Not automatic excuse
- Depends on specific impact on obligation
