# Obligations alternatives

> **Type**: Obligations à objets multiples  
> **Articles**: 1189-1195 Code civil  
> **Feature**: Multiple objects, performance of one liberates

---

## Definition (Poelmans §328)

> Obligation alternative exists when obligation comprises **multiple objects** but debtor is discharged by performing **only one**.

**Example**: "I will deliver horse A **or** horse B."

---

## Distinction from Other Multiple-Object Obligations

| Type | Requirement | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Conjonctive** | Perform **all** objects | Deliver A **and** B |
| **Alternative** | Perform **one** of multiple | Deliver A **or** B |
| **Facultative** | Principal object + substitution option | Deliver A (or may substitute B) |

---

## Who Has the Choice?

### Default Rule (art. 1190)

> Choice belongs to **debtor**.

### Contractual Variation

Parties may give choice to **creditor**.

---

## Exercise of Choice

### By Debtor

Debtor chooses by:
- Declaration to creditor
- Performance of chosen object

### By Creditor (if agreed)

Creditor chooses by:
- Declaration to debtor
- Demand for specific object

### Irrevocability

Once exercised, choice is **irrevocable**.

---

## Impossibility Rules

### One Object Becomes Impossible

**Without debtor's fault**: Obligation subsists for remaining object(s).

**With debtor's fault**: 
- If choice was debtor's → must perform remaining
- If choice was creditor's → may demand remaining or damages

### All Objects Become Impossible

**Without fault**: Obligation extinguished.

**With fault**: Liable for damages (value of last object to perish).

### Art. 1193 — Disappearance of Value

> Poelmans: Mere loss of **value** (e.g., shares becoming worthless) ≠ disappearance of object itself. Art. 1193 requires physical or legal impossibility.

---

## Effects of Choice

### Concentration

Once choice made, obligation becomes **simple** — as if only one object existed from start.

### Retroactive Effect

Choice operates **retroactively** to contract formation.

---

## Comparison: Alternative vs. Facultative

| Aspect | Alternative | Facultative |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **In obligatione** | Multiple objects | One object |
| **In solutione** | One performance | One (or substitute) |
| **Choice holder** | Debtor or creditor | Debtor only |
| **Creditor can demand** | Any object | Only principal object |
| **If principal impossible** | Other object(s) remain | Obligation extinguished |

---

## Banking Application

### Alternative Payment Methods

Loan agreements may provide:
- Payment in EUR or USD (alternative currency)
- Delivery of securities or cash equivalent

### Settlement Options

- Physical delivery **or** cash settlement
- Different forms of collateral

### Careful Drafting

Distinguish clearly:
- True alternative (either/or)
- Facultative (principal + substitution right)
- Conjunctive (all required)
