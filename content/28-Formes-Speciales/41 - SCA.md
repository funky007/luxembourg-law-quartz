# Societe en Commandite par Actions (SCA)

> **Type**: Special Company Form (Hybrid)
> **Source**: Steichen, *Precis de droit des societes*, Section 3, n 1099-1131
> **Key Legislation**: Art. 600-1 et s. L1915; Loi du 12 juillet 2013; Loi du 10 aout 2016

---

## 1. Overview & History

### 1.1 Introduction

**Steichen, n 1099**: In the SCA, there exist, as in the SEC (societe en commandite simple), two categories of shareholders (Art. 600-1 L1915). The first category, which may be reduced to a single person, is that of the **commandites** (general partners). The second is that of the **commanditaires** (limited partners).

**Steichen, n 1099**: The commandites are, as in the SEC, general partners (*associes en nom*), indefinitely liable for corporate debts. The commanditaires, as in the SEC, are only liable up to the amount of their contributions, but unlike the commanditaires of the SEC, the rights of the commanditaires of the SCA are negotiable. The personal character of the partner, even commanditaire, in the SEC is not found in the commanditaires of the SCA.

**Steichen, n 1099**: Instead of holding partnership interests (*parts d'interet*), they hold **shares** (*actions*), the status of commanditaires resembling that of shareholders in the SA.

### 1.2 Key Characteristics

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Legal Nature** | Hybrid form combining SEC and SA features |
| **Commandites Liability** | Unlimited and joint liability (*indefinie et solidaire*) |
| **Commanditaires Liability** | Limited to contributions (*mise determinee*) |
| **Minimum Capital** | Same as SA (EUR 30,000) |
| **Share Capital** | Divided into shares (actions) for commanditaires |
| **Management** | By gerant(s), often a commandite |
| **Key Advantage** | Stability of management; separation of financial and operational power |

### 1.3 Regulatory Framework

**Steichen, n 1100**: The SCA is therefore an original composite of the SEC and the SA. The legislator determined the legal regime of the SCA by reference to that of SAs, referring to the provisions applicable to SAs (Art. 600-2 L1915). The 1915 Law merely indicates the differences by providing specific derogatory provisions where it considered necessary.

**Steichen, n 1100**: The derogatory rules are inspired by rules governing SECs. They only apply in principle where the legislator has so provided. In addition to explicit derogations, there is, however, an implicit derogation: the reference to SA rules exists only insofar as there are no incompatibilities resulting from the specificities of the SCA. The reference is therefore **not automatic**.

### 1.4 Historical Interest and Revival

**Steichen, n 1101**: The SCA had its hour of glory in the 19th century, at a time when governmental authorization was required for the formation of an SA. The SCA, due to the commandite's unlimited obligation, did not need this authorization, which largely explains its success at the time (the "fever of commandites" - *fievre des commandites*).

**Steichen, n 1101**: It experienced a certain eclipse since then, to the point that for a moment consideration was given to abolishing it as a legal form during the overhaul of French company law in 1966. But it enjoys a clear resurgence of interest since then.

**Steichen, n 1101**: The resurgence is due less to supposed flexibility (the SCA regulation being modeled on that applicable to SAs) than to the **stability of management**: it is not difficult for the commandites to escape the control of the commanditaires by arranging for themselves the management of the company through an appropriate clause in the articles. The SCA thus offers great capacity to resist a hostile takeover of the company.

**Steichen, n 1101**: A second advantage is the **dissociation of financial and operational power** that it allows: the commanditaire provides the funds, and the commandite his work force. The SCA therefore often constitutes an ideal vehicle for organizing succession in family businesses and as a buyout holding in the context of LBO (*leveraged buy-out*) operations.

## 2. Formation

### 2.1 Founders

**Steichen, n 1102**: At least **one commandite** and at least **one commanditaire** are required to form an SCA, these two qualities not being able to be united in the same person.

**Steichen, n 1102**: The minimum number of commanditaires was long debated. The use of the plural for commanditaires in the original text suggested that the law required a plurality of commanditaires. But as the SA could in the meantime be created as a single-member company, there was no longer any reason to remain more demanding for the SCA. The 2013 law confirmed the practice that had already been established that **a single commanditaire suffices**.

### 2.2 Capital and Shares

**Steichen, n 1102**: The rules regarding minimum share capital and its release governing SAs are applicable to the SCA. The share capital of the SCA is divided into shares, except for the contributions of the commandite who receives partnership interests (*parts d'interet*) due to his status which resembles that of commandites in the SEC.

**Steichen, n 1102**: Often the commandites also make contributions in services (*apports en industrie*) not entering into the company's share capital. These contributions in services are logically remunerated by a share of profits fixed by the articles, which is generally quite high, as it remunerates the extent of the commandite's financial commitments.

### 2.3 Company Name

**Steichen, n 1103**: The SCA now only has a **denomination sociale** (company name). Since the 2013 law, Article 104 L1915 was repealed. Since then, the SCA operates only under a denomination sociale (Art. 600-3 n 1 L1915). The reason for the legislative change was that it no longer seemed necessary to provide a special provision on the question of naming the SCA, since the SA regime is, in any case, applicable by virtue of Article 600-2 L1915.

**Steichen, n 1103**: It follows that there are no longer any obstacles to a commanditaire's name appearing in the SCA's name; in particular, this will not entail their unlimited and joint liability.

### 2.4 Publicity Requirements

**Steichen, n 1103**: As the SCA can easily be confused with the SA, the legislator provides accompanying publicity measures for all SCAs. Thus, the SCA must mention, on all documents emanating from it, the term "**societe en commandite par actions**." It will also indicate its registered office, its registration number in the RCS, and the seat of the competent district court in legal actions against the company.

### 2.5 Securities

**Steichen, n 1103**: The kinship with the SA is found in many respects, including financing of the SCA. Thus, SCAs can issue shares, bonds, and more generally transferable securities (*valeurs mobilieres*) like societes anonymes.

## 3. Governance (Gerance)

### 3.1 Appointment of Gerant(s)

**Steichen, n 1108**: According to Article 600-5 L1915: "The management of the company belongs to one or more gerants, commandite shareholders or not, designated in accordance with the articles." Gerants therefore do not have to be chosen from among the commandites; the task of corporate management can also be entrusted to third parties, but **not to commanditaires**.

**Steichen, n 1108**: As the commandite can be a legal entity, the management of the SCA is often entrusted in practice to a **limited liability legal entity** (e.g., to an SARL).

**Steichen, n 1108**: The gerant or gerants are designated in accordance with the articles, at the time of formation as well as during the life of the company. If the gerant is appointed in the articles (*gerant statutaire*), he will thereby benefit from de facto irrevocability.

**Steichen, n 1108**: In practice, management is most often single (*gerance unique*), but it is possible to appoint **several gerants**. In this case, the gerants, unless the articles provide otherwise, do not form a college; their powers remain concurrent.

### 3.2 Gerant Statutaire vs. Gerant Non-Statutaire

**Steichen, n 1109**: A distinction must be made between the statutory gerant (*gerant statutaire*) and the gerant chosen outside the articles. For the gerant chosen outside the articles, the rules applicable to societes anonymes will apply regarding dismissal.

**Steichen, n 1109**: The situation is more complicated for statutory gerants. The starting point is that the rules applicable to amendments to the articles must in any case be followed. The **consent of the commandite gerant** is also required. If he was appointed in the company's articles, the gerant, even if not a commandite, must approve his own dismissal because he is part of the company contract.

**Steichen, n 1109**: As the dismissal of the statutory gerant in principle only takes place with the consent of the person concerned, the risk of deadlock within the company is real. In this situation, only the possibility of **judicial dismissal** remains, for example when the gerant's activity repeatedly and sufficiently seriously disregards the corporate interest.

**Steichen, n 1110**: But the consent of the statutory gerant regarding his dismissal is only a default rule, applicable in the absence of statutory provisions to the contrary. The law allows the **articles to provide for other rules** (Art. 600-9 L1915), so it is quite possible to provide for the dismissal of the gerant, third party or commandite, under the usual conditions for amending articles; or even *ad nutum* as in the SA.

### 3.3 Resignation

**Steichen, n 1110**: The gerants not commandites, even if they are statutory, can **resign** as in societes anonymes.

**Steichen, n 1110**: The situation of the **statutory gerant** is again different. By analogy with the situation of commandite gerants in the commandite simple, it is doubtful that the statutory gerant can freely resign, as he is part of the company contract. His resignation must have a **legitimate cause** and be accepted by the extraordinary general meeting of commanditaires who must appoint a new gerant on the same occasion.

### 3.4 Remuneration

**Steichen, n 1108**: The remuneration of the gerant constitutes, depending on the case, a salary or the remuneration of a contribution in services. This distinction is of interest from a tax point of view. Remuneration representing a salary constitutes deductible charges for the company. It is otherwise for remuneration that compensates a contribution in services, which therefore constitutes non-deductible dividends for the company.

**Steichen, n 1108**: It will obviously not suffice to establish an employment contract in order to deduct the salary from the SCA's taxable income: the employment contract must meet the substantive conditions for an employment contract, which includes the requirement of a **subordination link** between the gerant and the company. Such a link will most often be very difficult to establish, as the SCA's gerant is in principle not "under the orders" of an employer.

## 4. Powers of the Gerant

### 4.1 External Powers

**Steichen, n 1111**: The law does not provide for specific provisions concerning the powers of the gerant; their powers are analogous to those of the gerant of the SEC. However, unlike the SEC, the SCA being a share company where the concern for protecting creditors prevails over considerations of safeguarding shareholder interests, the gerants have the most extensive powers to act in all circumstances in the company's name.

**Steichen, n 1111**: In their relations with third parties, the gerants bind the company so that the company will be bound even for acts that do not fall within the **corporate purpose** (*objet social*), unless the third party knew that the act exceeded that purpose. As in the SA, the mere publication of the articles is not sufficient to constitute this proof.

**Steichen, n 1111**: Similarly, **limitations on the powers** of the gerant are unenforceable against third parties. On the other hand, in relations between gerants and shareholders, the corporate purpose as well as statutory restrictions on the powers of the gerants are perfectly valid and will be taken into consideration in connection with an action for liability.

### 4.2 Multiple Gerants

**Steichen, n 1111**: In case of plurality of gerants, each of them holds separately all powers of external management. Thus, the opposition possibly made by one gerant to the acts of another gerant will be without effect with regard to third parties, unless it can be established that they had knowledge of it at the time of the execution of the act.

**Steichen, n 1111**: On the other hand, statutory clauses requiring the signature of two or more gerants to bind the company are enforceable against third parties as in the SARL.

### 4.3 Approval of Statutory Amendments

**Steichen, n 1111**: The gerants must also approve, unless the articles provide otherwise, any **modification of the articles** (Art. 600-9 L1915).

### 4.4 Specific Attributions

**Steichen, n 1112**: To the general power of decision in the internal order and representation in the external order are added a number of specific attributions modeled on those applicable to SAs: the gerants are obliged, as in the SA, to convene a general meeting in case of loss of half of the share capital and again when the loss reaches three-quarters of the share capital; they must convene a meeting upon request of shareholders holding at least 10% of the share capital; etc.

### 4.5 Liability

**Steichen, n 1112**: The gerants are also liable, as is the case for administrators of societes anonymes, for **management faults** (*fautes de gestion*) as well as **regulatory faults** (*fautes de regularite*) they may have committed. This liability under the regime applicable to societes anonymes is especially useful in the presence of third-party gerants.

**Steichen, n 1112**: When management is assumed by a commandite of the company (as is most often the case), third parties generally do not need to seek their liability, their obligation to the company's liabilities being unlimited. The civil liability of gerants is invoked through the **social action** (*action sociale*); commanditaires do not have an individual direct action.

## 5. Partners (Associes)

### 5.1 Commandites (General Partners)

#### 5.1.1 Status

**Steichen, n 1114**: The status of commandites in the SCA is identical to that of commandites in the SEC: only the commandites, and among them the gerants commandites, have the quality of **commercants**. Just as in the SEC, the commandite in the SCA may content himself with making contributions in services. The commandites (or third parties, but not the commanditaires) can perform acts of **corporate management**.

**Steichen, n 1114**: The commandites are certainly the guarantors of the SCA, but they do not constitute the **primary debtors** of the corporate creditors. These creditors contracted with the company, and it is to the company that they must first turn for settlement of their claims. Article 1400-1 L1915 provides that a **condemnation of the SCA must precede** that of the commandites.

**Steichen, n 1114**: The commandites can be **natural persons** as well as **legal entities**; in the latter case, a commercial company form must be chosen (which therefore excludes the civil company).

#### 5.1.2 Transfer of Rights

**Steichen, n 1115**: One might have thought that the social rights of commandites could only be transferred under the same conditions as in the SEC. Inalienability would in that case be the principle, although the articles could arrange without totally eliminating it.

**Steichen, n 1115**: But the legislator took another route by also attributing to the commandite the status of "**actionnaire**." The essence of the share being its free negotiability, the commandites can transfer their **droits sociaux** according to the simplified forms of commercial law. No consent is therefore needed from the other commandites or from the commanditaires to transfer the shares of commandites to other commandites or to third parties.

#### 5.1.3 Contributions and Rights

**Steichen, n 1115**: The commandites normally make **contributions to the share capital** (*apports au capital social*), for which they also receive "shares." The commandites who also exercise gerant functions can additionally make **contributions in services** (*apports en industrie*) not entering into the company's share capital.

**Steichen, n 1115**: The **rights** of the commandites, whether shareholder rights or rights arising from contributions in services, are generally **priority** compared to the rights of commanditaires, which is justified by the debt obligation incumbent upon them. This priority right covers not only ordinary dividends and accumulated reserves but also the liquidation surplus (*boni de liquidation*).

### 5.2 Commanditaires (Limited Partners)

#### 5.2.1 Limited Liability

**Steichen, n 1116**: According to Article 600-1 L1915, the commanditaires only answer for corporate debts **up to the amount of their contributions**. Their status is therefore, as regards their liability, that of partners of a limited liability company.

#### 5.2.2 Prohibition on Management (Non-Immixtion)

**Steichen, n 1117**: The principle of non-immixtion of commanditaires in the **management** of the company finds its justification in the limitation of liability they enjoy. It would be paradoxical for partners, whose risks are limited, to be able to bind the company vis-a-vis third parties, while only the commandites are indefinitely and jointly liable for corporate debts.

**Steichen, n 1117**: Before the 2013 reform, the scope of the non-immixtion principle was quite broad: it prohibited commanditaires not only from performing acts of management themselves but also from acting under a specific power of attorney. A Law of 12 July 2013 changed this regime.

**Steichen, n 1117**: While it continues to prohibit commanditaires from becoming the de jure gerants of the SCA, nothing prevents them henceforth from acting either on a one-off or regular basis on behalf of the management, provided only that they indicate their **quality as representative of the management** in their acts (Art. 600-6 L1915). In this case, they can even benefit from a general power of representation.

**Steichen, n 1117**: The power of representation must not be confused with the power of decision: the commanditaire who has a power of representation cannot perform acts of management autonomously; he will only act on the basis of resolutions adopted by the company's management that he will merely implement vis-a-vis third parties.

#### 5.2.3 Acts Not Constituting Immixtion

**Steichen, n 1117**: The non-immixtion principle concerns only corporate management. It does not, however, prevent simple **acts of advice or technical opinions**. In the same vein, the fact of **participating in the appointment of gerants** constitutes only an internal management act that is not contrary to the non-immixtion principle. Similarly, this principle does not prohibit the conclusion of an **employment contract** with the company, provided that the salaried functions do not create any ambiguity in the minds of third parties.

**Steichen, n 1117**: Article 600-6 L1915 specifies that the following do not constitute management acts: the exercise of shareholder prerogatives, advice and opinions given to the company or its affiliated entities or their gerants, control and supervision acts, granting of loans, guaranties or security or any other assistance to the company or its affiliated entities, and authorizations given to gerants in cases provided for in the articles for acts exceeding their powers.

#### 5.2.4 Sanctions for Immixtion

**Steichen, n 1118**: When a commanditaire performs an act of external management, Article 600-6 L1915 provides that he loses the benefit of the limitation of liability. He is indeed **jointly liable** (*solidairement*) with the commandites for all corporate debts resulting from external management acts he performed.

**Steichen, n 1118**: Due to the reference by the legislator to analogous provisions applicable to commanditaires of SECs, the judges may, depending on the number and importance of the prohibited management acts, either limit the joint liability to the sole consequences of the acts performed, or decide that the commanditaire will be jointly liable for the consequences of all the company's commitments.

#### 5.2.5 Rights of Commanditaires

**Steichen, n 1118**: While they cannot interfere in corporate management, commanditaires nonetheless have the quality of partner with all the prerogatives that may result from this. They therefore **participate in general meetings**, of which they can request the convocation, provided the request is made by one or more commanditaires representing at least 10% of the company's share capital.

**Steichen, n 1118**: Like shareholders of the SA, commanditaires have the right to receive **dividends**, to participate in the distribution of accumulated reserves, and in the liquidation surplus (*boni de liquidation*). However, account must be taken of the often priority rights granted to commandites in the exercise of financial rights.

#### 5.2.6 Transfer of Shares

**Steichen, n 1119**: The law is silent on the transfer of social rights in the SCA, but it leaves no doubt that these are "**actions**" and not "*parts sociales*." SA rules are therefore applicable. They imply that the shares of commanditaires constitute **freely negotiable** transferable securities; they are therefore capable of stock exchange listing.

**Steichen, n 1119**: There is therefore no need for any consent, neither from other commanditaires nor from commandites, in order to transfer commanditaire shares to other commanditaires or to third parties. The free negotiability of commanditaire shares being of the essence of the SCA, it can only be **limited, without being able to be totally excluded**, by an approval clause (*clause d'agrement*).

## 6. Reorganization

### 6.1 Amendments to Articles

**Steichen, n 1106**: The shareholders' meeting brings together commanditaires and commandites. Decisions will only be validly taken if they meet the quorum and majority conditions required for statutory amendments in the SA.

**Steichen, n 1106**: Furthermore, decisions taken at an extraordinary general meeting must in principle be **approved by the gerants**, therefore by all or part of the commandites (Art. 600-9 L1915: "only in agreement with the gerants"). The refusal of a single gerant can therefore lead to an insoluble conflict, if the articles do not provide for contrary clauses.

### 6.2 Dissolution

**Steichen, n 1106**: In addition to the causes of dissolution applicable to all companies, SCAs are subject to all provisions relating to the dissolution of societes anonymes, and in particular provisions relating to the **loss of half or three-quarters of the share capital**.

**Steichen, n 1106**: Concerning the status of commandites, the provisions relating to **societes en nom collectif** apply. For this reason, in case of death, bankruptcy, or incapacity affecting one of the gerants, the company is in principle dissolved. However, the articles may provide for the company's continuation with the other gerants.

**Steichen, n 1107**: The SCA presupposing the presence of two categories of shareholders (commandites and commanditaires), certain events affecting each of these two categories will lead to the dissolution of the company: this will be the case if the company comes to have no more commandite or commanditaire.

## 7. Supervisory Board (Conseil de Surveillance)

### 7.1 Composition and Appointment

**Steichen, n 1124**: One of the main originalities of the SCA is that it provides in principle for the mandatory designation, by the ordinary general meeting, of a **supervisory board** composed of at least **three members**. The status of commissioners (conditions of appointment, dismissal, duration of functions) is fixed by the articles and failing that by reference to the status of administrators of societes anonymes.

**Steichen, n 1124**: As the SCA is intended to gather more shareholders than the SEC has partners, the legislator assumed that these dispersed shareholders would not be able to truly supervise corporate management themselves. This is why he imagined a sort of intermediate body between the gerants and the commanditaires, the "supervisory board," whose role is to **represent the commanditaires** vis-a-vis the gerants.

### 7.2 Functions

**Steichen, n 1124**: The supervisory board is notably responsible for:
- **Controlling the company accounts** and assessing the regularity and appropriateness of the various management operations
- Obtaining the same information as the commissioner in the SA
- Presenting a **report** to the ordinary general meeting that must approve the company accounts

**Steichen, n 1125**: To this traditional **control function**, the legislator adds an **advisory function**: "giving its opinions on matters that the gerants submit to it and authorizing acts that exceed their powers" (Art. 600-8 L1915).

**Steichen, n 1125**: The supervisory board therefore appears to have a permanent control power over all acts of the gerant and thus serves as a sort of counterweight and brake to the latter's power. It is the counterpart of the statutory gerant's irrevocability in principle in the SCA.

**Steichen, n 1125**: But its power is limited to giving **advice** (*avis*), mere internal measures, without ever performing **management acts** itself; the supervisory board cannot interfere in the management of the company.

### 7.3 Composition Rules

**Steichen, n 1126**: The **commandites**, even non-gerants, cannot be part of the supervisory board. But as they are also shareholders and can also be commanditaires, nothing prohibits their participation and vote at the meeting.

**Steichen, n 1126**: Often, the supervisory board is composed of **commanditaires** only, but nothing prohibits appointing **third parties** (even exclusively). Members of the supervisory board are not subject to any of the causes of incompatibility for kinship or marriage with the gerants found in reviseurs d'entreprises.

### 7.4 Liability

**Steichen, n 1125**: The mission of the supervisory board being similar to that of the supervisory board of the SA, its members are subject to the same **liability** as the members of the supervisory board in the SA.

**Steichen, n 1125**: Being a **collegial body**, the members of the supervisory board will be declared jointly liable for their faults if they have committed a common indivisible fault; otherwise their liability will be individual and dependent on the personal faults committed by each in the exercise of his functions. The faults of supervisory board members can only be supervision faults, and they can never be liable for management acts performed by the gerants and the results of corporate management.

### 7.5 Replacement by Reviseur d'Entreprises

**Steichen, n 1127**: By charging the general meeting with designating an approved reviseur d'entreprises when certain size conditions are met, Article 69 para. 3 of the RCS law eliminates the supervisory board for companies having their accounts audited by an approved reviseur d'entreprises.

**Steichen, n 1127**: However, the mission of the approved reviseur d'entreprises is less extensive than that of the supervisory board, its mission being limited to certifying annual accounts. This is explained in part by the fact that control by the supervisory board is too often **control of complacency** (*controle de complaisance*) exercised by persons singularly lacking in competence.

## 8. Taxation

**Steichen, n 1101**: The duality of the SCA's legal regime is also found on the tax level.

**Steichen, n 1101**: On the one hand, the legislator ranks the SCA rather on the side of societes anonymes than of SECs. Thus the SCA constitutes an "**opaque**" company like the SA and not a "**transparent**" company like the SEC. The SCA thereby benefits, as a separate tax subject, from conventional advantages resulting from double taxation conventions signed by Luxembourg as well as Community directives on business taxation.

**Steichen, n 1101**: This notably allows the SCA to benefit, under certain conditions, from a dividend flow without withholding tax abroad and an exemption of these dividends and capital gains relating to the subsidiary at the level of the SCA.

**Steichen, n 1101**: But the SCA also participates in the nature of **partnerships**, at least as regards the commandite. Thus, if income other than that from participation in the commandite's profits is certainly deductible from the SCA, this same income will constitute taxable commercial profits for the commandite, regardless of the nature of this income (e.g., salaries, rents). This approach is consistent with the principles governing transparent companies, where all income received by the partner constitutes a variety of his share of commercial profit.

## Banking & Finance Application

The SCA is particularly relevant in banking and finance for the following reasons:

1. **Private Equity Funds**: The SCA constitutes an ideal vehicle for organizing private equity funds, where the general partner (commandite-gerant) manages the fund while limited partners (commanditaires) provide capital but have no management role.

2. **Fund Structures**: The 2013 reform specifically modernized the SCA regime to accommodate alternative investment fund structures, facilitating its use as a vehicle for AIFMD-compliant funds.

3. **Family Office Structures**: The separation between management power (commandite) and capital contribution (commanditaires) makes the SCA ideal for succession planning in family businesses.

4. **Protection Against Hostile Takeovers**: The stability of the gerant and the consent requirements for statutory changes provide significant protection for founding shareholders against hostile takeovers.

5. **LBO Structures**: The SCA serves as an effective buyout holding vehicle where management (commandite) and investors (commanditaires) can have clearly defined roles.

6. **Hybrid Tax Treatment**: The partial transparency for commandites combined with opacity at company level can provide tax planning opportunities.

**Key considerations for finance**:
- The commandite bears unlimited liability, which can be mitigated by using an SARL or other limited liability entity as the commandite
- The supervisory board (or reviseur d'entreprises for larger companies) provides investor protection
- Commanditaires cannot actively manage the fund but can provide advice and authorize specific transactions
- The 2013 and 2016 reforms significantly enhanced the flexibility of the SCA for fund structuring
