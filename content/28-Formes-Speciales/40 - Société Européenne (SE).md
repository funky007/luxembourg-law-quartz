# Societe Europeenne (SE)

> **Type**: Special Company Form
> **Source**: Steichen, *Precis de droit des societes*, Section 1, n 1046-1078
> **Key Legislation**: Reglement (CE) n 2157/2001; Directive 2001/86/CE; Loi du 13 juillet 2006; Art. 410-1 et s. L1915

---

## 1. Overview & History

### 1.1 Introduction

**Steichen, n 1046**: The birth date of the European Company (*Societe europeenne* or "SE") is 8 October 2004. Being a Community regulation, it was directly applicable in Luxembourg since then, even if no national provisions had been adopted in the matter. The Law of 13 July 2006 finally implemented the Community regulation on a national level.

### 1.2 Historical Development

**Steichen, n 1046**: The SE has a long history behind it. The first draft of a "European company" was presented to the General Secretariat of the Council of Europe in 1949. After the drafting of a report by a group of experts gathered around the Dean of the Faculty of Law of Rotterdam (1967), the Brussels Commission drew up a proposal for a Council Regulation in 1970. Modified in 1975, the text was not only of considerable scope (284 articles) but also too innovative.

**Steichen, n 1047**: The true cause of the SE project's stagnation was undoubtedly the desire to give workers a large place in this type of company. Worker participation in corporate management, based on the German co-management (*cogestion*) system, was rejected by certain States and finally resulted in the rejection of the project in the form of a polite and prolonged silence.

**Steichen, n 1047**: Taking note of these difficulties, work was relaunched in 1987 and resulted in a new proposal in 1989, modified once again in 1992. After the adoption in 1994 of the directive on the works council and last-minute negotiations, a compromise was reached at the Nice Summit in 2000.

### 1.3 Key Characteristics

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Legal Nature** | European societe anonyme with transnational vocation |
| **Liability** | Limited to share capital (like SA) |
| **Minimum Capital** | EUR 120,000 (Art. 4 Reglement) |
| **Currency** | Must be expressed in euros |
| **Legal Personality** | Acquired upon registration (not at formation) |
| **Governance Options** | Monistic (board) or Dualistic (management + supervisory) |
| **Registered Office** | Must be in same Member State as central administration |

### 1.4 Regulatory Framework

**Steichen, n 1048**: The matter is regulated by Articles 9 and 10 of the Regulation combining the provisions applicable to the SE resulting from the Regulation with a *renvoi en cascade* (cascading reference). Thus, Article 9 postulates that the SE is primarily governed by the provisions of the Regulation. It is only in areas where the Regulation is silent or expressly refers to national legislation that such legislation, or, as the case may be, the company's articles of association, will apply.

**Steichen, n 1048**: The *renvoi* exists with respect to matters outside the scope of the Regulation. Thus, areas as varied as tax law, competition law, bankruptcy law, or criminal law are not covered by the Regulation. The *renvoi* also exists regarding national provisions concerning companies, subject, however, to their compliance with Community directives applicable to societes anonymes.

**Steichen, n 1049**: As Henri SYNVET remarked: "The Community regulation provides the theme, national legislation adds its variations." It follows that there is not a single SE model, but as many different types as there are different legislations within the European Union.

## 2. Formation

### 2.1 General Requirements

**Steichen, n 1058**: The formation of the SE is reserved solely for companies and other business groupings having sufficient links with the European Union. Consequently, only companies having their registered office (*siege statutaire*) and their central administration (corresponding to the effective management seat) in a Member State can participate in the formation of an SE.

**Steichen, n 1058**: The Regulation provides, however, for the option to open the formation of an SE to a company having its registered office in a Member State while having its central administration in a third State, provided, however, that the company maintains an effective and continuous link with the economy of a Member State.

### 2.2 Capital Requirements

**Steichen, n 1058**: The SE is a societe anonyme (its capital is "divided into shares"; Art. 1-2 Regulation) with a **minimum capital of EUR 120,000**. It is therefore not possible, unlike ordinary societes anonymes, to form the SE with share capital expressed in a currency other than the euro.

**Steichen, n 1058**: The Regulation also allows Member States to provide for a higher minimum share capital for certain types of activities. This covers insurance undertakings, credit institutions, and generally operators in the financial sector. Luxembourg has not made use of this option.

### 2.3 Personality and Registration

**Steichen, n 1059**: Like any societe anonyme, the SE will be formed by notarial deed. But it is not at this moment that the SE acquires legal personality, this being conditional on the registration of the SE. The SE acquires legal personality on the date of registration in the RCS. Acts performed on behalf of the SE in formation before registration will bind the persons who performed them jointly and severally unless the SE, once registered, takes over such acts retroactively.

### 2.4 Modes of Formation

**Steichen, n 1060**: The SE cannot be created *ex nihilo*, but only by using one of the limited routes of access enumerated. The SE is therefore only intended for existing businesses: business groupings under private as well as public law.

#### 2.4.1 Formation by Merger

**Steichen, n 1060**: This is the "royal" route to form an SE. Two or more national companies decide to merge in order to acquire an additional dimension. They can merge their activities within an SE, either as a **merger by absorption** (the absorbing company taking the form of an SE) or as a **merger by formation of a new company**.

**Steichen, n 1060**: The preliminary formalities for the merger include the obligation to establish a **merger project** (*projet de fusion*) subject to publication measures in the Member State of each company participating in the merger. After examination of the merger project by independent experts, the shareholders' meeting of the merging companies votes on the merger.

**Steichen, n 1061**: The **verification of legality** is complex and takes place in two stages. The first verification takes place in the Member State of each company participating in the merger. The competent authority of that Member State (the notary for Luxembourg) verifies that the substantive and formal conditions for mergers have been effectively respected. After verification, the competent authority issues, within a maximum of six months, a certificate to the competent authority of the Member State where the SE will be established.

#### 2.4.2 Formation of Holding SE

**Steichen, n 1062**: SAs or SARLs can form a holding SE if at least two of them are subject to the law of a different Member State. Failing that, they must have had for at least two years a subsidiary subject to the law of another Member State or a branch in another Member State.

**Steichen, n 1062**: The operation consists for the shareholders of the companies concerned in contributing at least the majority of voting rights to the holding SE in exchange for newly issued shares by the holding SE. Unlike the formation of the SE by merger, the legal personality of the participating companies continues after formation of the SE.

#### 2.4.3 Formation of Joint Subsidiary

**Steichen, n 1063**: Rather than asking shareholders of companies to contribute their shares to an SE, it is possible to leave the shareholding of these companies unchanged but to form the SE at a lower level, in the form of a joint subsidiary (*filiale commune*) by these companies.

**Steichen, n 1063**: The greatest flexibility of this mode of formation of the SE is also found in the companies that can participate in its formation. These include companies under civil or commercial law, including cooperatives and other legal entities under public or private law. Only groupings of persons not pursuing a profit-making purpose are excluded.

#### 2.4.4 Transformation

**Steichen, n 1064**: A societe anonyme formed under the law of a Member State and having its registered office as well as its central administration in the European Union can transform itself into an SE if it has had, for at least two years, a subsidiary subject to the law of another Member State.

**Steichen, n 1064**: The transformation cannot result in the transfer of the registered office to another Member State. The absence of substantial changes resulting from the transformation undoubtedly also justifies why the transformation of the societe anonyme does not cause either the dissolution of the company or the creation of a new legal entity.

## 3. Worker Involvement

### 3.1 Negotiation Procedure

**Steichen, n 1065**: Community authorities feared that the formation of the SE might be the pretext for dismantling certain acquired rights for workers. For this reason, Directive 2001/86/CE provides for a regime of worker involvement. This law was transposed into Luxembourg law by a Law of 25 August 2006.

**Steichen, n 1065**: This regime requires that the modalities relating to worker involvement be determined following a negotiation procedure prior to the formation of the SE, in concert with a **special negotiating body** (*groupe special de negociation*). To this end, a special negotiating body composed of worker representatives will be created. The negotiations will, in principle, not exceed six months, which may be extended by a further period of six months.

### 3.2 "Before-After" Principle

**Steichen, n 1066**: The Community instances also wished to avoid worker participation becoming a veritable Trojan horse for the SE. Hence the introduction in the SE directive text of the "**before-after**" principle.

**Steichen, n 1066**: According to this principle, if before the formation of the SE, none of the participating companies had introduced worker representation in their administrative or supervisory bodies, the SE can limit itself to informing and consulting its employees. No obligation, in that case, to organize worker participation in the management or supervision of the SE.

**Steichen, n 1066**: If, on the other hand, employee representation existed at the level of the participating companies, the SE must organize the same participation system for all its employees. To avoid a minority that is too weak from being able to impose its views on the majority, there must have been participation of at least a quarter of employees before the merger or a majority in the case of formation of a holding or joint subsidiary.

### 3.3 Default Rules (Reference Provisions)

**Steichen, n 1067**: If no agreement has been reached, so-called "reference provisions" (*dispositions de reference*) provided for in the Directive itself will apply for the organization of worker involvement. These reference provisions notably provide for a proportional distribution of the number of workers in each constituent company of the SE.

## 4. Governance

### 4.1 Choice of System

**Steichen, n 1068**: In the articles of the SE, one can opt for a **monistic system** which comprises only one administrative body (*organe d'administration*) or for a **dualistic system** in which a supervisory body (*organe de surveillance*) and a management body (*organe de direction*) coexist.

**Steichen, n 1068**: The Regulation does not impose a system. Luxembourg not only introduced the dualistic system for the SE but also extended it to ordinary societes anonymes under domestic law.

### 4.2 Common Rules

**Steichen, n 1069**: The Regulation establishes a number of common rules for both monistic and dualistic systems:

- **Term of Office**: Members of the SE's bodies are appointed for a period fixed by the articles. Their term cannot exceed six years. Terms are renewable unless the articles provide otherwise.
- **Eligibility**: A legal entity may be a member of an SE's body, unless the law of the Member State of the SE's registered office applicable to societes anonymes prohibits it (this is not the case in Luxembourg). The legal entity must designate a natural person to exercise the powers on its behalf.
- **Incompatibilities**: Incompatibilities are provided by the Regulation.

**Steichen, n 1069**: Unless the Regulation itself or the SE's articles provide otherwise, the internal rules concerning **quorum** and **decision-making** of the SE's bodies are as follows:
- Concerning quorum: at least half of the members must be present or represented
- Concerning decisions: they must be adopted by a majority of members present or represented
- In case of a tie, the chairman's vote is decisive

**Steichen, n 1069**: **Confidentiality** is enshrined in Article 49 of the Regulation: members of the SE's bodies are required not to disclose, even after the cessation of their functions, information they have about the SE whose disclosure could harm the company's interests.

### 4.3 Monistic System

**Steichen, n 1070**: The structure is simpler: the administrative body (*organe d'administration*) alone manages the SE.

**Steichen, n 1070**: The **number of members** of the administrative body or the rules for determining this number are fixed by the SE's articles. However, a Member State may set a minimum and, possibly, a maximum number of members. The administrative body must, however, be composed of at least **three members** when worker participation in the SE is organized in accordance with the directive.

**Steichen, n 1070**: The members of the administrative body are **appointed by the general meeting**. The members of the first administrative body may, however, be designated by the articles. The administrative body must meet at least every three months to deliberate on the company's affairs and their foreseeable development.

### 4.4 Dualistic System

**Steichen, n 1071**: In this system, a distinction is made between:
- The **management of the company**, which is assumed by the management body (*organe de direction*)
- The **control of management**, which is exercised by a supervisory body (*organe de surveillance*)

**Steichen, n 1071**: The management body thus manages the company under its own responsibility. At least every three months, the management body informs the supervisory body of the company's affairs and their foreseeable development. Besides this periodic information, the management body must communicate in due time to the supervisory body any information regarding events likely to have significant repercussions on the SE's situation.

**Steichen, n 1071**: The members of the management body are **appointed and dismissed** by the supervisory body. However, a Member State may permit the articles to provide that the member(s) of the management body be appointed and dismissed by the general meeting (this is the case in Luxembourg).

**Steichen, n 1071**: The articles determine the number of members of the management body. There may be only one. A member cannot belong simultaneously to both bodies (**incompatibility rule**), except in case of vacancy where a member of the supervisory body may exercise the functions of a member of the management body.

**Steichen, n 1072**: As for the supervisory body, its **function** consists of controlling management exercised by the management body, but not ensuring corporate management itself. The supervisory body:
- Receives quarterly or particular information from the management body
- Requests from the management body information deemed necessary
- Carries out or has carried out the verifications it considers necessary for the accomplishment of its mission

### 4.5 Authorization Requirements

**Steichen, n 1069**: The articles of the SE shall enumerate the categories of operations requiring authorization of the management body by the supervisory body (*Zustimmungsvorbehalt*). This system is also transposed in the monistic system, since the same text calls on the articles to determine the operations that "require an express decision of the administrative body."

**Steichen, n 1069**: Luxembourg had the good sense not to fix a national catalog, as this decision would have deterred SE promoters from establishing the company in the country.

## 5. General Meeting of Shareholders

### 5.1 Competence

**Steichen, n 1073**: The general meeting is competent in matters reserved to it by the Regulation itself, the legal provisions of the Member State where the SE has its registered office, and provisions taken in application of the Council directive of 8 October 2001 (on worker involvement).

**Steichen, n 1073**: Without prejudice to the rules provided by the Regulation, the organization and conduct of the general meeting as well as voting procedures are governed by the law of the Member State of the SE's registered office applicable to societes anonymes.

### 5.2 Meetings and Agenda

**Steichen, n 1073**: A general meeting must be held at least once per calendar year, within six months of the close of the financial year. Luxembourg adopted the option allowing the first general meeting to take place within 18 months of the SE's formation.

**Steichen, n 1073**: In principle, the **agenda** is determined by the body convening the meeting. Nevertheless, one or more shareholders holding together at least 10% of the subscribed capital (or a lower amount if provided by the articles) may request the inclusion of one or more new items on the agenda.

### 5.3 Voting Rules

**Steichen, n 1074**: General meeting decisions are taken by a **majority** of votes validly cast, unless the Regulation or national law applicable to societes anonymes requires a higher majority.

**Steichen, n 1074**: A **modification of the articles** can only be decided by a majority at least equal to two-thirds of the votes cast. However, the Regulation provides an exception: national law may authorize an absolute majority when at least half of the capital is represented. Luxembourg adopted this option which facilitates decision-making.

## 6. Reorganization

### 6.1 Retransformation

**Steichen, n 1075**: The SE can, at the earliest two years after its registration and after the accounts of the closed financial years have been approved, (re)transform itself into a societe anonyme of the State of its registered office. The transformation will not, unsurprisingly, result in the creation of a new legal entity.

### 6.2 Transfer of Registered Office

**Steichen, n 1075**: The transfer of the SE's registered office is certainly one of the Regulation's successes. The right to transfer one's registered office within the European Union is not enshrined by the Treaty on European Union (principle of freedom of establishment), so Member States are free to consider that the transfer of registered office can only take place through dissolution of the legal entity in the departure State with its reconstitution in the arrival State.

**Steichen, n 1075**: The Regulation, on the contrary, establishes the **principle of continuity of legal personality** of the SE upon transfer of its registered office from one Member State to another. The decision to transfer the registered office is taken by the shareholders following the procedure for statutory amendments.

**Steichen, n 1076**: The transfer of the registered office implies a **change in the law** applicable to the SE. As the central administration must be located in the country of the registered office, the transfer of the registered office will imply the transfer of the central administration abroad as well.

**Steichen, n 1076**: The management or administrative body must establish a **transfer project** specifying, in addition to the planned location of the new registered office, the consequences of the transfer on the worker involvement regime, the timetable for the operation, and the measures to be taken to protect the rights of shareholders and creditors.

**Steichen, n 1076**: The **competent authorities** (the Minister of Justice) then have a two-month period to oppose the transfer on grounds relating to the public interest. The SE cannot transfer its registered office when **insolvency or similar proceedings** have been opened against it.

## 7. Taxation

**Steichen, n 1077**: Recital 20 of the Regulation's explanatory memorandum specifies that the Regulation "does not cover other areas of law such as taxation...". The reason for the exclusion of tax matters from the Regulation lies in the desire to reach agreement on the text of the Regulation, failing agreement of the parties on tax matters.

**Steichen, n 1077**: It is therefore the national legislations that continue to apply. In other words, on the tax level, the SE is treated like any **transnational company**, i.e., it is taxed according to the principle of territoriality. SEs therefore remain subject to taxes of all Member States where their permanent establishments are located.

**Steichen, n 1077**: If Luxembourg is the registered office of the SE, taxation will in principle be on the **worldwide income** of the SE. However, in practice, taxation should be limited to **Luxembourg-source profits** only. Indeed, dividends (and capital gains) realized in connection with holding participations (hypothesis of the holding SE) will, in principle, be exempt in Luxembourg based on the parent-subsidiary privilege.

### 7.1 Future Developments (CCCTB/ACCIS)

**Steichen, n 1078**: The Commission in Brussels rightly considers that the success of the SE status is closely linked to the establishment of a **common European tax regime**. The approach advocated by the Commission is based on a consolidated tax base (CCCTB - Common Consolidated Corporate Tax Base or "ACCIS" in French), eliminating most international taxation problems such as cross-border loss compensation, intra-group transfer pricing, etc.

## Banking & Finance Application

The SE is particularly relevant in banking and finance for the following reasons:

1. **Cross-Border Mergers**: The SE facilitates reorganization of banking groups across EU Member States through mergers, particularly useful for consolidating operations or restructuring post-acquisition.

2. **Branch Networks**: An SE can operate through branches throughout the EU rather than through subsidiaries, potentially simplifying regulatory compliance and capital allocation.

3. **Holding Structures**: The holding SE benefits from Luxembourg's parent-subsidiary regime, allowing tax-efficient dividend flows within corporate groups.

4. **Transfer of Seat**: The ability to transfer registered office within the EU with continuity of legal personality is particularly valuable for financial institutions seeking to optimize their regulatory or tax position.

5. **Reputational Benefits**: The "European" designation signals transnational scope and European vocation, potentially beneficial for financial institutions seeking to emphasize their European character.

**Key limitations for finance**:
- The minimum capital of EUR 120,000 is well below regulatory capital requirements for banks and insurance companies
- Member States may impose higher capital requirements for financial sector entities
- The SE does not benefit from any special tax regime; it is taxed like an ordinary company
- Worker involvement requirements may add procedural complexity
