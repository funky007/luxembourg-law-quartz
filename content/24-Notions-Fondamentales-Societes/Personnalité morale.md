# Personnalité morale (Legal Personality)

> **Type**: Notion fondamentale
> **Source**: Steichen, *Précis de droit des sociétés*, Titre 3, Chapitre 1
> **Key Legislation**: Loi du 10 août 1915, arts. 2, 100-1 et s.

---

## Definition

> The *personnalité morale* (legal personality) is the capacity of a legal entity to be a subject of rights and obligations, distinct from its members.

**Steichen, Titre 3**: Companies acquire legal personality upon registration with the *Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés* (RCS). This grants them:
- An autonomous patrimony separate from associates' assets
- Capacity to contract, sue, and be sued
- Own domicile (*siège social*)
- Own name (*dénomination sociale*)

---

## 1. Acquisition of Legal Personality

### 1.1 Principle: Registration Requirement

**Art. 2 L1915**: Commercial companies acquire legal personality from the date of *dépôt* (filing) of the articles of association with the RCS.

| Company Type | Acquires Personality |
|-------------|---------------------|
| SA, SARL, SAS, SCA | Upon RCS filing |
| SNC, SCS | Upon RCS filing |
| Société civile | Upon RCS filing (since 2003) |
| SE | Upon RCS registration |

### 1.2 Pre-incorporation Period

**Problem**: Acts performed before registration.

**Art. 12 L1915**: Founders and persons acting on behalf of a company in formation are jointly and severally liable for their acts, unless:
- The company ratifies the acts after formation
- Ratification releases the founders

**Banking implication**: Banks must verify incorporation status before lending; pre-incorporation loans expose individual founders.

---

## 2. Attributes of Legal Personality

### 2.1 Separate Patrimony

The company owns assets distinct from associates:
- **Asset protection**: Creditors of associates cannot seize company assets
- **Liability shield**: In limited liability companies (SA, SARL, SAS), associates are not liable for company debts

**Exception — Unlimited liability companies**: In SNC and for *commandités* in SCS/SCA, associates remain personally liable despite separate patrimony.

### 2.2 Capacity to Act

**Steichen, Titre 3, Ch. 1**: Legal personality grants:

| Capacity | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Contractual** | Enter agreements in own name |
| **Procedural** | Sue and be sued (*ester en justice*) |
| **Property** | Own, acquire, dispose of assets |
| **Criminal** | Bear criminal liability (since reforms) |

### 2.3 Own Identity

- **Dénomination sociale**: Company name (must be unique in RCS)
- **Siège social**: Registered office (determines nationality, applicable law, jurisdiction)
- **Nationalité**: Luxembourg company if *siège statutaire* in Luxembourg

---

## 3. Limits of Legal Personality

### 3.1 Principle of Speciality (*Spécialité*)

**Art. 1er L1915**: Companies are formed for an *objet social* (corporate purpose) defined in the articles.

**Steichen**: Acts outside the *objet social* may be challenged, though third parties are protected by *théorie de l'apparence*.

### 3.2 Piercing the Corporate Veil

In exceptional cases, courts may disregard legal personality:
- **Fraud**: Use of company to evade personal obligations
- **Confusion of patrimony**: Commingling of personal and company assets
- **Abuse of legal form**: Company as mere facade (*société écran*)

**Luxembourg jurisprudence**: Courts are reluctant to pierce; requires clear abuse.

---

## 4. Loss of Legal Personality

### 4.1 Dissolution

Legal personality does not end immediately upon dissolution:
- Company subsists for liquidation purposes
- Can still sue/be sued for liquidation matters

### 4.2 End of Liquidation

**Art. 151 L1915**: Personality ends upon:
- Completion of liquidation
- Filing of liquidation closure with RCS

**Steichen**: "Dead companies tell no tales" — but liability may survive through personal actions against directors.

---

## Banking & Finance Application

### Due Diligence Checklist

| Verification | Source |
|-------------|--------|
| Company exists | RCS extract (*extrait RCS*) |
| Legal personality acquired | Date of RCS filing |
| Company not dissolved | RCS status |
| Corporate purpose covers transaction | Articles of association |

### Key Risks

1. **Pre-incorporation lending**: Founders personally liable until ratification
2. **Ultra vires acts**: Outside corporate purpose, may be challenged
3. **Dissolved companies**: Cannot enter new contracts
4. **Piercing risk**: Fraud or commingling may expose shareholders
