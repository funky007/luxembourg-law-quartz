# Abus de majorité (Abuse of Majority)

> **Type**: Notion fondamentale
> **Source**: Steichen, *Précis de droit des sociétés*, Titre 3, Chapitre 2
> **Key Legislation**: Arts. 1382-1383 C.civ.; Jurisprudence

---

## Definition

> *Abus de majorité* (abuse of majority) occurs when majority shareholders exercise their voting rights contrary to the company's interest, with the sole purpose of favoring themselves at the expense of minority shareholders.

**Steichen, Titre 3, Ch. 2**: The doctrine limits majority power — the majority may direct the company but may not oppress the minority for personal gain.

---

## 1. Legal Basis

### 1.1 Constitutional Principle

**Steichen**: Shareholders exercise sovereign power through majority voting:
- Majority rule enables efficient governance
- Minority accepts decisions of properly constituted majority
- But majority power is not absolute

### 1.2 Limits on Majority

| Source | Limit |
|--------|-------|
| **Law** | Mandatory provisions |
| **Articles** | Contractual limits |
| **Good faith** | Duty to act loyally |
| **Corporate interest** | Must serve company, not just majority |

---

## 2. Elements of Abuse

### 2.1 Classic Definition

**Steichen**: Abuse requires TWO cumulative elements:

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Contrary to corporate interest** | Decision harms company |
| **Favors majority at minority's expense** | Breaks equality among shareholders |

### 2.2 Both Elements Required

- Decision harming company BUT not favoring majority: **not abuse**
- Decision favoring majority BUT not harming company: **not abuse**
- **Both elements present**: abuse of majority

---

## 3. Examples

### 3.1 Classic Cases of Abuse

| Situation | Why Abusive |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Systematic non-distribution** | Profits retained while majority paid via salaries |
| **Below-market sale** | To majority-controlled buyer |
| **Excessive remuneration** | To majority-directors |
| **Dilutive capital increase** | To exclude minority |
| **Transfer of assets** | To majority's other companies |

### 3.2 What is NOT Abuse

| Situation | Why Not Abusive |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Business judgment** | Legitimate decision even if minority disagrees |
| **Reasonable retention** | Profits reinvested for valid reasons |
| **Market-rate transactions** | Even if with majority |
| **Capital increase** | At fair terms, minority can participate |

---

## 4. Distinction from Related Concepts

### 4.1 Abus de minorité (Abuse of Minority)

**Steichen**: Minority may also abuse their rights:
- Blocking necessary decisions
- Demanding excessive price for consent
- Systematic opposition

| Aspect | Abus de majorité | Abus de minorité |
|--------|------------------|------------------|
| **Who** | Majority shareholders | Minority shareholders |
| **How** | Voting for harmful decisions | Blocking necessary decisions |
| **Remedy** | Nullity, damages | Court may override block |

### 4.2 Abus de biens sociaux

**Distinct concept**: Criminal offense of misusing company assets:
- Personal enrichment at company expense
- Applies to directors/managers
- Criminal sanctions

### 4.3 Abus de droit

**General civil law concept**: Exercise of right for sole purpose of harming another.

---

## 5. Proof and Assessment

### 5.1 Burden of Proof

**Steichen**: Minority shareholder claiming abuse must prove:
- Decision contrary to corporate interest
- Decision favors majority at minority's expense
- Causal link between voting and harm

### 5.2 Assessment Criteria

Courts examine:

| Factor | Analysis |
|--------|----------|
| **Business justification** | Legitimate reason for decision? |
| **Impact on company** | Does it harm company? |
| **Impact on minority** | Are minority rights affected? |
| **Benefit to majority** | Does majority gain personally? |
| **Pattern** | Repeated behavior? |

---

## 6. Remedies

### 6.1 Nullity

**Steichen**: Abusive resolution may be annulled:
- Action by minority shareholder
- Within prescription period
- Court examines circumstances

### 6.2 Damages

Minority may claim damages:
- Based on arts. 1382-1383 C.civ.
- Compensate actual loss
- From majority shareholders personally

### 6.3 Appointment of Expert/Administrator

Court may appoint:
- Expert to investigate
- Provisional administrator
- In serious cases

### 6.4 Judicial Dissolution

**Steichen**: In extreme cases:
- Irreparable breakdown
- No other solution
- *Justes motifs* for dissolution

---

## 7. Prevention

### 7.1 Statutory Protections

Articles may provide:

| Protection | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| **Supermajority** | Require higher majority for certain decisions |
| **Minority rights** | Reserved matters for minority |
| **Board representation** | Minority director |
| **Veto rights** | On specific matters |

### 7.2 Shareholders' Agreements

May include:
- Dividend policy
- Reserved matters
- Exit mechanisms
- Anti-dilution protection

---

## 8. Abuse in Specific Contexts

### 8.1 Dividend Decisions

**Steichen**: Non-distribution may be abusive if:
- Company has distributable profits
- No valid business reason for retention
- Majority compensated through salaries
- Pattern of non-distribution

### 8.2 Capital Increases

May be abusive if:
- Terms unfavorable to minority
- Pre-emption rights improperly excluded
- Dilution purpose

### 8.3 Mergers/Restructurings

May be abusive if:
- Minority squeezed out unfairly
- Exchange ratio disadvantages minority
- No independent valuation

## Banking & Finance Application

### Due Diligence

| Check | Purpose |
|-------|---------|
| **Shareholder structure** | Identify majority/minority |
| **Historical disputes** | Litigation between shareholders |
| **Dividend history** | Pattern of non-distribution |
| **Related-party transactions** | Majority self-dealing |

### Risk Factors

| Situation | Risk |
|-----------|------|
| **Controlling shareholder** | Potential for abuse |
| **Family companies** | Conflicts among branches |
| **JV with unequal stakes** | Minority vulnerability |
| **PE-backed companies** | Tensions on exit |

### Covenant Considerations

May include protections such as:
- Minimum dividend requirements
- Related-party transaction limits
- Change of control provisions
- Minority buyout rights

### Financing Minority-Controlled Entities

Consider:
- Risk of majority decisions affecting borrower
- Upstream guarantee validity
- Cash trap concerns
- Access to information
