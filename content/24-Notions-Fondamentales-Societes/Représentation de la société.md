# Représentation de la société (Corporate Representation)

> **Type**: Notion fondamentale
> **Source**: Steichen, *Précis de droit des sociétés*, Titre 3, Chapitre 3
> **Key Legislation**: Arts. 420-17, 441-7, 500-14, 710-15 L1915

---

## Definition

> *Représentation de la société* (corporate representation) refers to the legal mechanism by which natural persons (directors, managers) act externally on behalf of the company, binding it to third parties through their acts.

**Steichen, Titre 3, Ch. 3**: Representation is the external face of corporate governance — how the company engages with the outside world through its organs.

---

## 1. Principles of Representation

### 1.1 Organ Theory

**Steichen**: Under Luxembourg law, company officers are "organs" (*organes*) — not mere agents:
- Their acts ARE the company's acts
- No separate mandate required
- Company directly bound

### 1.2 Distinguished from Mandate

| Aspect | Organ | Mandataire |
|--------|-------|------------|
| **Source** | Corporate law | Contract |
| **Scope** | Statutory powers | Defined by principal |
| **Termination** | Corporate procedures | Revocation |
| **Third-party protection** | Extensive | Limited |

---

## 2. Who May Represent

### 2.1 By Company Type

| Company | Representative Organ | Article |
|---------|---------------------|---------|
| **SA** | Board of directors (collectivement) or délégué | Art. 441-7 L1915 |
| **SA** (dualist) | Directoire | Art. 442-8 L1915 |
| **SARL** | Gérant(s) | Art. 710-15 L1915 |
| **SNC** | All associates or designated gérant | Art. 200-2 L1915 |
| **SCS** | Commandité(s)/gérant | Art. 310-2 L1915 |
| **SAS** | Président or designated persons | Art. 101-19 L1915 |

### 2.2 Daily Management Delegate

**Art. 441-10 L1915** (SA): Board may delegate daily management to:
- *Délégué à la gestion journalière*
- With representation power for daily matters
- Published at RCS

---

## 3. Scope of Representative Power

### 3.1 General Principle

**Steichen**: Representatives bind company for:

| Scope | Rule |
|-------|------|
| **Within corporate purpose** | Company always bound |
| **Beyond corporate purpose** | Company bound unless third party knew |
| **Beyond internal limits** | Company bound (limits not opposable) |

### 3.2 Third-Party Protection

**Art. 420-17 L1915** (SA):

> Acts of the board bind the company vis-à-vis third parties even if they exceed the corporate purpose, unless the company proves the third party knew or could not have been unaware.

**Key points**:
- Publication of articles ≠ constructive knowledge
- Internal restrictions NOT opposable to third parties
- Good faith third parties protected

### 3.3 Multiple Representatives

| Structure | Rule |
|-----------|------|
| **Each may act alone** | Default for most forms |
| **Joint signature required** | If provided in articles + published |
| **Board collective action** | For certain major decisions |

---

## 4. Formalities

### 4.1 Publication Requirements

**Art. 9 L1915**: Must be published at RCS:
- Identity of representatives
- Extent of powers
- Single or joint signature
- Any special limitations (if opposable)

### 4.2 Effect of Non-Publication

**Steichen**: Unpublished appointments/restrictions:
- Not opposable to third parties
- Unless third party had actual knowledge

### 4.3 Signature Specimens

Practice recommends filing:
- Specimen signatures
- Authentication of authority
- Powers of attorney where used

---

## 5. Limitations on Powers

### 5.1 Internal Limitations

**Steichen**: Articles may impose:

| Type | Effect on Third Parties |
|------|------------------------|
| **Monetary thresholds** | NOT opposable |
| **Category restrictions** | NOT opposable |
| **Approval requirements** | NOT opposable |
| **Joint signature** | Opposable IF published |

### 5.2 Statutory Limitations

Certain acts reserved to shareholders:
- Capital changes
- Merger/demerger
- Amendment of articles
- Dissolution

Representatives cannot bind company for these without proper resolution.

---

## 6. Ultra Vires Acts

### 6.1 Traditional Doctrine

**Steichen**: Traditional *ultra vires* doctrine abolished for capital companies:
- Acts beyond corporate purpose still bind company
- Exception only if third party had knowledge

### 6.2 Modern Position

| Company Type | Ultra Vires Effect |
|--------------|-------------------|
| **SA, SCA, SAS** | Company bound |
| **SARL** | Company bound |
| **SNC, SCS** | More traditional rules may apply |
| **Société civile** | Ultra vires may be raised |

---

## 7. Conflicts of Interest

### 7.1 Self-Dealing

**Steichen**: Representative acting in personal interest:
- May constitute breach of duty
- Transaction may be voidable
- See [[Conventions réglementées]]

### 7.2 Multiple Directorships

Representative serving multiple companies:
- Conflicts must be managed
- Disclosure obligations
- May need to abstain

---

## 8. Powers of Attorney

### 8.1 Delegation

Representatives may grant powers of attorney (*procuration*) for:
- Specific acts
- Categories of acts
- Subject to any restrictions in articles

### 8.2 Requirements

| Requirement | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Authority to delegate** | Must have power to grant |
| **Form** | Written; notarial for certain acts |
| **Scope** | Clearly defined |
| **Termination** | Revocable |

---

## 9. Liability

### 9.1 Representative Personally

**Steichen**: Representative may be personally liable for:

| Ground | To Whom |
|--------|---------|
| **Exceeding powers** | Company |
| **Breach of duty** | Company, shareholders |
| **Fraud** | Third parties |
| **Acting without authority** | Third parties |

### 9.2 Company's Position

If representative exceeds powers:
- Company still bound externally (third-party protection)
- May have recourse against representative
- Unless ratified by competent organ

## Banking & Finance Application

### Verification of Authority

| Check | Source |
|-------|--------|
| **RCS extract** | Current representatives |
| **Articles** | Any signature requirements |
| **Board minutes** | Specific authorization |
| **Powers of attorney** | If signatory not director |

### Documentation Requirements

For major transactions:
- Corporate resolution
- Certificate of incumbency
- Specimen signatures
- Legal opinion on authority

### Common Issues

1. **Joint signature not respected**: Company may deny liability (if published)
2. **Expired authority**: Check currency of appointments
3. **Acting without authority**: May bind individual, not company
4. **Internal approval missing**: Company still bound, but representative liable

### Red Flags

- Recent director changes
- Unusual signing arrangements
- Single signatory for large transactions
- Discrepancy between RCS and documents
