# Responsabilité des dirigeants (Director Liability)

> **Type**: Notion fondamentale
> **Source**: Steichen, *Précis de droit des sociétés*, Titre 3, Chapitre 3
> **Key Legislation**: Arts. 59, 59bis, 192 L1915; Arts. 1382-1383 C.civ.

---

## Definition

> *Responsabilité des dirigeants* encompasses the civil, criminal, and fiscal liability of company directors (*administrateurs*, *gérants*, *membres du directoire*) for their acts and omissions in managing the company.

**Steichen, Titre 3, Ch. 3**: Directors bear multiple layers of accountability:
- To the company itself
- To shareholders
- To third parties (including creditors)
- To the State (tax, criminal)

---

## 1. Types of Director Liability

### 1.1 Liability to the Company (*Responsabilité envers la société*)

**Art. 59 L1915** (SA): Directors are liable to the company for:
- Breach of legal duties
- Violation of articles of association
- Management faults (*fautes de gestion*)

**Nature**: Contractual liability (mandate relationship).

**Action**: *Action sociale* — brought by company or (derivatively) by shareholders.

### 1.2 Liability to Shareholders (*Responsabilité envers les associés*)

**Steichen**: Directors may be liable to individual shareholders for:
- Personal harm distinct from harm to company
- False information affecting investment decisions
- Breach of equality of treatment

**Nature**: Delictual (arts. 1382-1383 C.civ.).

### 1.3 Liability to Third Parties (*Responsabilité envers les tiers*)

**Art. 59bis L1915** (SA): Directors are personally liable to third parties for:
- Faults causing damage distinct from company's non-performance

**Classic example**: Pursuing business when insolvency is evident (*poursuite abusive d'activité*).

---

## 2. Grounds for Liability

### 2.1 Breach of Legal Duties

| Duty | Examples |
|------|----------|
| **Duty of care** | Negligent management decisions |
| **Duty of loyalty** | Self-dealing, conflicts of interest |
| **Duty to act within powers** | *Ultra vires* acts |
| **Statutory duties** | Filing accounts, convening assemblies |

### 2.2 Violation of Articles

- Acting beyond corporate purpose (*objet social*)
- Exceeding limitations on authority
- Non-compliance with internal procedures

### 2.3 Management Faults (*Fautes de gestion*)

**Steichen**: Broad category including:
- Imprudent business decisions
- Failure to supervise
- Inadequate risk management
- Failure to maintain proper accounts

**Standard**: "Bon père de famille" — reasonable and prudent director.

---

## 3. Business Judgment Rule

### 3.1 Principle

**Steichen**: Directors are not liable for mere errors of business judgment if:
- Decision was informed
- No conflict of interest
- Rational basis existed at the time

### 3.2 Luxembourg Approach

**Art. 59 al. 2 L1915**: No liability for "acts of normal management" (*actes de gestion normale*).

**Steichen**: Courts defer to business decisions unless:
- Manifest imprudence
- Conflict of interest
- Legal violation

---

## 4. Specific Liability Situations

### 4.1 Insolvency-Related Liability

**Art. 495 C.com.**: Directors may face *action en comblement de passif* (contribution to liabilities) if:
- Management fault contributed to insolvency
- Pursued business with no reasonable prospect of recovery

**Steichen**: Common grounds:
- Delaying bankruptcy filing
- Incurring new debts when insolvent
- Preferential payments to related parties

### 4.2 Tax Liability

**Art. 103-2 LGIR**: Directors personally liable for company's unpaid taxes if:
- Caused by their fault
- Recovery from company impossible

### 4.3 Criminal Liability

Directors may face criminal prosecution for:
- *Abus de biens sociaux* (misuse of company assets)
- *Abus de confiance* (breach of trust)
- Fraud, false accounts
- Bankruptcy offences

---

## 5. Defenses and Limitations

### 5.1 Exculpatory Provisions

**Art. 59 al. 3 L1915**: Directors not liable if:
- They opposed the decision
- They reported disagreement in writing

### 5.2 Ratification by Shareholders

**Steichen**: Shareholders may ratify certain acts, but cannot:
- Ratify illegal acts
- Prejudice third-party rights
- Discharge liability for manifest fault

### 5.3 Limitation Period

**Art. 59 L1915**: Action against directors prescribes after **5 years** from the act or, if concealed, from discovery.

### 5.4 D&O Insurance

**Steichen**: Common practice to maintain directors' and officers' liability insurance (*assurance responsabilité des dirigeants*).

**Limits**: Coverage typically excludes:
- Intentional misconduct
- Criminal acts
- Personal benefits

---

## 6. Joint and Several Liability

### 6.1 Principle

**Art. 59 L1915**: Board members are jointly and severally liable (*solidairement responsables*) for collective decisions.

### 6.2 Individual Escape

Directors may escape joint liability if:
- No personal fault
- Opposed the decision
- Reported to next assembly

## Banking & Finance Application

### Due Diligence on Directors

| Check | Purpose |
|-------|---------|
| **Identity and capacity** | Authorized to act |
| **Disqualifications** | Not barred from directorship |
| **Conflicts of interest** | Related party transactions |
| **Past bankruptcies** | Pattern of failed companies |

### Director Guarantees

**Link to [[Caution dirigeante]]**: Banks often require personal guarantees from directors.

**Considerations**:
- Director's personal net worth
- Existing liabilities (other guarantees)
- Proportionality requirements

### Lending to Distressed Companies

**Warning signs**:
- Director resignations (may signal foreseen insolvency)
- Delays in filing accounts
- Related party transactions increasing
- Capital below legal minimum

### Security over Directors' Shares

When taking pledge over director's shares:
- Verify no liability claims pending
- Assess risk that shares may be worthless if liability materializes
