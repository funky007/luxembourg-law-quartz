# Affectio societatis (Will to Associate)

> **Type**: Notion fondamentale
> **Source**: Steichen, *Précis de droit des sociétés*, Titre 2, Chapitre 2, Section 2
> **Key Legislation**: Art. 1832 Code civil

---

## Definition

> *Affectio societatis* is the subjective element of the company contract — the common intention of associates to collaborate on equal footing in pursuit of a shared objective, accepting the risks and benefits of the venture.

**Steichen, Titre 2, Ch. 2**: This intentional element distinguishes a true company from other legal relationships (employment, loan, sale).

---

## 1. Elements of Affectio Societatis

### 1.1 Core Components

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Common purpose** | Intention to pursue shared objective |
| **Collaborative spirit** | Work together, not subordination |
| **Equality of status** | Associates, not employer-employee |
| **Risk sharing** | Accept potential losses |
| **Profit motive** | Share in gains (or savings) |

### 1.2 Distinguished from Other Relationships

**Steichen**: *Affectio societatis* is absent where:

| Relationship | Missing Element |
|-------------|-----------------|
| **Employment** | Subordination exists (lien de subordination) |
| **Loan** | Lender does not share in losses |
| **Indivision** | No active collaboration |
| **Simple co-ownership** | Passive holding, no common enterprise |

---

## 2. Legal Functions

### 2.1 Formation Requirement

**Art. 1832 C.civ.**: *Affectio societatis* is essential for valid company formation.

**Consequence**: Absence may lead to:
- **Nullity** of the company contract
- **Requalification** as another legal relationship

### 2.2 Ongoing Requirement

*Affectio societatis* must persist throughout company life:

**Steichen**: Its disappearance (*disparition de l'affectio societatis*) is a recognized ground for:
- Judicial dissolution (*dissolution judiciaire*)
- Forced exit of an associate (*exclusion*)
- Voluntary withdrawal (*retrait*)

---

## 3. Proof and Assessment

### 3.1 Objective Indicators

Courts look for concrete evidence:
- **Participation in decisions**: Attending assemblies, voting
- **Contribution to management**: Active involvement
- **Risk assumption**: Exposure to losses
- **Sharing of profits/losses**: Actual distribution

### 3.2 Problematic Situations

| Situation | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Sleeping partner** | May lack *affectio societatis* |
| **99%/1% split** | Form may mask loan or employment |
| **No participation** | Suggests absence of collaborative intent |
| **Fixed return** | Resembles loan, not true partnership |

---

## 4. Specific Applications

### 4.1 Société Civile (SC)

**Steichen**: *Affectio societatis* is particularly important in *sociétés civiles* where personal collaboration is often the essence.

**Example**: Family property companies (*SCI familiales*) — courts examine whether members truly collaborate or merely hold assets.

### 4.2 SNC (General Partnership)

Strong *affectio societatis* presumed due to:
- Unlimited personal liability
- *Intuitu personae* character
- Unanimous decision-making (often)

### 4.3 SA (Stock Corporation)

**Steichen**: *Affectio societatis* is attenuated in SA:
- Shareholders may be passive investors
- No personal relationship required
- Transfer of shares unrestricted

**Debate**: Some authors question whether *affectio societatis* applies to SA at all.

---

## 5. Disappearance of Affectio Societatis

### 5.1 Grounds for Dissolution

**Art. 1871 C.civ.** (applicable by analogy): A company may be dissolved for *justes motifs* (just cause), including:
- Serious and persistent discord between associates
- Total breakdown of trust
- Impossibility of continuing collaboration

### 5.2 Jurisprudential Criteria

**Steichen**: Courts require:
- **Gravity**: Not mere disagreements
- **Persistence**: Not temporary disputes
- **Attribution**: Often examines fault

## Banking & Finance Application

### Due Diligence Considerations

| Issue | Banking Implication |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Requalification risk** | If company requalified as loan, security structure fails |
| **Dissolution risk** | Conflict between associates may threaten borrower |
| **Control questions** | Who truly directs the company? |

### Red Flags

- Associate with guaranteed return (= disguised loan)
- No participation in decisions despite significant stake
- Fixed payments regardless of profits
- Single associate doing all work (= employment?)

### Structuring Considerations

In acquisition finance and joint ventures:
- Document collaborative decision-making procedures
- Ensure genuine risk sharing
- Avoid arrangements that look like disguised loans
