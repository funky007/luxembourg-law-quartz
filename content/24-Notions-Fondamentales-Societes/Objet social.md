# Objet social (Corporate Purpose)

> **Type**: Notion fondamentale
> **Source**: Steichen, *Précis de droit des sociétés*, Titre 2, Chapitre 3
> **Key Legislation**: Arts. 2, 100, 420-1 L1915; Art. 1833 C.civ.

---

## Definition

> The *objet social* (corporate purpose or objects clause) defines the activities that the company is authorized to undertake. It delimits the scope of the company's capacity and the extent of directors' powers vis-à-vis third parties.

**Steichen, Titre 2, Ch. 3**: The corporate purpose (*objet social*) is a mandatory clause in the articles of association that describes the company's authorized activities.

---

## 1. Functions of Corporate Purpose

### 1.1 Internal Function

**Steichen**: The corporate purpose serves to:

| Function | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Define activities** | Specifies what the company can do |
| **Limit directors' powers** | Acts outside object may expose directors to liability |
| **Inform shareholders** | Shareholders know the business they invested in |

### 1.2 External Function (Third-Party Protection)

**Art. 420-1 L1915** (SA): Acts beyond corporate purpose bind the company unless:
- Third party knew act exceeded the purpose
- Third party could not have been unaware given circumstances

**Steichen**: Mere publication of articles does NOT constitute constructive knowledge of corporate purpose limitations.

---

## 2. Drafting the Corporate Purpose

### 2.1 Requirements

| Requirement | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Specificity** | Must be sufficiently precise |
| **Lawfulness** | Must not be contrary to law or public policy |
| **Possibility** | Must be achievable |
| **Commercial character** | For commercial companies (except société civile) |

### 2.2 Common Formulations

**Steichen**: Practice has developed various approaches:

| Approach | Description | Risk |
|----------|-------------|------|
| **Restrictive** | Narrow, specific activities | May require frequent amendments |
| **Broad** | Wide range of activities | Less protection for shareholders |
| **Catch-all clause** | "All commercial activities" | May be challenged as too vague |

### 2.3 Standard Clause Structure

Typical corporate purpose includes:
1. **Primary activities** — Core business
2. **Ancillary activities** — Supporting activities
3. **Extension clause** — Related activities
4. **General clause** — Any activities promoting corporate interest

---

## 3. Legal Effects

### 3.1 Ultra Vires Doctrine

**Steichen**: Luxembourg has largely abolished the ultra vires doctrine for capital companies:

| Company Type | Effect of Ultra Vires Acts |
|--------------|---------------------------|
| **SA, SCA, SAS** | Company bound unless third party had knowledge |
| **SARL** | Company bound unless third party had knowledge |
| **SNC, SCS** | Traditional ultra vires rules may apply |
| **Société civile** | Ultra vires acts may not bind company |

### 3.2 Director Liability

Even where company is bound, directors may face:
- **Liability to company** — For damages caused
- **Revocation** — For exceeding powers
- **No discharge** — Assembly may refuse quitus

### 3.3 Internal vs External Effects

| Sphere | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| **External** | Company generally bound vis-à-vis good faith third parties |
| **Internal** | Directors liable for exceeding purpose |

---

## 4. Modification of Corporate Purpose

### 4.1 Procedure

**Art. 67-1 L1915** (SA): Modification requires:

| Step | Requirement |
|------|-------------|
| **Decision** | EGM (extraordinary general meeting) |
| **Quorum** | 50% of capital (first meeting) |
| **Majority** | 2/3 of votes cast |
| **Form** | Notarial deed |
| **Publication** | RCS registration and RESA publication |

### 4.2 SARL

**Art. 189 L1915**: Modification requires:
- 3/4 majority of capital
- Notarial deed
- RCS filing

### 4.3 Limitations

Certain changes may require:
- **Unanimous consent** — Fundamental changes affecting shareholders' rights
- **Special procedures** — Regulatory approvals for certain activities

---

## 5. Corporate Purpose and Interest

### 5.1 Intérêt Social (Corporate Interest)

**Steichen**: The *intérêt social* is related but distinct from *objet social*:

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Objet social** | What company CAN do |
| **Intérêt social** | What company SHOULD do (for whose benefit) |

### 5.2 Theories of Corporate Interest

| Theory | Perspective |
|--------|-------------|
| **Shareholder primacy** | Interest of shareholders |
| **Enterprise** | Interest of company as entity |
| **Stakeholder** | Broader interests (employees, creditors, etc.) |

**Steichen**: Luxembourg leans toward enterprise theory — directors must act in company's interest, not merely shareholders'.

---

## 6. Special Applications

### 6.1 Regulated Activities

Certain activities require:

| Activity | Requirement |
|----------|-------------|
| **Banking** | CSSF authorization |
| **Insurance** | CAA authorization |
| **Investment funds** | CSSF authorization |
| **Legal services** | Professional qualification |

**Consequence**: Corporate purpose must be consistent with authorizations held.

### 6.2 Holding Companies

Common formulation for holding companies:
- Acquisition, holding, management of participations
- Financing of subsidiaries
- All related activities

## Banking & Finance Application

### Due Diligence on Corporate Purpose

| Check | Purpose |
|-------|---------|
| **Scope of activities** | Company authorized for intended transaction |
| **Lending/borrowing** | Expressly permitted in objects |
| **Guarantees** | Authority to provide security |
| **Investments** | Authority to make investments |

### Common Issues

1. **Guarantee capacity**: Objects clause silent on guarantees
2. **Upstream guarantees**: May exceed purpose if no group interest
3. **Financial assistance**: Objects cannot authorize prohibited acts
4. **Regulatory scope**: Activities must match licenses held

### Red Flags

- Very narrow corporate purpose (may not cover transaction)
- Absence of financing/guarantee authorization
- Mismatch between stated purpose and actual activities
- Recent change of purpose without clear business rationale

### Corporate Benefit Link

**See [[Corporate benefit]]**: When assessing guarantee capacity, consider:
- Whether guarantee falls within corporate purpose
- Whether transaction benefits company (intérêt social)
- Whether adequate corporate governance followed
