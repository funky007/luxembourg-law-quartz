# Financial Collateral Arrangement (Contrat de garantie financière)

> **Type**: Special pledge regime / Real security
> **Source**: Westendorf, T.1, §401-539
> **Key Legislation**: Loi du 5 août 2005 sur les contrats de garantie financière; Directive 2002/47/CE
> **Key Cases**: C.J.U.E., 10/11/2016, C-156/15; Trib. Lux. 20/05/2010; CA 22/01/2020
> **Related**: [[11 - Gage civil (droit commun)]], [[12 - Gage sur créances]], [[Financial collateral (loi 5 août 2005)]]

---

## Overview

The *contrat de garantie financière* (financial collateral arrangement) is a special pledge regime introduced by the **loi du 5 août 2005**, transposing Directive 2002/47/CE. It provides enhanced protection for secured creditors, particularly in insolvency situations, through simplified formalities, self-help enforcement, and immunity from insolvency claw-back rules.

**Westendorf, T.1, §401**:
> The loi du 5 août 2005 origins lie in the need to provide efficient, reliable security mechanisms for financial market participants, protecting them against the risks inherent in counterparty default.

**Practical importance**: The CGF regime is fundamental in:
- LBO financing structures
- Derivatives collateral (ISDA Credit Support Annexes)
- Securities lending
- Bank account pledges
- Intercompany financing

---

## Legal Framework

### Directive 2002/47/CE

The directive establishes a harmonized EU framework for financial collateral, prioritizing:
1. **Rapid enforcement** without judicial intervention
2. **Protection in insolvency** against claw-back
3. **Simplified formalities** for constitution and proof

### Loi du 5 août 2005

**Westendorf, T.1, §402**:
The law creates a special regime departing from civil code principles where necessary, but the civil code rules apply residually where the law does not derogate.

**Key principle** (Westendorf, T.1, §408):
> The loi de 2005 does not exclude droit commun principles; it only modifies them where specifically provided.

## Scope of Application

### Secured Obligations (*Obligations financières couvertes*)

**Art. 1(3) loi 5 août 2005**: The secured obligations must be "*obligations financières couvertes*" — financial obligations.

**Westendorf, T.1, §413-418**:

The concept is broadly defined to include:
- Obligations giving rise to payment of money or delivery of instruments
- Present, future, actual, conditional, or potential obligations
- Obligations owed to the collateral taker or third parties
- Obligations arising from master agreements (ISDA, GMRA, etc.)

| Included | Excluded |
|----------|----------|
| Loan repayment obligations | Purely non-financial obligations |
| Derivatives payment obligations | Maintenance/alimony claims |
| Guarantee/indemnity obligations | Obligations not susceptible of monetary expression |
| Close-out netting amounts | |

### Object: *Avoirs* (Collateral Assets)

**Art. 1(4) loi 5 août 2005**: The collateral must consist of "*avoirs*" — financial instruments or claims for money.

**Westendorf, T.1, §420-427**:

#### Financial Instruments (*Instruments financiers*)

Broadly defined to include:
- Shares (SA, SCA, SàRL, SCS, SCSp)
- Bonds and debt securities
- Units in collective investment schemes
- Any negotiable securities
- Dematerialized securities
- Book-entry securities

#### Claims (*Créances*)

**Westendorf, T.1, §425-427**:
Claims for money qualify as *avoirs*, including:
- Bank account balances
- Intercompany loan receivables
- Trade receivables (subject to debate)
- Insurance claims (where monetary)

### Future Assets

**Art. 4, 13 loi 5 août 2005**:

> **Westendorf, T.1, §428-431**: Future claims and instruments can be pledged. They are deemed given in guarantee from the moment of their coming into existence.

This permits:
- Global pledges over fluctuating pools of assets
- Security over after-acquired assets
- Pledges over future receivables

## Parties

### Beneficiary (*Preneur de garantie*)

**Art. 1(8) loi 5 août 2005**:

No personal scope limitation — any person can be a beneficiary. The law applies regardless of whether the beneficiary is a regulated entity.

### Grantor (*Constituant*)

**Art. 1(9) loi 5 août 2005**:

**Westendorf, T.1, §432-440**:

Similarly unrestricted. However, specific issues arise:
- **Corporate benefit**: Upstream/cross-stream guarantees require analysis
- **Presumption of ownership**: The grantor is presumed to own the pledged assets (Art. 7)

#### Presumption of Ownership

**Art. 7 loi 5 août 2005**:
> "[L]e preneur de la garantie n'a pas à vérifier, avant ou après la constitution du gage, si le constituant est propriétaire des avoirs nantis [...]"

*Translation*: "The collateral taker need not verify, before or after constitution of the pledge, whether the grantor owns the pledged assets."

**Westendorf, T.1, §438-440**:
This presumption protects the pledgee who acts in good faith, but does not validate a pledge of assets the grantor does not own.

### Waiver of Recourse

**Westendorf, T.1, §441-446**:

A grantor may waive recourse rights against the debtor in group financing contexts. Such waiver is not a gift (*libéralité*) where the grantor has an interest in the financing structure.

## Formation: Possession or Control

### The Dispossession Requirement

**Art. 5 loi 5 août 2005**:

**Westendorf, T.1, §448-452**:

> "Une garantie financière est constituée par sa livraison, son transfert, sa détention, son enregistrement ou tout autre traitement ayant pour effet que le preneur de la garantie [...] acquiert la possession ou le contrôle de cette garantie financière."

The pledge is constituted by any mechanism giving the pledgee **possession or control**.

### The Concept of Control

**Westendorf, T.1, §451**:

The notion of "control" (*contrôle*) derives from UCC Article 8-106 and Directive 2002/47/CE as modified by Directive 2009/44/CE.

**C.J.U.E., 10 novembre 2016, C-156/15** (Private Equity Insurance Group):
> The directive requires "negative control" — meaning the grantor cannot dispose of the assets without the pledgee's involvement.

Control may be:
- **Negative**: Grantor cannot act without pledgee consent
- **Positive**: Pledgee can act without grantor consent
- **Legal**: Control arising from contract terms

### Modes of Constitution by Asset Type

**Art. 5(2) loi 5 août 2005**:

| Asset Type | Mode of Dispossession |
|------------|----------------------|
| Book-entry securities (pledgee is depositary) | Contract conclusion alone |
| Book-entry securities (third-party depositary) | Control agreement or notation |
| Bearer instruments | Physical delivery or immobilization |
| Registered instruments | Inscription in register |
| Order instruments | Pignorative endorsement |
| Other instruments | Notification to/acceptance by issuer |
| Claims | Contract conclusion (since 2011 reform) |

### Pledges over Claims: Simplified Formalities

**Art. 5(4) loi 5 août 2005** (as amended):

**Westendorf, T.1, §460-461**:

> Dispossession of claims is achieved "by the conclusion of the pledge contract" — notification to the debtor is no longer required for validity or third-party effectiveness.

**Practical consequence**: The pledgee should still notify the debtor to:
- Prevent valid payment to the grantor
- Cut off defenses/set-off rights
- Secure priority against subsequent pledgees

**Luxembourg jurisprudence**: Trib. Luxembourg, 15 octobre 2021:
> "Le contrat de gage n'est pas nul en raison de l'absence de dépossession, mais il y a lieu de déclarer le contrat de gage inopposable au créancier saisissant."

## Third-Party Effectiveness (*Opposabilité*)

**Art. 5(6) loi 5 août 2005**:

> "Le rang des gages est déterminé par rapport à la date où ils ont été rendus opposables aux tiers."

**Westendorf, T.1, §475**:

Priority is determined by the date of third-party effectiveness, not by the date of contract conclusion. This follows the *prior tempore* principle.

**Practical implication**: Complete all perfection formalities promptly to secure priority.

---

## Effects Before Maturity

### Right of Utilization (*Droit d'utilisation*)

**Art. 10 loi 5 août 2005**:

**Westendorf, T.1, §495-505**:

> The parties may agree that the pledgee has a "right of utilization" (*droit d'utilisation*) — defined as "the right to dispose of the pledged assets as if he were owner."

This remarkable provision allows the pledgee to:
- Use the pledged assets
- **Alienate** them to third parties
- Pledge them further (rehypothecation)

**Legal nature**: Not ownership transfer, but a right to act "as if" owner.

> **Westendorf, T.1, §502**: If the pledgee uses the right of utilization, "the property passes directly from the grantor to the third party acquirer, without transiting through the pledgee's patrimony."

**Counterparty protection** (Art. 10(2)):
The pledgee must provide equivalent collateral by the date for performance of the secured obligations.

### Fruits, Products, and Capital

**Art. 8 loi 5 août 2005**:

**Westendorf, T.1, §506**:

> "Sauf convention contraire, le créancier perçoit aux échéances les capitaux et, s'il y a lieu, les fruits et les produits des biens donnés en gage, et soit les impute sur sa créance soit les conserve comme avoirs nantis en sa faveur."

The pledgee may:
1. Collect interest, dividends, and capital repayments
2. Impute collections on the secured debt, OR
3. Hold collections as additional collateral

### Voting Rights and Other Rights

**Art. 9 loi 5 août 2005**:

**Westendorf, T.1, §507-509**:

> "L'attribution de l'exercice des droits attachés aux instruments financiers nantis est régie par la convention des parties."

The parties freely determine who exercises:
- Voting rights
- Attendance at meetings
- Information rights
- Other membership rights

**Default rule**:
- If no *droit d'utilisation*: grantor exercises rights
- If *droit d'utilisation*: pledgee exercises rights

### Right of Retention

**Art. 5(5) loi 5 août 2005**:

**Westendorf, T.1, §510-511**:

> "Le créancier gagiste dispose dans tous les cas d'un droit de rétention sur les avoirs nantis en sa faveur."

The pledgee has a right of retention even where dispossession is fictive.

## Enforcement (*Réalisation*)

### Triggering Events

**Art. 1(6) loi 5 août 2005**:

**Westendorf, T.1, §515**:

> "*Fait entraînant l'exécution de la garantie*" means "a default or any other event agreed between the parties, whose occurrence [...] entitles the collateral taker to realize or appropriate the financial collateral."

**Key innovation**: Enforcement can be triggered by events **other than** maturity of the secured debt — e.g., breach of financial covenants.

**Luxembourg jurisprudence**: Trib. Luxembourg, 12 juillet 2017:
> "Le concept de 'tout autre évènement convenu entre les parties' constitue un cas de réalisation autonome rompant avec l'exigence traditionnelle de l'exigibilité de la dette garantie."

**Cour d'appel, 22 janvier 2020**: This possibility does not destroy the accessory character of the pledge.

### No Prior Formalities

**Art. 11 loi 5 août 2005**:

**Westendorf, T.1, §517**:

Enforcement may proceed:
- Without prior demand (*mise en demeure*)
- Without court authorization
- Without specifying the triggering event

> **Cour d'appel, 8 février 2012**: "Une des innovations de la directive précitée est que l'exigence de la mise en demeure avant l'exécution du gage est interdite."

### Modes of Enforcement

**Art. 11(1) loi 5 août 2005**:

| Mode | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Appropriation** | Pledgee acquires ownership (Art. 11(1)(a)) |
| **Private sale** | Sale de gré à gré at "normal commercial terms" (Art. 11(1)(b)) |
| **Public sale** | Auction (Art. 11(2)) |
| **Set-off** | Compensation under Part V of the law (Art. 11(1)(d)) |
| **Judicial attribution** | Court-ordered transfer (Art. 11(1)(c)) |

#### Appropriation (*Pacte commissoire*)

**Westendorf, T.1, §536-539**:

The pledgee may appropriate the collateral, becoming owner, with obligation to account for any surplus value.

Appropriation may be:
- By the pledgee directly
- By a third party designated by the pledgee (e.g., SPV)

#### Private Sale

**Westendorf, T.1, §530-534**:

Must be at **"conditions commerciales normales"** (normal commercial terms).

**Trib. Luxembourg, 20 mai 2010** (defining the concept):
> "[L]es conditions commerciales normales correspondent à des conditions résultant du libre jeu de l'offre et de la demande, au moment de la réalisation du gage et en tenant compte des informations disponibles à cette époque, de la nature et des particularités concrètes des actifs concernés et des conditions du marché applicable."

**Sanction for breach**: Damages, not nullity of the sale.

### Enforcement Liability (See also [[Enforcement - Banker Liability]])

**Key jurisprudential principle**: Improper enforcement results in **damages, not nullity**.

**Case**: CA, 16 mai 2018, n° 39.827 — Violation of "conditions commerciales normales" → damages only; Art. 20(4) protects execution contracts; fraus omnia corrumpit inapplicable as subsidiary principle.

**Case**: Cour de cassation, 14 février 2019 — Where enforcement event results from abuse/fraud, judge may order restitution despite Art. 20(4).

**Limits on enforcement**:
- Good faith (Art. 1134 al. 3 C.civ.)
- Abuse of rights (Art. 6-1 C.civ.)
- Proportionality (accessory nature limits enforcement to secured obligation)

**Source**: P. Geortay, "Le caractère accessoire du gage et la loi sur les contrats de garantie financière", Strada Lex 2014, pp. 1271-1292

#### Set-Off (*Compensation*)

**Art. 11(1)(d), Arts. 18-19 loi 5 août 2005**:

**Westendorf, T.1, §520-523**:

Bilateral and multilateral close-out netting clauses are enforceable, even in insolvency.

## Protection in Insolvency

### Core Protection

**Art. 20(4) loi 5 août 2005**:

**Westendorf, T.1, §491**:

> "Les dispositions du Livre III, Titre XVII du Code civil, du Livre 1er Titre VIII et du Livre III du Code de commerce [...] ne font pas obstacle à l'exécution" des contrats de garantie financière.

The bankruptcy rules do not prevent enforcement of financial collateral arrangements.

### Immunity from Claw-Back

**Art. 20(1)-(3) loi 5 août 2005**:

Financial collateral arrangements are valid and enforceable notwithstanding:
- Suspect period rules (*période suspecte*)
- Fraudulent preference actions
- Insolvency moratorium

**Westendorf, T.1, §434-435**:

This protection extends to top-up collateral provided during the suspect period.

---

## Subordinate Pledges (*Gages de rang subséquent*)

**Art. 6 loi 5 août 2005**:

**Westendorf, T.1, §477-490**:

Multiple pledges over the same assets are permitted. Requirements:
1. Prior-ranking pledgees must consent
2. Third-party custodian must be informed
3. Subordinate pledgee cannot have greater rights than senior

**Special rule** (Art. 6(3)): If senior pledgee has *droit d'utilisation*, subordinate pledges cannot be granted without its consent.

---

> **Common Law Comparison**
>
> **English law**: The Financial Collateral Arrangements Regulations 2003 implement Directive 2002/47/CE. Key features:
> - Disapplies certain formality requirements
> - Permits appropriation and close-out netting
> - Excludes floating charges unless "in possession or under control"
>
> **UCC Article 9**: Security interests in investment property require "control" for perfection (§9-314). Control agreement with intermediary is standard. Rehypothecation rights depend on agreement under §9-207.
>
> The Luxembourg *droit d'utilisation* is comparable to UCC "repledge" rights and English "right of use."

## Banking Application

### Structuring Financial Collateral

**For banks taking security over financial assets**:

1. **Confirm CGF applicability**:
   - Assets qualify as *avoirs*
   - Obligations qualify as *obligations financières couvertes*

2. **Perfect the security**:
   - Book-entry securities: Control agreement with depositary
   - Registered securities: Register inscription
   - Claims: Document the pledge (notification recommended)

3. **Consider enforcement mechanics**:
   - Define triggering events clearly
   - Include appropriation and private sale rights
   - Reserve *droit d'utilisation* if required

4. **Document comprehensively**:
   - Identify secured obligations (present and future)
   - Describe collateral pool (including future assets)
   - Specify enforcement procedures

### Due Diligence Checklist

- [ ] Verify grantor's ownership/right to pledge
- [ ] Confirm no prior pledges or adverse claims
- [ ] Execute control agreements with depositaries
- [ ] Register pledge on share registers
- [ ] Consider notification to debtors of pledged claims
- [ ] Define enforcement triggers and procedures
- [ ] Address *droit d'utilisation* requirements
- [ ] Consider intercreditor arrangements for subordinate pledges
