# Pledge over Receivables (Gage sur créances)

> **Type**: Real security / Pledge over incorporeal movables
> **Source**: Westendorf, T.1, §146-150, §190-191
> **Key Articles**: Arts. 2074-2075, 2081 C.civ.; Loi 21 décembre 1994
> **Key Cases**: CA 13/10/1993, nos 14.839-14.840; Trib. Lux. 03/03/2010
> **Related**: [[11 - Gage civil (droit commun)]], [[13 - Contrat de garantie financière]], [[Dépossession]]

---

## Overview

The pledge over receivables (*gage sur créances*) is a pledge having as its object an incorporeal movable — specifically, a claim (*créance*) held by the grantor against a third-party debtor. It follows the general rules of civil pledge, with specific adaptations for the intangible nature of claims.

**Practical importance**: Pledges over receivables are fundamental in:
- Account pledges (cash balances)
- Receivables financing
- Intercompany loan security
- Security over insurance claims

---

## Legal Framework

### Civil Code Provisions

> **Art. 2074, al. 2, C.civ.**: "Le gage sur les meubles incorporels tels que les créances, les droits et les actions sur un tiers n'est opposable aux tiers que par la notification en faite au débiteur du meuble incorporel donné en gage ou par l'acceptation faite par ce débiteur."

*Translation*: "The pledge over incorporeal movables such as claims, rights and actions against a third party is only opposable to third parties by notification to the debtor of the incorporeal movable pledged or by acceptance by that debtor."

### Loi du 21 décembre 1994

The law of 21 December 1994 on *cession de créances et nantissement de créances* modernized the regime for pledges over claims by:

1. **Confirming pledgeability of future claims** — Art. 2075 C.civ. expressly permits
2. **Aligning cession and pledge regimes** — Similar rules for both
3. **Simplified notification** — Streamlined procedures

## Object: Claims as Pledgeable Assets

### Which Claims May Be Pledged?

**Westendorf, T.1, §146-149**:

Any claim that is:
- **Assignable** (*cessible*) — Not subject to inalienability
- **Transferable** — Not strictly personal (*intuitu personae*)
- **Seizable** (*saisissable*) — Not exempt from seizure

| Pledgeable | Not Pledgeable |
|------------|----------------|
| Trade receivables | Maintenance claims (*aliments*) |
| Loan receivables | Salary (below quota) |
| Insurance claims | Strictly personal rights |
| Bank account balances | Claims with valid non-assignment clause |
| Intercompany loans | |

### Future Claims

> **Art. 2075 C.civ.** (as amended by loi 21 décembre 1994): The pledge may be granted over future claims, provided they are sufficiently identifiable.

**Westendorf, T.1, §60**:

Pledge over future claims requires:
- Identification of the claim's source or category
- Specification of the parties involved
- No need for precise quantification at constitution

## Formation: Dispossession of Claims

### The Problem

Incorporeal movables cannot be physically delivered. For claims, *dépossession* must occur through a legal mechanism equivalent to physical delivery.

### Notification or Acceptance

**Westendorf, T.1, §146-148**:

Dispossession of claims requires either:

1. **Notification** (*signification*) to the debtor of the pledged claim
2. **Acceptance** by that debtor

**Luxembourg jurisprudence**: Cour d'appel, 13 octobre 1993, nos 14.839 et 14.840 du rôle:
> "Conformément aux doctrines de jurisprudences récentes, il est suffisant, pour ce qui est des créances non constatées par un titre, de signifier la mise en gage au tiers débiteur pour mettre le créancier en possession."

*Translation*: "In accordance with recent doctrines and case law, for claims not evidenced by a title, it is sufficient to notify the third-party debtor of the pledge in order to put the creditor in possession."

### Formal Requirements

**Art. 2074, al. 1, C.civ.**: Written instrument required, specifying:
- The pledged claim(s)
- The secured obligation(s)
- Identity of debtor of pledged claim

### Third-Party Opposability

| Without Notification | With Notification |
|----------------------|-------------------|
| Pledge valid inter partes | Pledge opposable to third parties |
| Debtor may pay grantor | Debtor must pay pledgee |
| Other creditors may seize | Pledgee has priority |

**Key principle**: The pledgee's rights against the debtor of the pledged claim only become effective upon notification or acceptance.

## Effects

### Rights of the Pledgee

#### 1. Right of Retention

The pledgee has a right of retention (*droit de rétention*) preventing release of the claim until the secured debt is paid.

**Westendorf, T.1, §204**:
For claims, the right of retention operates through the pledgee's entitlement to receive payment from the third-party debtor.

#### 2. Right of Preference

> **Art. 2073 C.civ.**: The pledgee is paid from the proceeds "par privilège et préférence aux autres créanciers."

#### 3. Collection Rights

When the pledged claim matures before the secured claim:

**Westendorf, T.1, §190-191**:

> **Art. 2081, al. 1, C.civ.**: If the pledged claim bears interest, the pledgee may collect such interest, provided they are imputed on amounts owed.

**Problem**: Can the pledgee collect the principal of the pledged claim when it matures before the secured claim?

**Traditional view** (Westendorf, T.1, §191): The pledgee cannot collect principal — only the grantor, as creditor of the pledged claim, has standing. But the grantor cannot collect either, having dispossessed himself.

**Solutions**:
1. **Consignment** — Parties agree to deposit collected amounts
2. **Mandate** — Grantor authorizes pledgee to collect for consignment
3. **Conditional delegation** — Upon maturity of secured claim

**⚠️ To Be Independently Verified**: The precise scope of collection rights remains debated in doctrine.

### Obligations of the Pledgee

1. **Conservation** — Preserve the value of the pledged claim
2. **No unauthorized disposal** — Cannot assign without right
3. **Accounting** — Account for interest collected (Art. 2081)
4. **Restitution** — Return claim upon extinction of secured debt

### Interest and Fruits

> **Art. 2081 C.civ.**: "[S]i elles portent intérêts, le créancier les impute sur ceux qui lui sont dus."

*Translation*: "If [the pledged claims] bear interest, the creditor imputes them on amounts owed to him."

**Westendorf, T.1, §190**:

| Type of Claim | Rule |
|---------------|------|
| Interest-bearing claim | Pledgee collects interest, imputes on secured debt |
| Non-interest-bearing | No fruits to collect |
| Dividends on securities | Subject to CGF regime (loi 5 août 2005) |

## Interaction with Financial Collateral Regime

### When CGF Applies

The **loi du 5 août 2005** on financial collateral arrangements (*contrats de garantie financière*) applies to pledges over claims when:

1. The pledged claims constitute **"avoirs"** under Art. 1 of the law
2. The secured obligations are **"obligations financières couvertes"**

**Westendorf, T.1, §401**:
> In Luxembourg, virtually all bank account pledges fall under the CGF regime, as standard banking conditions typically include a general pledge over all client accounts.

### Advantages of CGF Regime

| Civil Code Pledge | CGF Pledge |
|-------------------|------------|
| Court approval for enforcement | Self-help enforcement permitted |
| Auction or judicial attribution | Appropriation, sale, set-off |
| Subject to stay in insolvency | Insolvency-proof |
| Complex formalities | Simplified requirements |

> See [[13 - Contrat de garantie financière]] for detailed analysis.

## Enforcement

### Under Civil Code

**Art. 2078 C.civ.**: Enforcement requires court authorization for:
- Public sale (*vente aux enchères*)
- Judicial attribution (*attribution judiciaire*)

**For claims**: Enforcement typically involves:
1. Collection of the pledged claim
2. Application of proceeds to secured debt
3. Return of surplus to grantor

### Under CGF Regime

**Art. 11, loi 5 août 2005**: Self-help remedies available:
- **Appropriation** — Direct acquisition at fair value
- **Sale** — Private sale at commercial terms
- **Set-off** — Compensation with secured debt

## Special Cases

### Bank Account Pledges

**Westendorf, T.1, §401, note 1197**:

Bank account balances constitute claims against the bank. Standard banking conditions typically include:
- General pledge over all client accounts
- Right to set off and retain

**Luxembourg jurisprudence**: Cour d'appel, 10 juin 2009, n° 33.419 du rôle:
> "Il ressort des articles en question que tous documents, titres, créances, effets de commerce confiés à un titre quelconque à la banque constituent de plein droit un gage établi pour sûreté du remboursement de tous montants dus à la banque."

### Insurance Claim Pledges

Pledges over insurance claims (*police d'assurance*) follow general rules, with specific provisions for:
- Life insurance (surrender values)
- Property insurance (indemnity proceeds)

**Art. 70, loi 27 juillet 1997** (insurance contracts): Insurance proceeds are subrogated to the pledged asset.

### Intercompany Loan Pledges

Common in group financing structures:
- Pledge by holding company over loans to subsidiaries
- Subject to corporate benefit analysis
- Often combined with subordination arrangements

---

> **Common Law Comparison**
>
> **English law**: Security over receivables typically takes the form of:
> - **Fixed charge**: Over specific receivables, requires control/dominion
> - **Floating charge**: Over changing pool of receivables
> - **Legal assignment** (by way of security): Requires notice to debtor (s.136 LPA 1925)
>
> The Luxembourg notification requirement resembles the notice requirement for legal assignment.
>
> **UCC Article 9**: Security interests in accounts receivable are perfected primarily by **filing** a financing statement, not by notification to account debtors. Notification only becomes relevant for collection after default.

## Banking Application

### Structuring Receivables Security

**For banks taking security over receivables**:

1. **Identify applicable regime**:
   - Bank account balances → CGF regime
   - Trade receivables → Civil Code or CGF
   - Insurance claims → May be CGF if "avoirs"

2. **Perfect the security**:
   - Notify debtor of pledged claim
   - Obtain acknowledgment where possible
   - Document comprehensively

3. **Monitor the collateral**:
   - Track maturity of pledged claims
   - Ensure collection arrangements
   - Watch for debtor insolvency

### Due Diligence Checklist

- [ ] Verify assignability of claims (no contractual prohibition)
- [ ] Confirm no prior pledge or assignment
- [ ] Notify debtor of pledged claim
- [ ] Document pledge in writing with claim description
- [ ] Establish collection procedures for maturing claims
- [ ] Consider subordination/priority arrangements
