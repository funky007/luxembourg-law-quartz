# Gage sur stocks (Pledge on Inventory)

> **Type**: Special Pledge (*gage sans dépossession*)
> **Source**: Westendorf, T.1, §§775-799
> **Key Articles**: Art. 28 A.G.D. 27 mai 1937; Loi 21 décembre 1994

---

## Overview

A specialized non-possessory pledge enabling businesses to secure loans using their inventory (*stocks*) or raw materials (*matières premières*) without losing physical possession, introduced as a standalone security separate from the *gage sur fonds de commerce*.

---

## Introduction and Purpose (§775)

**Westendorf, T.1, §775**:
The arrêté grand-ducal of 27 mai 1937 has provided since the loi du 21 décembre 1994 the possibility of pledging inventory (*stock*). This provision was introduced to enable the pledge of inventory **outside** the constitution of a *gage sur fonds de commerce*.

> **Doc. parl., n° 2564/00, p. 9**: The reform allowed standalone stock pledges, expanding financing options for businesses.

---

## Applicable Rules (§776)

**Art. 28 arrêté**: The rules concerning *gage sur fonds de commerce* apply to the "nantissement d'une quantité de marchandises ou de matières premières" (pledge of a quantity of merchandise or raw materials).

**Westendorf, T.1, §776**:
> In general, it is possible to refer to the rules set out in the chapter on *gage sur fonds de commerce*.

This includes:
- Formation requirements (written instrument, registration)
- Limited circle of beneficiaries (approved institutions)
- Effects (no right of retention, limited *droit de suite*)
- Realization procedures

**Cross-reference**: See [[16 - Gage sur fonds de commerce]] for detailed rules.

---

## Key Feature: Fluctuating Collateral (§777-778)

### The Fluctuation Principle (§777)

**Art. 28(2) arrêté**:
> "Les parties peuvent convenir en cas de mise en gage d'une quantité de marchandises ou de matières premières que le débiteur gagiste sera en droit d'y prélever des biens et de les remplacer."

*Translation*: The parties may agree that the pledgor shall have the right to withdraw goods and replace them.

**Condition**: A **minimum consistency** (*consistance minimale*) must always be maintained, agreed between the parties and mentioned in the registration.

**Westendorf, T.1, §777**:
> This is an entirely exceptional text since it allows the grantor to diminish the pledge's scope without sanction, if the parties have so provided. In reality, the object of the pledge — the stock — is not affected, only its consistency, and therefore its value.

### Express Stipulation Required (§778)

**The faculty to dispose exists only if expressly provided** in the agreement.

**Parties must determine**:
1. **Disposal limit**: Percentage of stock that may be disposed
2. **Minimum consistency**: Either:
   - Percentage threshold, OR
   - Value threshold

**Creditor preference**: Value-based threshold — pledge secures access to *value*, which may fluctuate.

**Grantor preference**: Percentage threshold — simpler to verify compliance without constant valuation.

**Westendorf, T.1, §778**:
> If the latter option is chosen, it is absolutely essential to clearly determine the reference by which this value is determined, which is more or less difficult depending on whether there is an official quotation for the goods concerned.

### French Comparison (Former Art. L. 527-6 C. com.)

The French regime (abrogated 1 January 2022) provided specific thresholds:
- **10% diminution**: Creditor may demand restoration of guarantee or partial repayment
- **20% diminution**: Creditor may demand total repayment (debt accelerated)

⚠️ **To Be Independently Verified**: Luxembourg law does not contain equivalent automatic triggers — parties must contractually specify consequences of stock diminution.

## Opposability and Third Parties (§779)

**Westendorf, T.1, §779** (citing P. Crocq):
> "Le gage sans dépossession est opposable aux tiers, mais il ne l'est que dans la mesure des dispositions contractuelles qu'il comporte, et [une] autorisation d'aliéner devrait, en conséquence, pouvoir être opposée au gagiste par le tiers acquéreur et permettre à ce dernier d'échapper au droit de suite du gagiste, l'autorisation d'aliéner pouvant être interprétée comme valant renonciation à l'exercice du droit de suite."

**Practical consequence**:
If the stock pledge expressly provides that the grantor has the right to:
- Constitute other pledges on the stocks, OR
- Alienate them

Then this pledge is only opposable to subsequent pledgees **within its own limits**.

**Result**: A second pledge respecting these stipulations is opposable to the first pledgee — including the right of retention.

---

## Multiple Stocks and Registration (§780)

**Parties may determine** whether the pledge covers:
- All stocks of the grantor, OR
- Only certain identified stocks

### Registration Requirements

**Location**: Bureau of Conservation des hypothèques of the judicial district where stocks are situated (per Art. 4 arrêté, adapted).

**Multiple locations**: If stocks are in different judicial districts:
- Registration required in **all** relevant districts
- Otherwise, only stocks in the registered district are covered

**Westendorf, T.1, §780**:
> Due to these national-level requirements, it is clear that a Luxembourg stock pledge validly concluded and rendered opposable to third parties through compliance with Luxembourg formalities **cannot cover stocks belonging to the grantor but situated outside the territory of the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg**.

### Belgian Position (for comparison)

**Liège, 14 décembre 1990, J.L.M.B., 1991, p. 487** (on *gage sur fonds de commerce*):
Registration in the district of the grantor's registered office (and principal place of exploitation) may suffice.

**Westendorf's caution**: Determining the principal place of exploitation can be difficult — prudence dictates multiple registrations.

---

## Distinction from Ownership-Based Security (§799)

**Westendorf, T.1, §799**:
> The pledge holder benefits from a *real right* (*droit réel*) over another's property — a right limited in scope as it serves only one purpose: securing a claim.
>
> Property transferred as security (*propriété transférée à titre de garantie*) confers a stronger right — the creditor becomes full owner, but it remains a *finalized* ownership. The creditor only definitively remains owner if the debtor fails to pay.

**Key distinction**:
- **Pledge**: Limited real right by nature
- **Ownership security**: Full ownership, but limitations generally not opposable to third parties

> In case of doubt about qualification, the **pledge** characterization should be retained as it is less constraining for the grantor.

## Comparative Notes

### vs. Gage sur fonds de commerce

| Feature | Gage sur stocks | [[16 - Gage sur fonds de commerce]] |
|---------|-----------------|-------------------------------------|
| Object | Merchandise/raw materials only | Entire business universality |
| 50% limitation | No | Yes (for merchandise) |
| Fluctuation | Expressly permitted | Not specifically provided |
| Incorporeal elements | Not required | Essential |

### vs. English Floating Charge

| Feature | Gage sur stocks | Floating charge |
|---------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Fluctuation | By express agreement | Automatic |
| Crystallization | N/A | On triggering event |
| Registration | Conservation des hypothèques | Companies House |
| Territorial scope | Luxembourg only | Per governing law |

### vs. UCC Article 9 (Inventory Financing)

| Feature | Gage sur stocks | UCC Art. 9 |
|---------|-----------------|------------|
| Filing | Multiple districts if needed | Central filing (state) |
| After-acquired property | By agreement | Automatic (§9-204) |
| Proceeds | Not expressly covered | Automatic (§9-315) |
| Territorial scope | National | Multi-state possible |

## Banking Practice

### Advantages
- Grantor retains inventory for business operations
- Fluctuation permits normal course dealing
- Standalone security (not tied to fonds de commerce)
- No 50% cap on merchandise value

### Risks
- Same limitations as *gage sur fonds de commerce* (no retention right)
- Art. 2279 C.civ. may protect good faith acquirers
- Multiple registrations required for multi-location inventory
- No coverage for inventory outside Luxembourg

### Due Diligence Checklist

1. Verify beneficiary has government approval
2. Define stocks clearly (all or specific)
3. Determine fluctuation terms:
   - Right to withdraw/replace?
   - Minimum consistency (% or value)?
   - Consequences of breach?
4. Register in all relevant judicial districts
5. Consider territorial limitations (Luxembourg only)
6. Monitor stock levels (reporting obligations)

### Drafting Considerations

**Minimum consistency clause example**:
> "The Grantor shall maintain at all times pledged inventory with a minimum value of EUR [X] / representing at least [Y]% of the initial pledged inventory value, determined by reference to [valuation method]."

**Disposal authorization example**:
> "The Grantor is authorized to dispose of pledged inventory in the ordinary course of business, provided that replacement inventory of equivalent value is immediately substituted therefor."
