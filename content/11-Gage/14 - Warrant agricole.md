# Warrant agricole (Agricultural Warrant)

> **Type**: Special Pledge (*gage spécial*)
> **Source**: Westendorf, T.1, §§650-694
> **Key Articles**: Loi du 3 mai 1934

---

## Definition and Nature

The **warrant agricole** (*agricultural warrant*) is a specialized form of pledge (*gage*) allowing farmers and winemakers to secure loans using agricultural products without physical dispossession.

> **Legal Basis**: Loi du 3 mai 1934 portant remplacement de celle du 14 février 1900 sur les warrants agricoles (Mémorial, 1934, p. 567)

### Origins (§650)

The Luxembourg law is based on the French law of 30 April 1906 modifying the law of 18 July 1898 on agricultural warrants. It is a special text (*texte spécial*) facilitating credit access for the agricultural and viticultural sectors.

**Key characteristics distinguishing from droit commun pledge**:
- [[Dépossession|Fictitious dispossession]] rather than actual delivery
- Special registration and publicity requirements
- Title transferable by endorsement (*endossement*)

## Formation (Section 1)

### A. Substantive Conditions (*Conditions de fond*)

#### I. Secured Claim (*Créance garantie*) (§654-656)

**Borrowings of farmer/winemaker** (Art. 1 loi 1934):
- Originally limited to loans contemporaneous with warrant creation
- French Cour de cassation: Can guarantee claims arising **before** constitution (Cass. fr. civ. I, 28 février 1966)
- Facilitates obtaining term extensions (*prorogation du terme*)

**All forms of credit** (§655):
- Not limited to loans (*emprunts*) despite statutory wording
- French jurisprudence: Can guarantee any form of credit
- Including reimbursement by debtor to surety who paid on his behalf

**Future claims** (§656):
- Classical position: Refused (warrant is endorsable title requiring certain determined claim)
- Recent French jurisprudence: Admitted for continuous financing of agricultural operations or cooperative working capital needs (Agen, 27 février 1985; Cass. fr. com., 15 juillet 1986)

#### II. Object of Warrant (*Objet du warrant*) (§657-661)

**Exclusion of immovables** (§657):
- Movable security only
- Excludes: Immovables by nature (pending crops/*récoltes pendantes*)
- Excludes: Immovables by destination (permanently attached)

**Products of exploitation** (§658):
- Agricultural or industrial products from the exploitation
- Not immovables by destination
- Includes animals belonging to grantor
- Excludes agricultural equipment (*matériel agricole*) - usually immovable by destination

**Origin determines scope**:
- Products from exploitation, even transformed (e.g., eau-de-vie)
- Includes commercialization materials (e.g., bottles for wine)
- French jurisprudence: All products on exploitation, whether harvested by grantor or not
- Real subrogation admitted for fungible products (Agen, 27 février 1985)

**Animals** (§659):
- Can be warranted if NOT immovables by destination
- Animals necessary for exploitation = immovable by destination (e.g., dairy cows for milk production)
- Animals for slaughter (*animaux de boucherie*) = simple movables, warrantable

**Replacement animals and offspring** (§660):
- Art. 3 loi 1934: Warrant must indicate nature, value, quantity, location
- Warrant does NOT cover: replacement animals, animal offspring (*croît*)
- These are new products not part of original warrant
- French law: Subrogation possible if expressly provided by parties

**Subrogation in insurance indemnities** (§661):
- Art. 5(3) loi 1934: Warrant holders have same rights/privileges on insurance indemnities as on damaged goods

#### III. Person of Grantor (*Constituant*) (§662-668)

**Professional capacity** (§663):
- Must be farmer (*agriculteur*) or winemaker (*viticulteur*)
- Art. 1(2): Associations and agricultural/viticultural cooperatives under laws of 27 March 1900 and 10 August 1915
- Need not be exclusively agricultural (French jurisprudence)
- Purely commercial livestock operations excluded

**Property or usufruct** (§664):
- Art. 1 loi 1934: Products from own exploitation or as usufructuary
- Art. 2: Tenant farmer (*cultivateur*) can warrant if special formalities respected
- Cooperatives: Products of which they have become owners

**Warrant on non-owned goods** (§665):
- Art. 2279 C.civ. protects good faith creditor against true owner
- Warrant holder assimilated to pledgee in possession
- Jurisprudence: Fictitious possession confers real possession equivalent (Cass. fr. civ., 23 avril 1918)

**Capacity to dispose** (§666):
- Warrant may lead to forced sale
- Grantor must have capacity to alienate goods

**Goods must be free** (§667):
- Art. 13 loi 1934: Criminal sanctions for false declaration or warranting already-warranted products without notice to new lender
- Penalty: 1 month to 5 years imprisonment + fine 251-5,000 EUR

**Insolvency** (§668):
- Agricultural exploitation not commercial activity (generally)
- Bankruptcy rules apply only to commercial activity as accessory
- Over-indebtedness rules (*surendettement*) applicable to individuals

---

### B. Formal Conditions (*La forme*) (§669-674)

#### I. Establishment of Warrant (*Établissement*) (§670-672)

**Preliminary notice when grantor not owner** (§670):
- Art. 2 loi 1934: Tenant farmer must notify landlord before borrowing
- Purpose: Allow landlord to oppose to preserve Art. 2102(1°) C.civ. privilege
- If formality omitted: Landlord's privilege preserved

**Constitution** (§671):
- Title issued by clerk of justice de paix
- Mandatory mentions (Arts. 3 & 5): Nature, value, quantity, location of products; insurance status

**Opposability to third parties**:
- Transcription in register kept at greffe of justice de paix where goods located
- Art. 6: Clerk must deliver certificate of warrants registered or absence thereof
- Art. 7(3): Automatic radiation after 3 years unless renewed

**Between parties without formalities** (§672):
- Art. 4 loi 1934: Valid between parties
- Only opposable to third parties after transcription

#### II. Fictitious Dispossession (*Dépossession fictive*) (§673-674)

**No material dispossession** (§673):
- "Gage sans déplacement" (pledge without displacement)
- Publicity of warrant effects dispossession
- Pledgee with material possession on same goods would prime warrant holder (Cass. fr. com., 11 juin 1969)

**Deposit with third party** (§674):
- Art. 1(1) loi 1934: Deposit with cooperative or agreed third party
- Third party is depositary for grantor, not tiers convenu
- Art. 1(4): No indemnity from warrant holder

## Effects (Section 2)

### A. Effects on Grantor (*Constituant*) (§676-679)

#### I. Obligations (§677-678)

**Conservation obligation** (§677):
- Art. 1(4) loi 1934: Borrower/depositary responsible for goods
- Fault appreciated per Art. 1137 C.civ. (French doctrine)
- Obligation: Conservation of possession, not just material conservation
- Must warn warrant holder of seizure by chirographic creditor

**Criminal liability** (§678):
- Art. 13 loi 1934: Diversion, dissipation, voluntary deterioration
- Penalty: 1 month to 5 years imprisonment + fine 251-5,000 EUR
- Linked to bad faith of grantor

#### II. Rights (§679)

**Sale of warranted goods** (Art. 8 loi 1934):
- Grantor can sell without creditor's concurrence
- BUT: Delivery only after creditor satisfied
- Goods subject to "sort of unavailability" (*indisponibilité*)
- Early repayment facility
- If holder refuses: Grantor may consign sum per Art. 1259 C.civ.

### B. Rights of Holder (*Droits du porteur*) (§680-694)

#### I. Right of Retention (§680)

- Warrant holder assimilated to pledgee in possession
- Enjoys retention right despite no material detention
- Exercised through actual detainer
- Art. 2279 C.civ. limits: Good faith purchaser prevails over fictitious possession

#### II. Transmission (§682-683)

**Endorsement** (*endossement*) (Art. 9 loi 1934):
- Warrant transferable by endorsement
- Becomes credit instrument for creditor
- Solidarity guarantee of all signers/endorsers

**Formal requirements**:
- Dated and signed
- Names, profession, domicile of transferee
- Notice to clerk within 8 days

**Exemption from notice** (§683):
- Grantor may exempt from notice requirement
- May prevent grantor from exercising early repayment

#### III. Realization (*Réalisation*) (§684-694)

**Mandatory preliminaries**:

1. **Payment demand** (§686):
   - Art. 10 loi 1934: Holder must demand payment
   - If unpaid: Confirm by registered letter with return receipt

2. **Notice of refusal** (§687-688):
   - If no payment within 5 days: Holder must notify non-payment
   - Within 15 days of maturity: Notice to each endorser via greffe
   - Loss of rights against endorsers if notice not given

**Modes of realization**:

1. **Sale** (§690-692):
   - Wait 15 days after registered letter from greffe
   - Petition to justice de paix for public sale authorization
   - Art. 11: Privileged payment from sale proceeds
   - Priority over all creditors except direct contributions, judicial costs, insurance premiums

2. **Judicial attribution** (§693-694):
   - Not expressly provided but not prohibited
   - Civil pledge = attribution judiciaire possible
   - Subsidiary recourse against endorsers

## Practical Considerations

### Banking Practice

**Advantages**:
- Grantor retains goods for exploitation
- Credit facility for agricultural sector
- Transferable by endorsement (liquidity)

**Risks**:
- Fictitious possession weaker than material possession
- Art. 2279 C.civ. protects good faith purchasers
- Identification of warranted goods may be difficult

### Due Diligence

1. Verify professional status of grantor (farmer/winemaker)
2. Check registry for existing warrants
3. Confirm goods are not immovables by destination
4. Obtain landlord consent if grantor is tenant
5. Consider insurance of warranted goods
