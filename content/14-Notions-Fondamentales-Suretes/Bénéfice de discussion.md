# Bénéfice de Discussion

> **Type**: Surety Defense
> **Source**: Westendorf, T.3, §§750-780
> **Key Articles**: C.civ. arts. 2021-2024

---

## Definition

The *bénéfice de discussion* (benefit of discussion, or right of excussion) is the surety's right to require the creditor to first pursue enforcement against the principal debtor's assets before seeking payment from the surety. It reflects the subsidiary nature of the surety's obligation.

> **Art. 2021 C.civ.**: "La caution n'est obligée envers le créancier à le payer qu'à défaut du débiteur, qui doit être préalablement discuté dans ses biens..."
>
> *Translation*: "The surety is obliged to pay the creditor only in default of the debtor, whose assets must first be discussed..."

---

## Requirements for Invocation

The surety must (Westendorf, T.3, §§755-760):

1. **Raise it on first demand** (*in limine litis*): The defense must be invoked when first called upon to pay, not later in proceedings

2. **Identify seizable assets**: The surety must point out to the creditor assets of the principal debtor sufficient to satisfy the debt

3. **Provide funds for execution**: The surety must advance the costs of execution against the debtor

> **Art. 2023 C.civ.**: "La caution qui requiert la discussion doit indiquer au créancier les biens du débiteur principal, et avancer les deniers suffisants pour faire la discussion."

---

## Effect

If properly invoked, the creditor must first attempt execution against the debtor's identified assets. Only if this proves insufficient may the creditor pursue the surety for the shortfall.

If the creditor fails to pursue the identified assets and they subsequently become unavailable (e.g., through the debtor's insolvency), the surety is discharged to the extent of the loss.

---

## Exclusions and Waivers

### Waiver (*Renonciation*)

The *bénéfice de discussion* is routinely waived in commercial guarantees. Standard bank guarantee forms include express renunciation clauses. Waiver may be:
- **Express**: In the guarantee document
- **Implicit**: By agreeing to solidarity with the debtor

### Solidarity (*Cautionnement solidaire*)

When the surety is bound jointly and severally with the debtor (*caution solidaire*), the *bénéfice de discussion* is automatically excluded. The creditor may pursue the surety immediately without first proceeding against the debtor.

> Banking practice: Virtually all bank guarantees are *cautionnements solidaires* with express waiver of *bénéfice de discussion*.

### Debtor Insolvency

The benefit is impractical when the debtor is insolvent — there are no assets to "discuss."

---

## Banking Application

### Documentation

Standard guarantee clauses include:
- "La caution renonce expressément au bénéfice de discussion..."
- "La caution s'engage solidairement avec le débiteur principal..."

### Practical Impact

In international banking transactions, the *bénéfice de discussion* is seen as an impediment to efficient enforcement. Creditors invariably require its waiver, making the protection largely theoretical in commercial contexts.

For consumer guarantees, however, the benefit may apply by default and provide meaningful protection.

---

> **Common Law Comparison**
>
> Common law does not generally recognize an equivalent right. The creditor may pursue the surety directly upon the debtor's default without first exhausting remedies against the debtor, unless the guarantee expressly requires otherwise.
>
> This is a significant difference: Luxembourg's default rule protects the surety; common law's default rule does not.
