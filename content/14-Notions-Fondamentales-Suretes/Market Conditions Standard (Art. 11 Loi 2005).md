# Market Conditions Standard (Art. 11 Loi 2005)

> **Type**: Notion fondamentale
> **Sources**: D. Boone, JurisNews 2011; H.W., JurisNews 2019; P. Geortay, Strada Lex 2014; F.G. de Liedekerke, Strada Lex 2014
> **Key Legislation**: Loi 5 août 2005, Art. 11(1)(b); Directive 2002/47/CE, Art. 4(6)

---

## Definition

**Article 11(1)(b) Loi du 5 août 2005**:
The pledgee may "céder ou faire céder les avoirs nantis par vente de gré à gré à des **conditions commerciales normales**."

This requirement derives from **Directive 2002/47/CE, Article 4(6)**, which offered Member States the option to require "normal commercial conditions" for private sales.

**Considérant 17 of Directive 2002/47/CE**:
> "Ce contrôle devrait permettre aux autorités judiciaires de vérifier que la réalisation ou l'évaluation a été effectuée dans des conditions commerciales normales."

---

## 1. In Concreto Definition

### The Landmark Definition

**T.A. Luxembourg, 20 mai 2010, n° 127843** established the authoritative definition:

> "Les 'conditions commerciales normales' correspondent à des conditions résultant du libre jeu de l'offre et de la demande, **au moment de la réalisation du gage** et en tenant compte:
> - des informations disponibles à cette époque,
> - de leur nature,
> - des particularités concrètes des actifs concernés, et
> - des conditions du marché applicable."

### In Concreto vs. In Abstracto

The court explicitly adopted an **in concreto** interpretation:

> "Si [...] il n'existe aucun marché réglementé ou organisé pour l'échange des instruments financiers en question, il convient de **ne pas s'arrêter à la valeur réelle intrinsèque des titres** (difficile, voire impossible à déterminer de manière définitive), mais de considérer **la meilleure offre qu'il est possible d'obtenir dans les conditions données** pour les actifs en question."

**Source**: T.A. Luxembourg, 20 mai 2010, n° 127843 du rôle

### What Is REJECTED

| Irrelevant Factors | Why Rejected |
|-------------------|--------------|
| Past valuations (e.g., 18 months prior) | Market conditions change |
| Future potential value | Speculative |
| Historical performance | Not current reality |
| Abstract "fair market value" | Theoretical, not practical |
| Original acquisition price | Irrelevant to current conditions |

### What Is RELEVANT

| Relevant Factors | Rationale |
|-----------------|-----------|
| Current market conditions | Assessment at moment of realization |
| Available information at time of sale | What pledgee could know |
| Actual buyer interest | Real market demand |
| Concrete circumstances of sale | Practical constraints |
| Asset-specific characteristics | Nature of the collateral |

## 2. Burden of Proof

### General Rule: Actori Incumbit Probatio

Per Art. 58 NCPC: "Il appartient à chaque partie de prouver conformément à la loi les faits nécessaires au succès de sa prétention."

### Application to Financial Collateral

**The party challenging the realization bears the burden of proving that normal commercial conditions were not respected.**

**T.A. Luxembourg, 20 mai 2010**:
> "Il appartient [au demandeur] d'établir en quoi cette cession au prix de [X] ne correspond pas à des conditions commerciales normales."

### Practical Consequence

| Party | Obligation |
|-------|------------|
| **Pledgee** | NOT required to prove compliance |
| **Challenger** | MUST demonstrate specific deficiencies |
| **Effect of failure** | Realization upheld |

**Source**: D. Boone, JurisNews Droit des sociétés, Vol. 4, No. 2/2011, p. 137

### What Challenger Must Show

1. The price was manifestly below market value
2. Better offers were available and ignored
3. The process was procedurally deficient
4. Bad faith or abuse of rights occurred

## 3. Evidence Standards

### Expert Valuation

While **not legally required**, expert valuation is strongly recommended:

**T.A. Luxembourg, 20 mai 2010**:
> "La Banque n'était pas obligée par les dispositions légales ou contractuelles de requérir la production du rapport d'évaluation établi par le cabinet de réviseurs indépendant."

**However**: The bank that obtained an independent valuation successfully defended its realization.

### Best Practice: Independent Expert Report

Requirements for a robust valuation:
- [ ] Independent expert (no conflict of interest)
- [ ] Recognized professional qualifications
- [ ] Valuation dated close to realization date
- [ ] Clear methodology statement
- [ ] Market data sources identified
- [ ] Range of values with assumptions stated

### Alternative Evidence

In absence of expert report:
- Auction results (even private auctions)
- Multiple buyer solicitations
- Comparable transactions
- Market data/indices
- Financial statements of target company

**Source**: F.G. de Liedekerke, "Les garanties financières en droit luxembourgeois", Strada Lex 2014, Vol. III, p. 1257

## 4. Timing Considerations

### No Obligation to Wait

**T.A. Luxembourg, 20 mai 2010**:
> "Sauf abus de droit, on ne peut en effet exiger du créancier gagiste, face à une défaillance caractérisée de son débiteur, d'attendre l'issue de développements spéculatifs affectant le cas échéant la valeur des actifs nantis, avant de procéder à la réalisation de son gage."

### Key Principles

| Principle | Implication |
|-----------|-------------|
| **No market timing obligation** | Pledgee need not wait for better conditions |
| **Snapshot assessment** | Value determined at realization moment only |
| **Reasonable diligence** | Process should be commercially reasonable |
| **Speed is legitimate** | Rapid realization is permitted under Loi 2005 |

### The "Bouleversements Boursiers" Factor

Courts acknowledge that dramatic market changes can legitimately explain value drops:

> "Entre deux évaluations faites à plus d'un an d'intervalle, la valeur des titres nantis pouvait avoir changé de manière radicale pendant ce laps de temps, tel que l'ont amplement montré les bouleversements boursiers et financiers de ces derniers temps."

**Source**: T.A. Luxembourg, 20 mai 2010

## 5. Key Case Law

### T.A. Luxembourg, 20 mai 2010, n° 127843 du rôle

**Facts**:
- Joint venture shares valued at EUR 86 million in April 2008
- Pledge realized in November 2009 at EUR 1 million
- Independent valuation showed range of EUR 0 - 1.7 million
- Challenger alleged violation of normal commercial conditions

**Holding**: Realization upheld
- Expert valuation supported the price
- Challenger failed to prove market conditions were violated
- Changed circumstances between valuations acknowledged

**Significance**: Foundational case establishing in concreto interpretation

### Cour d'appel, 3 novembre 2010, n° 35824 du rôle

**Holding**: Even if normal commercial conditions not met, remedy is:
- Damages (dommages et intérêts)
- NOT nullity of sale
- NOT suspension of enforcement effects

**Source**: D. Boone, JurisNews Droit des sociétés, Vol. 3, n° 9/2010

### Cour d'appel, 16 mai 2018, n° 39.827 du rôle

**Facts**: Shares sold at EUR 1 and EUR 3 respectively

**Holdings**:
1. Normal commercial conditions standard applies to all private sales
2. Violation = damages only, not nullity
3. Special law (Loi 2005) prevails over general law (Code civil)
4. Defect of price rules in Code civil do not apply

**Source**: H.W., JurisNews Droit des Obligations, Vol. 4, No. 1/2019, p. 67

### T.A. Luxembourg, 16 novembre 2012, n° 143752

**Holding**: Financial collateral contracts and agreed valuation methods cannot be challenged; curator cannot demand annulment.

**Source**: D. Boone, "Face aux falaises de marbre de la loi sur les garanties financières", JurisNews Droit des sociétés, 9/2012

## 6. Relationship to Other Enforcement Methods

### Appropriation (Art. 11(1)(a))

- Requires agreed valuation methodology
- "Conditions commerciales normales" not explicitly required
- BUT valuation must be commercially reasonable
- Expert valuation or contractual methodology essential

### Public Sale/Bourse (Art. 11(1)(b) alternative)

- Market price presumptively meets normal conditions
- Default: Bourse de Luxembourg (suppletive)
- Parties may agree alternative venue

### Market Price Appropriation (Art. 11(1)(e))

- For listed instruments or OPCs with published NAV
- "Prix en cours" or "dernière valeur nette d'inventaire"
- Clearest standard; least discretion

**Source**: F.G. de Liedekerke, op. cit., pp. 1256-1259

## Banking & Finance Application

### Pre-Sale Preparation

- [ ] Document the enforcement event and its occurrence
- [ ] Confirm debt quantum and secured obligations
- [ ] Assess asset type and applicable market (if any)
- [ ] Determine if regulated market/published NAV applies (Art. 11(1)(e))

### Valuation Process

- [ ] **Recommended**: Obtain independent expert valuation
- [ ] If no expert: document basis for price determination
- [ ] Record market conditions at time of realization
- [ ] Consider multiple valuation approaches for complex assets
- [ ] Maintain file with all supporting documentation

### Sale Process Options

| Option | Advantages | Considerations |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| **Independent expert valuation** | Strongest defense | Cost; time required |
| **Private auction** | Market-tested price | Confidentiality concerns |
| **Solicitation of multiple buyers** | Evidence of best price | May not yield offers |
| **Direct sale to identified buyer** | Speed; certainty | Ensure arm's length terms |

### Documentation Requirements

- [ ] Written valuation report or price justification
- [ ] Evidence of market conditions (indices, comparables)
- [ ] Record of any buyer solicitation process
- [ ] Contemporaneous file notes of decision-making
- [ ] Evidence of pledgor notification (if required by contract)

### Red Flags to Avoid

- [ ] Sale to connected party without independent valuation
- [ ] Price significantly below recent valuation without justification
- [ ] Failure to consider available market information
- [ ] Ignoring expressed buyer interest
- [ ] Rushed process without documentation
