# Dispossession (Dépossession)

> **Type**: Essential element of pledge / Condition of formation
> **Source**: Westendorf, T.1, §30-36
> **Key Articles**: Arts. 2071, 2073, 2076 C.civ.
> **Key Cases**: Trib. Lux. 28/02/2018; CSJ (Cass.) 23/12/1892; Trib. Lux. 22/06/2010

---

## Definition

Dispossession (*dépossession*) is the transfer of possession of the pledged asset from the grantor (*constituant*) to the pledgee (*créancier gagiste*) or an agreed third party (*tiers convenu*). Under Luxembourg law, it remains an essential element of the pledge contract (*contrat de gage*), making the pledge a "real contract" (*contrat réel*).

> **Art. 2071 C.civ.**: "Le nantissement est un contrat par lequel un débiteur remet une chose à son créancier pour sûreté de la dette."
>
> *Translation*: "A pledge is a contract by which a debtor delivers a thing to his creditor as security for the debt."

**Luxembourg jurisprudence**: Trib. Luxembourg, 7 février 2006, n° 67.687 du rôle held:
> "Le gage est un contrat par lequel un débiteur remet une chose mobilière à son créancier en garantie du paiement de la dette. Il entraîne généralement la dépossession du débiteur."

---

## Legal Nature: Real Contract (Contrat Réel)

The pledge is classified as a *contrat réel* - a contract whose formation requires not only the exchange of consents but also the delivery (*remise*) of the thing. (Westendorf, §30)

**Trib. Luxembourg, 28 février 2018, n° 58/2018, n° 176.491 du rôle**:
> "Les contrats réels sont les contrats dont la formation suppose non seulement l'échange des consentements, mais encore la remise de la chose qui est l'objet de l'obligation. Les articles 1875 du Code civil pour le prêt à usage, 1892 pour le prêt de consommation, 1919 pour le dépôt et 2071 pour le gage font de la remise de la chose un élément de formation du contrat."

**CSJ (Cass.), 23 décembre 1892, Pas., 3, p. 168**:
> "Le contrat de gage est donc un contrat réel qui n'est parfait que par la dépossession du débiteur."

**Consequence**: In the absence of dispossession, only a *promesse de gage* (promise of pledge) exists, not a perfected pledge. (Westendorf, §30)

---

## Functions of Dispossession

### 1. Protection of the Creditor (§31)
The pledge does not confer a complete *droit de suite* (right to follow the asset) unlike a mortgage (*hypothèque*). Without such protection, dispossession substitutes for publicity to protect the creditor against acts of the debtor and third parties.

### 2. Protection of Third Parties (§31)
Third parties cannot rely on assets forming part of another creditor's security. Dispossession warns them that the asset is encumbered.

### 3. Formalism of Protection (§34)
Dispossession expresses "definitive consent" and warns the grantor of the gravity of his act (*formalisme de protection*). (Westendorf, §34, citing Aynès & Crocq)

### 4. Facilitation of Enforcement (§34)
The creditor in possession can directly enforce without first seizing the asset - a "saisie anticipée" (anticipated seizure). (Westendorf, §34)

---

## Validity vs. Opposability Distinction

Modern Luxembourg jurisprudence distinguishes between:
- **Validity** (*validité*): The pledge contract is valid *inter partes* upon agreement
- **Opposability** (*opposabilité*): Dispossession is required to invoke the privilege against third parties

> **Art. 2076 C.civ.**: "Dans tous les cas, le privilège ne subsiste sur le gage qu'autant que ce gage a été mis et est resté en la possession du créancier, ou d'un tiers convenu entre les parties."
>
> *Translation*: "In all cases, the privilege subsists on the pledge only insofar as the pledge has been placed and remains in the possession of the creditor, or of a third party agreed between the parties."

**Trib. Luxembourg, 16 février 2011, n° 62/2011, n° 128.901 du rôle**:
> "Le texte de l'article 2076 ne vise que 'le privilège' du créancier gagiste et ne détermine que l'opposabilité du contrat aux tiers. [...] le contrat de gage n'est certes pas nul en raison de l'absence de dépossession, mais il y a lieu de déclarer le contrat de gage inopposable [aux tiers]."

---

## Effect of Absence of Dispossession

A pledge without dispossession is declared "inoperative" (*inopérant*) against third parties.

**Trib. Luxembourg, 22 juin 2010, n° 94/2010, n° 120.271 du rôle**:
> "Étant donné que les parties sont d'accord pour dire qu'il n'y a jamais eu dépossession [...], cette garantie, à la supposer correspondre au contrat réel que constitue le gage, sera inopérante."

**Cour d'appel, 20 juin 2012, n° 36.962 du rôle** (confirming):
> "Au vu des articles 2071 et 2076 de notre Code civil, les premiers juges sont à confirmer en ce qu'ils ont dit cette garantie inopérante."

This case also confirmed that French reforms (2006) abolishing dispossession requirement do **not** apply in Luxembourg.

---

## Practical Disadvantages (§33)

1. **Loss of use value**: The grantor loses use of the asset needed to generate income to repay the credit (*caractère antiéconomique*)
2. **Single-use limitation**: The asset cannot secure multiple debts (unless via *entiercement* - third-party custody)
3. **Conservation burden**: The pledgee must conserve the asset - a "heavy responsibility"

These disadvantages led to development of:
- Pledge without dispossession (*gage sans dépossession*)
- Fiduciary securities (*sûretés fiduciaires*)

---

> **Common Law Comparison**
> Under English law, the possessory pledge similarly requires delivery of possession to the pledgee. However, English law developed the non-possessory "charge" (fixed or floating) as an alternative, registered at Companies House. Luxembourg lacks an equivalent general registration system for movable security, making dispossession more critical.
>
> Under UCC Article 9, "perfection" of a security interest can occur by possession, control, or filing a financing statement - offering multiple alternatives to physical dispossession.
>
> *Note*: The Luxembourg *contrat de garantie financière* (Financial Collateral Law of 5 August 2005) provides a modern alternative with fictive dispossession for financial instruments.

## Banking Application

### Custody Arrangements
Banks taking pledges over physical assets (e.g., gold, bearer instruments) must establish proper custody arrangements. The pledged assets should be:
- Physically segregated from the grantor's other assets
- Held by the bank or an agreed third-party custodian (*tiers convenu*)
- Identifiable and traceable

### Due Diligence
Before accepting a pledge, verify:
1. No prior pledge exists (dispossession to another creditor would be visible)
2. Proper transfer of possession is documented
3. Third-party custodian arrangements (if any) are properly contracted
