# Subrogation de la Caution

> **Type**: Surety Right After Payment
> **Source**: Westendorf, T.3, §§900-950
> **Key Articles**: C.civ. arts. 2028-2029, 1251

---

## Definition

Upon paying the creditor, the surety is subrogated (*subrogé*) to the creditor's rights against the principal debtor. This subrogation operates by law (*de plein droit*) and allows the surety to recover from the debtor using all the creditor's rights, actions, and securities.

> **Art. 2028 C.civ.**: "La caution qui a payé a son recours contre le débiteur principal, soit que le cautionnement ait été donné au su ou à l'insu du débiteur."
>
> *Translation*: "The surety who has paid has recourse against the principal debtor, whether the suretyship was given with or without the debtor's knowledge."

---

## Legal Basis

Subrogation arises from two sources:

### 1. Legal Subrogation (Art. 1251)

The surety benefits from automatic legal subrogation under Art. 1251(3) C.civ. as a person bound with others or for others who had an interest in paying.

### 2. Personal Action (Art. 2028)

Independent of subrogation, the surety has a personal action against the debtor for reimbursement. This action includes:
- Principal amount paid
- Interest from the date of payment
- Costs and damages suffered

---

## Rights Transmitted

Through subrogation, the surety acquires (Westendorf, T.3, §§915-925):

### All Creditor Rights

- The claim against the principal debtor
- All accessory rights and securities
- Priority and ranking the creditor held
- Enforcement rights (titles, judgments)

### Securities Held by Creditor

Critical for banking: the surety steps into the creditor's securities:
- Mortgages (*hypothèques*)
- Pledges (*gages*)
- Other personal guarantees
- Any other security interest

> **Banking Implication**: A surety who pays may enforce the bank's mortgage or pledge against the debtor, even though the surety did not originally hold that security.

---

## Loss of Subrogation Rights (Art. 2037)

A critical protection: if the creditor's fault causes the surety to lose the benefit of subrogation into securities, the surety is discharged to that extent.

> **Art. 2037 C.civ.**: "La caution est déchargée, lorsque la subrogation aux droits, hypothèques et privilèges du créancier ne peut plus, par le fait de ce créancier, s'opérer en faveur de la caution."
>
> *Translation*: "The surety is discharged when subrogation into the rights, mortgages, and privileges of the creditor can no longer, by the creditor's act, operate in favor of the surety."

### Examples

The surety may be discharged if the creditor:
- Releases a security without the surety's consent
- Allows a security to lapse (e.g., fails to renew a registration)
- Grants a subordination or cedes priority
- Releases a co-surety

The discharge is proportionate to the loss suffered.

### Banking Practice

Banks protect against this by:
- Obtaining waiver of Art. 2037 rights
- Reserving discretion to manage securities
- Including clauses permitting release of securities without surety consent

---

## Subrogation Among Co-Sureties

When one surety pays more than their share, they may:
1. Seek contribution from co-sureties directly
2. Exercise subrogated rights proportionately against the debtor

The paying surety is subrogated to the creditor's rights but only to the extent of payment, and contribution claims against co-sureties are separate from subrogation against the debtor (Westendorf, T.3, §940).

---

## Timing and Notice

### When Subrogation Operates

Subrogation is automatic upon payment — no formality required. However, the surety should:
- Notify the debtor of payment (Art. 2031)
- Perfect any registrations inherited from the creditor
- Take possession of securities if required

### Debtor's Defenses

If the surety pays without notifying the debtor and the debtor had defenses against the creditor, the debtor may raise those defenses against the surety (Art. 2031). The surety should inform the debtor before paying.

---

## Banking Application

### Documentation Considerations

**Intercreditor agreements**: In syndicated facilities, subrogation rights of guarantors must be subordinated to senior lenders to prevent a paying guarantor from competing with the bank.

**Subordination clauses**: Standard guarantee forms require the surety to subordinate any subrogated claims until the bank is fully repaid.

**Turnover provisions**: Any amounts recovered by a subrogated surety may be required to be turned over to the bank.

### Practical Effect

In distressed situations:
- Solvent group company pays bank under guarantee
- Company is subrogated to bank's claims against debtor
- Intercompany claim arises
- May be subordinated in debtor's insolvency

---

> **Common Law Comparison**
>
> **English Law**: Sureties have similar subrogation rights. Upon payment, the surety is entitled to the benefit of securities held by the creditor. The principle from *Duncan, Fox & Co v North and South Wales Bank* (1880) is similar.
>
> **UCC (US)**: Sureties have equitable subrogation rights. The surety may enforce the creditor's security interests after paying the obligation.
>
> **Key Similarity**: Both civil and common law recognize that a paying surety should not be worse off than the creditor they satisfied.
