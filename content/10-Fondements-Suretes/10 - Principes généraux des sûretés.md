# General Principles of Security Interests (Principes généraux des sûretés)

> **Type**: Foundational concepts
> **Source**: Westendorf, T.3, §1-15 (Introduction générale)
> **Key Articles**: Arts. 2092-2093 C.civ. (droit de gage général)
> **Related**: [[🧭 Droit des sûretés — Index]]

---

## Definition of Sûreté (Security Interest)

### The Problem: Creditor Risk

Credit (*crédit*) is central to all economic activity. (Westendorf, T.3, §1)

> **Art. 2092 C.civ.**: "Quiconque s'est obligé personnellement, est tenu de remplir son engagement sur tous ses biens mobiliers et immobiliers, présents et à venir."
>
> *Translation*: "Whoever has personally obligated himself is bound to fulfill his commitment on all his movable and immovable property, present and future."

The unsecured creditor (*créancier chirographaire*) has only a general right of pledge (*droit de gage général*) over the debtor's assets. In insolvency, creditors share proportionally (*au marc le franc*) per Art. 2093 C.civ. — often receiving only partial or no payment.

**Core insight**: "Pas de crédit sans sûretés" — No credit without security. (Westendorf, T.3, §1, citing Bourassin & Brémond)

---

## Two Approaches to Defining Sûreté

### 1. Conceptual Approach (Professor Pierre Crocq)

A *sûreté* is characterized by three elements (Westendorf, T.3, §5):

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Finalité** (Purpose) | Guarantees payment; improves creditor's position vs. chirographaire |
| **Effet** (Effect) | Mise en œuvre satisfies creditor through full or partial payment |
| **Technique** | Gives creditor supplementary right of action (*droit d'agir*), accessory to the claim |

**Synthetic definition** (Mestre, Putman & Billiau): "La sûreté est la combinaison d'une affectation et d'un droit d'agir."

*Translation*: "A security is the combination of an allocation [of assets] and a right to act."

### 2. Functional Approach (Simler & Delebecque)

A *sûreté* is "la garantie conférée au créancier contre le risque d'insolvabilité de son débiteur" — the guarantee conferred on the creditor against the risk of the debtor's insolvency. (Westendorf, T.3, §11)

This broader approach includes all mechanisms whose function is to guarantee obligations.

---

## Distinction: Sûreté vs. Garantie

**Garantie** (broader concept): Any technique making creditor satisfaction more probable. (Westendorf, T.3, §9)

Two families of guarantees:
1. **Indirect techniques** — Inherent to the obligation relationship (résolution, exception d'inexécution, compensation, action paulienne, solidarité)
2. **Specific techniques** — Sûretés proper, which are added to the obligation

> **Key distinction**: Guarantees inherent to the debt relationship vs. security interests added to it.

---

## Classification of Sûretés

### Sûretés Réelles (Real Securities)

The creditor obtains a **real right** (*droit réel*) over specific assets dedicated to payment. (Westendorf, T.3, §13)

**Definition** (Séjean-Chazal): "Un aménagement positif du droit de l'exécution visant à renforcer sur un ou plusieurs biens du débiteur; par une technique de rupture d'égalité, les prérogatives que le créancier détient en vertu de son droit de gage général."

Two sub-categories (Westendorf, T.3, §14):

| Type | Mechanism | Examples |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **Traditional** | Right of preference (*droit de préférence*) on proceeds | Gage, privilèges, hypothèque |
| **Propriété-sûretés** | Transfer of ownership as security | Réserve de propriété, fiducie-sûreté, cession à titre de garantie |

### Sûretés Personnelles (Personal Securities)

The creditor obtains a **right to pursue a third party** (*droit de poursuite contre un tiers*) whose patrimony is engaged alongside the debtor's. (Westendorf, T.3, §15)

**Luxembourg jurisprudence**: Cour d'appel, 12 novembre 2014, n° 40.268 du rôle:
> "La garantie à première demande, encore appelée garantie autonome ou indépendante, fait partie des garanties personnelles, qui se caractérisent par l'engagement d'une tierce personne aux côtés du débiteur."

**Key feature**: The guarantor is never definitively liable — always has recourse against the principal debtor. (Westendorf, T.3, §15)

---

## Advantages of Personal Securities

(Westendorf, T.3, §17-22)

| Advantage | Explanation |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Simple constitution** | Generally quick and inexpensive to establish |
| **Easy enforcement** | Creditor uses ordinary enforcement procedures |
| **No asset waste** | Entire patrimony backs guarantee, no specific asset tied up |
| **Flexible coverage** | Can cover all present and future debts between identified parties |
| **Party autonomy** | Cautionnement regime largely supplétif in Luxembourg law |

---

## Disadvantages of Personal Securities

(Westendorf, T.3, §23-26)

| Disadvantage | Explanation |
|--------------|-------------|
| **Chirographaire only** | Creditor has no preference; vulnerable to guarantor's insolvency |
| **Guarantor resistance** | "Le cautionnement se donne dans l'euphorie et s'exécute dans les larmes" |
| **Uncertain recourse** | Guarantor's recourse against debtor often worthless (debtor already insolvent) |
| **Weaker deterrent** | Less psychological pressure on debtor than asset pledge |

---

## The Accessory Character (Caractère Accessoire)

All sûretés have a functional accessory relationship to the secured claim — the claim must exist at constitution. (Westendorf, T.1, §51)

**Degrees of accessoriété** (Westendorf, T.3, §5, note 54):

| Degree | Description | Examples |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Structural** | Existence depends on claim throughout life | Cautionnement, gage, hypothèque |
| **Functional only** | Claim must exist at constitution; may survive claim | Garantie indemnitaire |
| **Autonomous** | Independent of underlying claim | Garantie à première demande |

> See [[Accessoriété]] for detailed analysis.

---

## The Principle: No Enrichment from Security

A sûreté is never a source of enrichment (*source d'enrichissement*) for the creditor. Its sole purpose is to guarantee payment, not to profit beyond what is owed. (Westendorf, T.3, §5, note 51, citing Crocq)

This is a **general principle of public policy** (*ordre public*).

---

> **Common Law Comparison**
>
> English law distinguishes between:
> - **Security interests** (charge, mortgage, pledge, lien) — proprietary rights in the debtor's assets
> - **Guarantees** (guarantee, indemnity) — personal undertakings by third parties
>
> The Luxembourg *sûreté réelle* corresponds broadly to the English security interest, while *sûreté personnelle* corresponds to guarantee/indemnity.
>
> Under UCC Article 9, security interests in personal property are unified under a single regime requiring perfection (filing, possession, or control). Luxembourg retains separate regimes for different types of security.

## Banking Application

### Security Package Structuring

When structuring financing, consider:

1. **Combined approach**: Real + personal securities provide complementary protection
2. **Personal securities** for:
   - Shareholder guarantees (corporate benefit analysis required)
   - Cross-guarantees in group financing
   - Quick, low-cost additional coverage
3. **Real securities** for:
   - High-value transactions requiring priority
   - Financial collateral for trading operations
   - Assets already in creditor's control

### Due Diligence Points

- [ ] Verify capacity of security provider (corporate objects, authorization)
- [ ] Check for existing security interests (no general register for movables)
- [ ] Assess corporate benefit for upstream/cross-stream guarantees
- [ ] Confirm proper perfection (dispossession/control for pledge, notification for receivables)
